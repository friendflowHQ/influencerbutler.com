The anatomy of a deal post that works

A scroller decides whether to tap in about two seconds, so structure matters more than clever copy. Lead with the saving, make the link unmissable, and give one honest reason to act now. Then disclose the affiliate relationship at the top, not buried at the bottom.

Show the drop

State the old price and the new price so the discount is obvious at a glance.

One line of urgency

Limited stock, a lightning-deal clock, or a coupon that expires: honest reasons to act now.

Link on its own line

Never bury [LINK] mid-paragraph. Give it space so a thumb lands on it.

Disclose up front

A short "#ad" or "affiliate link" near the top keeps you FTC and Amazon compliant.

Note on disclosure: as an Amazon Associate you earn from qualifying purchases, and both the FTC and the Associates program require you to say so clearly. Keep the disclosure in every post below intact.

Template 1: Facebook group deal drop

Everyday deal drop

When to use: your standard daily deal in a Facebook deals group. Scannable, friendly, and disclosed.

DEAL ALERT [ad]

[PRODUCT]
Was [OLD PRICE], now [PRICE] ([DISCOUNT] off)

Grab it here: [LINK]

I earn a small commission if you buy through my link, at no extra cost to you. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Replace the square brackets. Post a clear product photo above the text for the biggest lift in a Facebook feed.

Template 2: Telegram deal blast

Fast Telegram blast

When to use: a rapid-fire deal channel where subscribers scan dozens of posts. Tight and structured.

[FIRE EMOJI] [PRODUCT]

[PRICE]  (was [OLD PRICE], [DISCOUNT] off)

[LINK]

#ad Affiliate link, I may earn a commission at no cost to you.

Telegram renders links inline, so a bare [LINK] on its own line is enough. Keep the whole post under five lines.

Template 3: Instagram broadcast channel

Instagram broadcast

When to use: an Instagram broadcast channel where followers expect quick, friendly picks in your voice.

Okay this one is a genuinely good buy [ad]

[PRODUCT] just dropped to [PRICE] (down from [OLD PRICE]).

I have been using mine for [SHORT PERSONAL NOTE] and at [DISCOUNT] off it is an easy yes.

Tap the link in the channel to grab it: [LINK]

Affiliate link. I earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you.

Broadcast channels reward personality. Keep the [SHORT PERSONAL NOTE] genuine, and only post deals you would actually recommend.

Template 4: Lightning or limited-time deal

Time-limited urgency

When to use: a lightning deal or a stock-limited price. The clock is the hook, so lead with it, honestly.

[LIGHTNING EMOJI] LIGHTNING DEAL [ad]

[PRODUCT] is [PRICE] right now ([DISCOUNT] off), but this is a limited-time lightning deal and the price can jump back at any moment.

If it has been on your list, now is the time: [LINK]

Affiliate link, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Only claim urgency that is real. If the deal is not actually time-limited, drop the urgency line rather than inventing a countdown.

Template 5: Price-drop alert

Lowest-price alert

When to use: a product that just hit its lowest price in a while. The "lowest in months" angle drives taps.

PRICE DROP [ad]

[PRODUCT] just fell to [PRICE], its lowest in [TIME PERIOD].

That is [DISCOUNT] off the usual [OLD PRICE].

Here is the link while it lasts: [LINK]

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. Buying through my link costs you nothing extra.

Only use "lowest in [TIME PERIOD]" when the price history backs it up. Pricecrash Butler and Campaign Deals surface these honestly.

Template 6: Deal roundup post

Multi-deal roundup

When to use: a weekly or themed roundup (top kitchen deals, back-to-school picks) in a group or newsletter.

[THEME] deals I am loving this week [ad]

1) [PRODUCT] - [PRICE] ([DISCOUNT] off): [LINK]
2) [PRODUCT] - [PRICE] ([DISCOUNT] off): [LINK]
3) [PRODUCT] - [PRICE] ([DISCOUNT] off): [LINK]

All affiliate links. I earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Cap a roundup at three to five items so it stays scannable. Put the single best deal first.

Template 7: Coupon or promo-code deal

Clip-coupon post

When to use: a product with an on-page coupon to clip or a code to enter. Spell out the exact steps.

EXTRA SAVINGS [ad]

[PRODUCT] is [OLD PRICE], but there is a [DISCOUNT] coupon on the page.

Steps:
1) Open the link: [LINK]
2) Tick the "clip coupon" box under the price
3) The discount comes off at checkout, bringing it to about [PRICE]

Affiliate link, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Coupons and codes expire fast. Double-check the code still works before you post, and remove the deal once it dies.

Frequently asked questions

What makes an Amazon deal post convert?

A deal post converts when it leads with the saving, creates a reason to act now, and makes the link impossible to miss. The strongest posts state the old price and the new price so the discount is obvious, add a light touch of urgency (limited stock or a lightning-deal clock), and put the affiliate link on its own line. Keep it scannable: a headline, one or two lines, the price, and the link.

Do I have to disclose affiliate links in deal posts?

Yes. The FTC requires a clear, hard-to-miss disclosure whenever you earn a commission, and the Amazon Associates program requires the "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" statement. Put a short disclosure like "#ad" or "affiliate link" near the top of every deal post, not buried at the bottom. All of the templates above include disclosure so you stay compliant.

What is the best format for a Telegram or Facebook group deal post?

Short and structured. Start with an emoji and the product, show the price drop on its own line, add one line of urgency, then the link. Telegram and Facebook groups reward posts that a scroller can read in two seconds. Avoid long paragraphs, and never bury the price or the link inside a block of text.

How often should I post deals without annoying my group?

Post deals that are genuinely good rather than everything you can find. A few strong deals a day usually outperforms a constant stream, because the group learns that your posts are worth opening. Filtering by discount depth and commission helps you post only the deals worth your audience's attention, which is exactly what Deals Butler automates.
