Amazon influencer brand outreach tips: how to pitch brands

Creator Connections is not the only way to earn from brands. Direct outreach lands partnerships that never hit the campaign board, and as an Amazon influencer you have a head start: proof you can sell. These tips cover finding the right brands, writing pitches that get replies, following up well, tracking your pipeline, and scaling without burning your reputation.

1. Find the right brands to pitch

Do not cold-pitch random brands. Start where you already have proof: the brands behind your top-selling ASINs. If your videos already move a product, its brand is the warmest lead you have, you are offering more of a result they can already see. Pull your best sellers from your commission data and build your target list from there.

Amazon Butler automates exactly this: Creator Connections outreach to the brands behind your top-selling ASINs, so your pitching starts from demonstrated demand instead of a cold list.

2. Write a pitch that gets replies

Brand inboxes are full of generic "I love your products" emails. Yours stands out by leading with proof and making one clear ask. A pitch that gets read usually has four parts:

Specific hook

Name the exact product and why you already feature it.

Proof

Show you sell or have made content for it, with a real signal, not a boast.

One clear ask

Say precisely what you want: a campaign, a commission bump, a collab.

Easy yes

Keep it short and make replying a single, low-effort decision.

Personalize the hook and proof for every brand. The rest of the structure can stay consistent, which is what makes outreach repeatable without feeling like spam.

3. Follow up without being annoying

Most deals happen on the follow-up, not the first message, but there is a line. One or two spaced, polite follow-ups are plenty, and each should add something useful rather than just "bumping" the thread. The trick is knowing exactly who you have already contacted, when, and whether they replied, so you never double-message someone or chase a person who already said yes. That record-keeping is what separates professional outreach from pestering.

4. Track your pipeline in one place

Outreach falls apart in scattered inboxes and half-remembered conversations. Keep every brand you are prospecting, pitching, or negotiating with in a single pipeline. Pitch Butler is one CRM for exactly that: prospects flow in from research tools and Creator Connections, and connecting Gmail lets it send pitches and auto-track replies, so a warm lead never gets forgotten and follow-ups stay organized.

5. Scale outreach safely

Once a pitch template works, the temptation is to blast it everywhere. Resist it. Scaling well means keeping personalization high while pacing volume, respecting rate caps and human-like delays, and maintaining a do-not-message list so you never re-contact the wrong people. The aim is more genuine conversations, not more noise. Protecting your sender reputation and your brand relationships is what lets outreach keep working month after month instead of flaming out.

Put it together

Good outreach is a loop: find brands behind your best sellers, pitch with proof, follow up politely, track everything in one pipeline, and scale at a safe pace. You can run all of it by hand, or let the butlers carry the repetitive parts, Amazon Butler for finding and reaching the brands behind your top ASINs, and Pitch Butler for the pipeline and reply tracking. Frequently asked questions

Which brands should I pitch first?

Start with the brands behind your top-selling ASINs. You already have proof you can move their products, which makes the pitch nearly self-evident. Amazon Butler automates Creator Connections outreach to exactly these brands, the ones behind what you already sell.

How do I write a brand pitch that gets replies?

Lead with proof, not flattery. Name the specific product, show that you already sell or have made content for it, and make one clear ask. Keep it short, personalized, and easy to say yes to. Generic mass emails get ignored; a pitch tied to real results gets read.

How often should I follow up without being annoying?

How do I scale outreach without getting flagged?

Scale with pacing, not volume-at-all-costs. Keep personalization high, respect rate caps and human-like delays, and maintain a do-not-message list. The goal is more genuine conversations, not more spam, so relationships and your sender reputation stay intact.
