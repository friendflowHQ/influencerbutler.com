The five levers of a converting deal post

Every example below is graded against the same five levers. You do not need all five in every post, but the ones that convert best usually pull at least four. Miss the disclosure and you are non-compliant; miss the hook and nobody taps.

Hook

The saving leads. Old price to new price, so the deal is obvious in the first line.

Urgency

An honest reason to act now: a lightning clock, low stock, or a verified lowest price.

Social proof

A personal note, a rating, or "I use this daily" that makes the pick feel trustworthy.

Call to action

One obvious next step pointing at the link, on its own line so a thumb finds it.

Disclosure

An up-front affiliate disclosure that keeps you FTC and Amazon Associates compliant.

Example 1: Facebook group deal drop

A daily deal posted to a home-and-kitchen deals group, with a product photo above the text.

Filled-in example

Platform: Facebook deals group. Goal: a scannable everyday drop that earns the tap.

DEAL ALERT (ad)

Stainless steel 12-piece knife set with block
Was $89.99, now $44.98 (50% off)

4.7 stars across 8,200 ratings, and I have had mine for two years with zero complaints.

Grab it here: amzn.to/example-knives

Affiliate link. I earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Why it converts

  • Hook: "Was $89.99, now $44.98 (50% off)" puts the whole deal in one glanceable line.
  • Urgency: light here, but the round 50% off and low price carry it for an everyday drop.
  • Social proof: the star rating plus a genuine personal note ("mine for two years") builds instant trust.
  • Call to action: "Grab it here" with the link on its own line makes the tap effortless.
  • Disclosure: "(ad)" up top and the full Associates line at the bottom keep it compliant.

  • Disclosure: one clear disclosure covering all the links, up where readers see it.

A product with an on-page coupon, where spelling out the exact steps removes checkout confusion.

Filled-in example

Platform: any group or channel. Goal: convert a coupon deal by making the savings steps crystal clear.

Steps:
1) Open the link: amzn.to/example-vacuum
2) Tick the "clip coupon" box under the price
3) The discount comes off at checkout, bringing it to about $41.99

Coupons like this expire without warning, so clip it while it is live. Affiliate link, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Why it converts

  • Hook: "EXTRA SAVINGS" plus the coupon promises a discount beyond the sticker price.

  • Social proof: lighter here, but the confident, helpful walkthrough signals the creator has done this before.
  • Call to action: numbered steps remove the friction of "how do I actually get this price?"
  • Disclosure: "(ad)" opens the post and the affiliate line closes it, keeping it compliant.

Frequently asked questions

What separates a deal post that converts from one that gets scrolled past?

Converting deal posts do five things: a hook that leads with the saving, honest urgency that gives a reason to act now, a touch of social proof or a personal note that builds trust, a clear call to action pointing at the link, and an up-front affiliate disclosure. Posts that get scrolled past bury the price, hide the link in a paragraph, or read like a generic ad with no reason to tap today.

How do I add urgency to a deal post without being misleading?

Only claim urgency that is real. If it is a genuine lightning deal or the stock is low, say so. If the price has hit its lowest point in months and you can verify that, the "lowest in months" angle is honest urgency. Never invent a fake countdown or a fake "only 2 left," because it erodes the trust that makes your group open your posts in the first place.

Where should the affiliate disclosure go in a deal post?

Near the top, where it is hard to miss, not buried under the link. A short line like "#ad" or "affiliate link, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you" satisfies the FTC, and the Amazon Associates program also wants the "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" statement. Clear disclosure protects you and, counterintuitively, tends to build trust with your audience.

Doing it by hand means finding the deal, checking the price, formatting the post, and pasting it into each destination.
