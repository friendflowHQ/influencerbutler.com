The five things every strong pitch does

Before the examples, here is the checklist each one is graded against. A pitch that nails all five gets read and gets a reply. Miss two or three and it lands in the same ignored pile as every "I love your brand" message a partnership manager deletes each morning.

1. Subject line

Names a product or a result, not "Collaboration opportunity." Under about 45 characters.

2. Opening hook

The first sentence proves it was written for this brand, not blasted to 200 of them.

3. Proof

One concrete number: views, click-through, or tracked Amazon orders you already drive.

4. The ask

One clear request. A campaign, a call, or a rate, never a menu of five options.

5. The easy yes

A low-friction next step, plus honest FTC disclosure that builds trust.

Example 1: Cold outreach to a brand you already sell

Maya runs a kitchen and home storefront. She already sells a mid-size brand's pour-over kettle organically and wants to turn that into a paid video.

Filled-in example

Situation: warm first contact, product already selling on her storefront.

Subject: Your pour-over kettle is my top kitchen sellerHi Priya,

I am Maya, an Amazon Influencer in the home-cooking space. Your Cedar and Sage pour-over kettle has quietly become the top seller on my storefront: over the last 60 days it drove 4,100 views and 62 tracked orders on Amazon, all organic.

I would love to make a dedicated review video for it and put it in front of more of the right buyers before the holiday gifting rush.

Would you be open to a Creator Connections campaign, or a quick call to talk about it? I can send a few examples of past videos.

Every paid or gifted collaboration is clearly disclosed in my content.

Thanks,
Maya

Why it works

  • Subject line: names the exact product and a result. Priya opens it because it is about her kettle, not a generic collab.
  • Opening hook: "top seller on my storefront" is specific and flattering without gushing. It proves Maya is not mass-blasting.
  • Proof: 4,100 views and 62 tracked orders in 60 days speaks the brand's language: sales, not vanity metrics.
  • The ask: one request (a campaign or a call) with a seasonal reason to move now.
  • The easy yes: offers examples and a disclosure line, lowering risk for the brand.

Example 2: Following up after a Creator Connections campaign

Devon completed a paid campaign for a fitness brand's resistance bands. The video is performing, so he pitches to expand while the results are fresh.

Filled-in example

Situation: post-campaign, video performing, brand can still see the numbers in their dashboard.

Subject: The FlexForm band video is convertingHi Marcus,

Thanks again for the Creator Connections campaign on the FlexForm resistance bands. The video went live on March 3rd and has already picked up 18,000 views and 210 tracked orders, and it is still climbing.

Since it is clearly landing with my audience, I would love to keep going: a follow-up video on your ankle straps, or a set of three videos over the next quarter at a partner rate.

Open to a 15-minute call this week to map it out?

Thanks,
Devon
[storefront link]

Why it works

  • Subject line: leads with the result ("is converting"). The brand already knows the campaign, so the news is the performance.
  • Opening hook: gratitude plus a concrete date anchors it to a real, verifiable campaign.
  • Proof: 18,000 views and 210 orders, and the phrase "still climbing," make the case that stopping now would waste momentum.
  • The ask: two concrete expansion options and a specific, low-cost next step (a 15-minute call).
  • The easy yes: he is pitching from a position of proven results, so the brand's risk is near zero.

Example 3: Proposing a paid collaboration with a rate

Aisha is a beauty creator ready to name a package and price to a skincare brand she has researched but never worked with.

Filled-in example

Situation: cold-to-warm brand, creator is confident enough to lead with a rate and deliverables.

Subject: Paid video package for Lume Glow serumHi Elena,

I make shoppable Amazon skincare videos for an audience of 54,000 across Instagram and my storefront. My reviews on serums in your price range average 22,000 views and about an 8% click-through to Amazon.

Here is what I am proposing for the Lume Glow vitamin C serum:
- One dedicated review video optimized for Amazon search
- One Instagram reel linking to my storefront
- Usage rights for your own channels for 90 days

My rate for this package is $650. I am also happy to start commission-only through Creator Connections if you would rather test the fit first.

Per FTC guidelines, every paid or gifted collaboration is clearly disclosed in my content.

Would this work for you?
Aisha

Why it works

  • Subject line: states exactly what the email contains, a paid package for a named product. No mystery, no fluff.
  • Opening hook: leads with audience size and, more importantly, an 8% click-through, which reframes the pitch around sales.
  • Proof: the click-through rate is the standout number, more persuasive to a brand than raw follower count.
  • The ask: a bulleted deliverable list and a named price make the offer concrete and easy to approve.
  • The easy yes: the commission-only fallback removes budget risk, and the disclosure line signals professionalism.

Example 4: Re-engaging a brand that went cold

Sam pitched an outdoor-gear brand months ago and got a polite "not right now." He reopens the door with fresh numbers and a seasonal hook.

Filled-in example

Situation: previously interested brand that went quiet, revived with a new stat and a Prime Day angle.

Subject: Circling back on Northbound, with new numbersHi Tom,

We talked back in spring about a video for your Northbound trail backpack, and the timing was not right then. A couple of things have changed on my side: my audience is now 41,000, and my last ten Amazon videos drove 380 tracked orders.

I noticed you will likely be running Prime Day deals in July. If a backpack or daypack video would help you make the most of that window, I would love to be part of it.

No pressure if not, I will keep recommending gear I actually use on the trail either way.

Best,
Sam
[storefront link]

Why it works

  • Subject line: "with new numbers" gives a quiet lead a reason to reopen the thread.
  • Opening hook: references the earlier conversation honestly, then pivots to what changed, so it does not feel like nagging.
  • Proof: updated audience size plus 380 tracked orders shows real growth since the last contact.
  • The ask: tied to a concrete calendar moment (Prime Day) that gives the brand a deadline to act against.
  • The easy yes: the graceful "no pressure" close keeps goodwill intact and makes a future yes easier.

Frequently asked questions

What does a good brand pitch message actually look like?

A good pitch names the brand and a specific product in the first line, offers one concrete piece of proof (views, clicks, or tracked Amazon orders), makes a single clear ask, and gives the brand an easy yes such as a call or a Creator Connections campaign. It reads like it was written for that one brand, stays under about 150 words, and notes that any paid or gifted collaboration is disclosed to the audience.

Should I include my stats in a brand pitch even if they are small?

Yes. Specific numbers, even modest ones, beat vague claims like "a large engaged audience." Tracked Amazon orders and click-through rates are especially persuasive because they speak the language brands care about: sales. If your follower count is small, lead with conversion or with the fact that you already sell the brand's product organically.

How long should a brand pitch email be?

Aim for under 150 words, roughly one phone screen. Brand and partnership managers skim dozens of pitches, so the winners are short: a personalized opening line, one proof point, one ask, and a friendly close. If you need to share more, offer to send a media kit or examples rather than pasting everything into the first message.
