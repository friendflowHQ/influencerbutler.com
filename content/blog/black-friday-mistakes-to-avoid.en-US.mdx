Black Friday rewards preparation and punishes carelessness, and the frustrating part is that most of the carelessness is completely avoidable. The creators who have a rough Black Friday rarely lose because of bad luck or a weak audience. They lose because of a handful of small, repeatable mistakes that quietly drain their commissions during the one week those commissions are biggest.

Black Friday 2026 is November 27, and you still have time to dodge every one of these. So here are the nine Black Friday mistakes creators make most often, and exactly how to avoid them before the rush hits.

## 1. Treating Black Friday as a single day

Black Friday is not one day, it is a stretch: the lead-up, Friday itself, the quiet weekend, and Cyber Monday on November 30. Creators who blow their whole load on Friday morning miss three other windows of real buying. Plan across the full run instead, and keep something in the tank for Cyber Monday, which is its own peak, not an afterthought.

## 2. Posting every deal instead of the right ones

When the deal flood hits, the instinct is to post everything. That is how you train your audience to scroll past you. A feed that is an undifferentiated firehose of discounts has no signal in it. Filter hard. Lead with categories your audience already buys from you, and let your judgment be the thing that makes your deals worth clicking. One great deal beats ten mediocre ones.

## 3. Leaving dead links in your storefront and content

This one is pure, painful waste. You do all the work to earn the click, and then the link is broken, out of stock, or mistagged, so the commission evaporates. Black Friday traffic makes every dead link more expensive because there are so many more clicks hitting them. Audit your storefront and your highest-traffic content before the event. A quick pass covers most of it:

- **Dead and delisted links** in your most-visited storefront lists.
- **Mistagged links** that are live but not actually crediting you.
- **Out-of-stock products** sitting at the top of a list.
- **Broken links in old content** descriptions that still pull traffic.

Our [storefront checklist](/blog/amazon-influencer-storefront-tips) walks through exactly what to check.

## 4. Scrambling to make content on the day

Nobody does their best work at 6am on Black Friday. Creators who leave all their content for the day itself end up fried by noon and posting worse stuff as the hours drag on. Batch your evergreen pieces now, this week, while you have calm hours. Gift guides, favorites, comparisons, all of it. Leave only the fast "this just dropped" updates for the day, and those should take two minutes each.

## 5. Ignoring whether a deal is actually good

A sticker that says "40% off" is not the same as a real price drop. Some Black Friday deals are inflated-then-discounted theater, and if you promote junk deals as great ones, your audience notices and your trust erodes right when you need it most. Check real price history before you vouch for something. Being the creator who says "this one is actually a deal, this one is not" is worth more than being the creator who hypes everything.

## 6. Missing boosted Creator Connections campaigns

Black Friday floods Creator Connections with high-bonus campaigns, and the best ones fill within hours. Creators who are heads-down posting deals forget to check, and the boosted rates on products their audience actually wants expire unclaimed. Decide your rules in advance so accepting is fast, and do not let a great campaign slip by because you were busy filming. Our guide on [cashing in on Creator Connections during Black Friday](/blog/automate-creator-connections-campaigns) has the workflow.

## 7. Spamming your audience into tuning out

There is a fine line between "helpfully present" and "exhausting." Posting nonstop, especially low-effort deal after low-effort deal, teaches people to swipe past you by Saturday, right before Cyber Monday. Keep a steady, reliable rhythm of genuinely good deals instead. Every post spends a little of your audience's patience. Make each one worth it.

## 8. Forgetting the quiet weekend

The Saturday and Sunday between Black Friday and Cyber Monday are a window everyone forgets. Competition drops, lots of deals are still live, and the people who were too busy on Friday are finally scrolling. Creators who go silent all weekend leave that traffic on the table. A simple "these Black Friday deals are somehow still live" post can pull nicely when everyone else has gone quiet.

## 9. Trying to do all of it by hand

This is the mistake underneath most of the others. Finding real deals, checking price histories, auditing links, catching boosted campaigns, posting across every platform, all day, for days, is simply more than one person can do well in real time. So things slip. Links stay broken, campaigns expire, deals go unposted. Not because you are lazy, but because it is genuinely too much.
