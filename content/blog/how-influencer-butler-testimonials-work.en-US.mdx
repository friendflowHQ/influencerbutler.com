You should be a little skeptical of testimonials on any company's own website, including this one. A business can pick its five best quotes out of hundreds, write a few itself, or just never publish the mediocre ones. Saying "these are real reviews" does not actually tell you anything, because that is exactly what a company would say either way. So instead of asking you to trust the label, here is the actual mechanism: when we ask people for a testimonial, what makes one show up automatically versus get read by a person first, and why what you see on the homepage is a curated subset, not the full list.

## We don't ask on day one

If we asked for a testimonial the moment someone signed up, we would be collecting first impressions, not opinions. Day one is onboarding, figuring out which butlers to turn on, maybe one small win if things go well. It is not enough time to know whether the product actually saved you hours a week or quietly broke something three weeks in.

So the in-dashboard prompt is timed to show up around day 45, roughly the start of someone's second month using the app. By then you have had a full billing cycle and then some to see whether the thing keeps working, not just whether the demo looked good. The prompt only shows to people who have not already submitted a testimonial and have not dismissed the prompt before, so it is not something you get nagged about repeatedly once you have already said no or already said your piece. When it does show up, submitting is a short form: a star rating, your name and role, and the review itself.

## The 4-star line, and what happens on either side of it

Here is the part that matters most for trust, and the part most sites never tell you: what happens after you hit submit.

A testimonial rated 4 or 5 stars is auto-approved. It goes live without anyone on our team reading it first. That is a specific, checkable claim rather than a vague "all our reviews are genuine" line, and it is also the honest tradeoff: a handful of mediocre word choices might slip into the live feed because nobody proofread them, in exchange for not quietly filtering out anything less than glowing.

Anything rated below 4 stars does not get deleted and does not get auto-published either. It goes to manual review, where someone on the team actually reads it. That matters because the easy failure mode for a testimonials system is letting the threshold double as a silent trash can for criticism. It doesn't. A 2-star review still gets looked at by a person, both because it might point at something worth fixing and because the person who wrote it deserves more than being auto-discarded.

## Approval is not the same as "on the homepage"

This is the detail that is easy to gloss over, so we won't. A testimonial being approved (whether automatically at 4-plus stars or by hand after review) only means it is in the system and eligible to be shown. It does not mean it is actually on the homepage.

There is a cap of 12 testimonials featured publicly at any one time, and the team manually chooses which approved testimonials get featured, plus the order they appear in on the homepage carousel. So if we have, say, sixty approved testimonials sitting in the system, you are only ever seeing up to twelve of them at once, and we picked which twelve.

We are not going to pretend that is not curation, because it obviously is. What it is not, though, is fabrication or cherry-picking of fake quotes. Every testimonial that gets featured was actually submitted by an actual customer through the actual form, went through the actual 4-star threshold or actual manual review, and the team just decides which real ones get the limited homepage slots and in what order. That is a meaningfully different claim than "these are all our reviews" or "these are definitely the only opinions anyone has about us," and we would rather you know the real version.

## Someone actually reads these

One more small detail that is easy to miss scrolling past the homepage carousel: some testimonials have a reply from the team sitting underneath them. That is not automated and not generic. It is someone on our side writing a real response to that specific piece of feedback, whether it is a thank-you, an answer to a question the person raised, or a note about something we fixed because of what they said.

It is a small feature, technically just one more text field on the testimonial record, but it is the clearest signal that a human on our end actually read what was submitted instead of a form just collecting stars and quotes into a pile. If you see a reply under a review, that conversation genuinely happened.

## Why we're telling you all this

None of this makes our testimonials some special, superior category of review. Plenty of companies run something structurally similar and just don't describe it. The point of this post is narrower: rather than ask you to take "real testimonials" on faith, we would rather tell you the actual mechanics, the day-45 timing, the 4-star line, the manual review path below it, the 12-slot cap, the manual ordering, and the team-response feature, so you can decide for yourself whether that process earns any trust. If you have submitted a testimonial yourself, this is what happened to it after you hit submit. And if you have not yet hit your second month with us, you will probably see the prompt soon.
