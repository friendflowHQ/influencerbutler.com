The prep is done. The event starts in the morning. This is not the night to build anything new, it is the night to confirm what you built actually works. Ten minutes, five checks, then sleep.

## 1. The schedule is running, not drafted

The most common silent failure: a queue full of posts that were never actually armed. Open your schedule and confirm it says running, with real posts lined up against real times. A queue that is saved but not started will sit quietly all through the event and post nothing.

## 2. The links go where you think

Click your link in bio. Click through to your event Idea List. Tap one product. If any step lands somewhere wrong or dead, you want to know now, not after a thousand people have already bounced.

## 3. The first post looks right

Pull up the first scheduled post exactly as it will appear. Check the caption, the price, the link, and the disclosure. If the first one is clean, your template is clean, and the rest will follow it.

## 4. Notifications will not block the butler

If you automate posting to Facebook, make sure no browser permission popup is sitting in the way. A single notifications prompt can quietly stop a butler from posting. Clear it tonight so the morning runs itself. (If you have hit this before, you know the two-minute fix; run it now rather than at 7 a.m.)

## 5. You know your one job tomorrow

With the schedule carrying the steady load, your job during the event is small: a fresh deal pass each morning, your top content posted by hand, and replies to comments. Write that on a sticky note. It keeps you from inventing busywork when the automation is already handling the hard part.

Five checks. If they all pass, you are done. Close the laptop and let tomorrow come to a system that is ready for it.
