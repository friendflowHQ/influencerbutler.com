For most of the year, a deal-posting creator's job is steady. You find a good price, you post it, you do it again tomorrow. Then Prime Day arrives and the whole thing turns into a fire hose. Thousands of deals drop at once, your audience is in full shopping mode, and the commissions available in 48 hours can rival a normal month. A Prime Day strategy is what separates the creators who cash in from the ones who spend two days frantically copy-pasting and still miss the best stuff.

The mistake almost everyone makes is treating Prime Day as a marathon of manual posting. It isn't. It's a planning event that happens to have a 48-hour execution window. The creators who win Prime Day do most of their work before it even starts.

## Why Prime Day is different from a normal deal day

On a normal day, the scarce resource is good deals. During Prime Day, good deals are everywhere; the scarce resource is your attention and your time. There are too many discounts to post them all, your audience is being hit with deals from every direction, and the window is short enough that hesitation costs real money.

That flips your job. Most of the year you're hunting. During Prime Day you're curating and pacing. The question stops being "can I find a deal?" and becomes "which of these hundred deals are worth my audience's click, and when do I post them?"

## Prepare before the event (this is where it's won)

The single biggest predictor of a good Prime Day is how much you set up in advance. When the deals start flying, you do not want to be inventing your workflow from scratch.

Here's the prep that pays off:

- **Build your wishlist of likely winners.** Look at what your audience already buys from you and note the products and categories that consistently convert. When Prime Day hits, you check those first instead of doom-scrolling the whole catalog.
- **Draft your post templates.** Write the skeleton of your deal posts now: the hook structure, the way you frame price and savings, the call to action. During the event you just drop in the product and number.
- **Line up your channels.** Know exactly where you're posting and in what order. Trying to remember your distribution plan at hour 14 is how good deals get posted to one channel and forgotten on the others.
- **Set your pacing plan.** Decide roughly how many posts per hour and when you'll take breaks. A plan you made while calm beats a decision you make while frazzled.

If you want a fuller version of this checklist, our [deal-posting guide](/blog/deal-posting-influencer-guide) breaks down the sustainable workflow these habits build on. Prime Day just compresses it into two intense days.

## Pace yourself so you don't crash on day two

Here's the trap: Prime Day is two days long, and most creators go all-out on day one and burn out by noon on day two. The surge doesn't stop just because you're tired. The audience that's still shopping at hour 30 is just as valuable as the one at hour two.

> The creator who posts steadily for 48 hours out-earns the one who sprints for 12 and collapses. Prime Day rewards endurance, not heroics.

A few rules that keep the tank from hitting empty:

1. **Post in focused blocks, not constant streams.** Set a timer, find and queue several deals, post them, then step away. Constant low-grade attention is more exhausting than a few intense bursts with breaks between.
2. **Don't chase every deal.** You will miss good ones. That's fine. A handful of well-chosen, well-framed deals beats a hundred rushed ones, and your audience can only act on so many anyway.
3. **Protect day two.** Save some energy. The second-day shoppers are often the more deliberate buyers, and they convert.

## Curate hard, because more isn't better

When everything is on sale, posting everything trains your audience to tune you out. The deals that perform during Prime Day are the ones that feel hand-picked: genuinely good prices on things your specific audience actually wants. A wall of generic discounts reads like noise. A few sharp picks read like a friend who did the work for you.

Lean on what you already know converts. If your followers buy kitchen gear from you, your Prime Day should be heavy on kitchen gear, not a random spray of every category. Specificity is what makes a deal feel real during an event where every product claims to be discounted. And the verification habit still applies. A fake-looking discount during Prime Day is still a fake-looking discount.

## After the surge: review and bank the lessons

When the dust settles, take twenty minutes to look back. Which deals actually drove clicks and commissions? Which channels carried their weight? Which post formats landed? Prime Day is a giant, concentrated data set about what your audience responds to, and most creators waste it by collapsing instead of reviewing. The notes you take this Prime Day make next Prime Day, and every ordinary deal day in between, sharper.

## The takeaway

Prime Day rewards preparation over hustle. Build your templates and wishlist before it starts, curate hard during the event, pace yourself across both days, and review when it's over. The creators who treat it as a planning event with a short execution window consistently out-earn the ones who try to brute-force it live. Do the setup, protect your energy, and let the surge work for you instead of against you.
