I posted about this in the group and got a lot of "wait, can you say that in English?" So here is the plain-English version, with a link you can send anyone who is confused.

The short version: if a tool promises to keep accepting your Creator Connections campaigns "even while your computer is off," that has to happen somewhere. It cannot happen on your computer, because your computer is off. So it is happening on someone else's server, around the clock, without you watching. That one fact is the whole story, and it is worth understanding before you hand any tool your Amazon login.

## There is no official shortcut for this

Amazon does not offer an official way for outside tools to accept Creator Connections campaigns on your behalf. No approved connection, no shortcut. That means any tool doing this "in the cloud" is running an actual web browser against Amazon's website, clicking around like a person would, except nobody is actually there. It is doing this on a server, while you sleep.

## Why that points at a data center IP

Here is the part that matters. Every device on the internet, including a server, connects from an address called an IP address. Your home internet connection has one. So does a server sitting in a data center somewhere.

Running an unattended browser 24/7 is expensive to do one person at a time, so it is cheap and common for a company to run many customers' automation from the same servers, which means from the same small pool of data center IP addresses. That is the biggest red flag: a shared data center IP, often doing the exact same kind of automated behavior for a lot of different accounts at once.

To a platform like Amazon or Facebook, a data center address looks nothing like a normal person browsing from home. It is one of the clearest signals bot-detection systems look for. Amazon's detection for this is not as aggressive as some other platforms today, but it exists, and nothing says it stays this way. Betting your account on "they have not cracked down yet" is not a plan.

> The only question that matters is whose IP address that browser is actually running on, and whether you actually know the answer.

## Why this is scary, not just annoying

This is not a minor inconvenience if it goes wrong. We have watched creators and agencies lose access to accounts entirely over exactly this kind of flag, sometimes permanently, sometimes on days that matter most (nobody wants a Christmas Eve ban). Once an account is fully banned, "I did not know my tool was doing this" does not get it back.

## Feature phrases that usually mean a data center IP

You will not find a company advertising "runs on a shared data center proxy," obviously. But certain ways of describing a feature almost always mean that is what is happening underneath, whether the company realizes it or not:

- **"Works even while your computer is off / closed / asleep."** The one from the start of this post. Someone else's server is doing the work, somewhere.
- **"Cloud-based automation" or "runs in the cloud"** as the entire pitch, with no mention of whose internet connection it actually uses.
- **"Our servers handle it for you" / "managed infrastructure."** Convenient language for "you will never see where this runs."
- **"24/7 automation included" on every plan**, with no separate step anywhere for you to set up your own computer or your own connection.
- **"Set it and forget it," positioned as zero ongoing setup on your end.** Real transparency usually means at least one setup step, like adding your own proxy.
- **No settings screen that shows a connection type, proxy, or IP address at all.** If there is nothing to look at, there is nothing being shown to you on purpose.

None of this means a company is trying to trick you. Often it just means the convenience is real, and so is the part nobody mentions: your account's activity is leaving from a connection you cannot see or choose.

## What to actually ask any tool that promises hands-off cloud automation

Before you connect an Amazon or social account to anything that claims to work while your computer is off, ask directly:

- **Where does this run, physically?** If the answer is vague, that is itself an answer.
- **What type of proxy is it, and is it shared with other customers?** A real answer names a specific type of connection (residential, static residential/ISP, mobile, or data center), not just "secure servers."
- **Can I see the connection it is actually using?** A tool with nothing to hide can show you.
- **Is this a data center address or a residential one?** These are not the same thing, and the difference is the entire point of this post.

Then ask for the one thing that actually proves it: **the real, specific IP address your account is connecting through, visible inside your own account.** Not a description, the actual address, the same way a bank shows you where your last login came from. Ask the company to build that in, a simple screen that shows the proxy type and the exact IP being used on your account right now, so you are never guessing. If they will not show you, or cannot, that tells you everything about how much visibility you actually have into what is running your account.

If a tool cannot answer those questions clearly, or the support team gets cagey, you have your answer already.
