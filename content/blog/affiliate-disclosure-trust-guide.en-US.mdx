A lot of creators treat affiliate disclosures like a chore they are hoping to get away with. They bury the disclosure at the bottom of a caption, write it in tiny gray text, or skip it entirely and hope nobody notices. Here is the reframe that changes everything: a good affiliate disclosure is not a tax on your content. It is one of the most trust-building things you can do, and trust is the entire reason people buy from creators instead of from a faceless ad. Done right, disclosing that you earn a commission makes you more credible, not less.

It also happens to be the law in most places, and the platforms you rely on have their own rules on top of that. So this is one of those rare situations where the honest move and the smart move are the same move.

## Why disclosure actually helps you sell

It feels counterintuitive. Why would telling people "I get paid if you buy this" make them more likely to buy? Because your audience already assumes it. They know creators earn commissions. What they are watching for is whether you are being straight with them about it.

When you disclose clearly and casually, you signal that you have nothing to hide. That you would recommend this thing whether or not there was a commission attached. The creators who hide their affiliate relationships and then get caught are the ones who torch their credibility. The ones who say "this is an affiliate link and I genuinely love this product" build the kind of trust that turns a casual follower into a repeat buyer.

> People do not resent that you make money. They resent feeling tricked. A clear disclosure removes the trick and leaves only the recommendation.

## What the rules actually require

You do not need to be a lawyer, but you should know the basics. In the United States, the FTC requires that any material connection between you and a brand, and earning a commission absolutely counts, be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Other countries have their own equivalents, and they tend to land in the same place.

The recurring theme across all of them is the word **clear**. A disclosure that technically exists but that nobody would ever notice does not count. The standard is whether an ordinary person would see and understand it.

Here is what "clear and conspicuous" generally means in practice:

- **Place it where people will actually see it**, near the recommendation, not buried fourteen lines down or hidden behind a "more" tab.
- **Use plain language.** "I earn a commission from purchases through my links" beats vague jargon or a wall of legalese.
- **Make it noticeable.** Not gray-on-gray six-point font. It does not need to be huge, just visible.
- **Disclose on every relevant piece**, not once in your bio and never again. Each post that contains affiliate links generally needs its own disclosure.

Amazon's own program terms add a layer too. As an Amazon associate or influencer, you agree to identify yourself as a participant in their program in a specific way. Read your program agreement and follow its exact wording requirements, because Amazon can and does enforce them.

## How to disclose without killing the vibe

The fear is that a disclosure makes your content feel stiff, corporate, or salesy. It does not have to. The trick is to make it feel like a natural part of how you talk, because at this point, your audience expects it.

### Keep it short and human

You do not need a paragraph of legal text. A simple, honest line works: "Heads up, these are affiliate links, so I earn a little if you buy through them." It is friendly, it is clear, and it is done in one breath.

### Put it up front, not buried

Lead with it or place it right next to the links, not at the very bottom where it reads like you were hoping nobody would scroll that far. Front-loading the disclosure actually reads as more confident, because it says you are not embarrassed about it.

### Be consistent across platforms

Every platform has its quirks. On video, say it out loud and show it on screen. In captions, put it before the fold. On your storefront, the affiliate relationship is generally understood, but a clear note still helps. The goal is that no matter where someone finds your recommendation, the disclosure is right there with it.

### Pair it with genuine enthusiasm

The strongest combination is disclosure plus authenticity: "This is an affiliate link, and honestly I would tell you to buy this even if it weren't." That one sentence does the legal job and the trust job at the same time.

## Build it into your workflow so you never forget

The most common reason creators miss disclosures is not defiance, it is forgetfulness. You are filming, editing, posting fast, and the disclosure slips your mind on the one post that happens to get the most reach.

The fix is to make disclosure a non-negotiable step in your posting routine, the same way you would never post without a link. Build a tiny checklist: link in, disclosure in, post out. Save a disclosure line as a text snippet you paste every time so you are never reinventing it under pressure.
