Prime Day 2026 runs June 23 to June 26 this year, which Amazon pulled earlier than the usual July date. That means your prep window is closing fast, and the single highest-leverage thing you can do right now is not posting deals. It is getting your storefront ready. This Prime Day storefront checklist is the pre-event work that decides whether all that incoming traffic turns into commissions or just bounces.

Here is the thing most creators miss: during Prime Day, your posts are just the front door. When someone taps your link, they often land on your storefront, and then they browse. If that page is a mess, you lose the sale you already earned the click for. So before June 23, you fix the shop. Then you post.

## Why your storefront is the real Prime Day asset

Think about what happens during the surge. You post a great deal. Someone clicks. Maybe they buy that exact item, maybe they don't, but either way Amazon gives you a commission window on everything they purchase for the next 24 hours. So a shopper who lands on your storefront, sees five other things they want, and grabs three of them just made you far more money than the one deal you posted.

That only happens if your storefront is shoppable. A cluttered, half-abandoned page does the opposite: it makes people leave. The week before Prime Day is when you turn that page from a junk drawer into a sales floor.

## The pre-Prime-Day storefront checklist

Work through this list before June 23. None of it is hard. It is just the stuff everyone skips.

### 1. Audit and prune your existing lists

Open every idea list you have. Be honest. Anything broken, outdated, or off-brand gets cut. A short storefront of strong picks beats a sprawling one stuffed with dead products. Shoppers trust a tidy shop and bounce off a chaotic one.

### 2. Pull your real order history first

Before you decide what to feature, look at what your audience has actually bought from you. Not what you think sells, what genuinely moved. Your real sales data is the best map you have for Prime Day, because the products people already bought from you are the ones they will buy again, especially at a discount. If you have ever guessed wrong about your own best earners, this step is the fix.

### 3. Build themed Prime Day idea lists in advance

Do not wait until the 23rd to assemble lists. Build them now so you have something ready to link the second a deal goes live. Good themes:

- **"Prime Day deals under $25"** for impulse buyers
- **"The stuff I actually use every day"** for trust-driven picks
- **A niche-specific list** (Prime Day kitchen, beauty, home, whatever you are known for)
- **"Gift-able under $50"** because people shop ahead during sales

Give each list a clear cover image and a title that says exactly what is inside. Our guide on [choosing products that actually convert](/blog/choose-products-that-convert) covers how to pick the items that belong on these lists in the first place.

### 4. Fix your thumbnails and ordering

Lead with your best stuff. The first few items in any list do the heaviest lifting, so put your proven winners at the top, not buried on the third scroll. Swap out any blurry or generic thumbnails for clear, appealing ones. People shop with their eyes during a rush.

### 5. Tighten your titles and descriptions

A list called "My Faves" tells a shopper nothing. "Prime Day kitchen upgrades I swear by" tells them exactly what they are about to see and why it matters. Specific beats clever, every time. There is more on this in the full [storefront checklist that turns browsers into buyers](/blog/amazon-influencer-storefront-tips).

> A clean storefront does not just convert better during Prime Day. It keeps converting for months after, long after the deal posts have scrolled away.

### 6. Test the whole path yourself

Click your own links like a shopper would. Does the post lead to the storefront? Does the storefront load clean? Are the products actually still available? Five minutes of testing before the 23rd saves you from sending Prime Day traffic into a dead end.
