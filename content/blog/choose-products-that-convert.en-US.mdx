Most Amazon storefronts do not fail because they look bad. They fail because of what they feature. You can have flawless thumbnails and tidy lists and still earn almost nothing if you are promoting the wrong products. Amazon influencer product selection is the lever that quietly decides everything else, and most creators pick on gut feeling alone.

The good news is that picking products that convert is not a mystery. It is a filter with four parts. Run a product through all four and you will know pretty quickly whether it earns a spot or whether you are about to waste a perfectly good post on something nobody will buy.

Let's build the framework.

## Filter 1: Does it match what your audience actually buys?

This is the most important filter, and it overrules the other three. A product can be perfect on every other measure and still flop if it does not fit what your audience is already in the market for.

Relevance is not "would my followers find this interesting." It is "have people like my followers bought something like this from me before." Those are very different questions. Interesting gets a like. Relevant gets a sale.

The honest version of this filter requires knowing what you genuinely sell, not what you assume you sell. Your memory drifts. You think you are a "home and kitchen" creator, but the data might say your audience actually buys beauty from you and ignores the kitchen stuff entirely. Promote to the reality, not the self-image.

## Filter 2: What is the commission rate?

Once a product passes relevance, look at how it pays. Commission rates run roughly 1% to 20% depending on category, and that range is enormous.

A relevant product in a 10% category is worth chasing. A relevant product in a 1% category needs to sell in serious volume to be worth the same post. Neither is automatically disqualified, but you should know the number before you commit, because two products that look equally appealing can pay you ten times differently.

The move is not "only promote high-commission categories." It is "between two equally relevant products, lean toward the one that pays more." Relevance first, then rate as the tiebreaker.

## Filter 3: Is the price in the sweet spot?

Price is the conversion dial most people ignore. There is a sweet spot, and it sits between two failure zones.

- **Too cheap** and the commission is not worth the post, even at a good rate. A 10% cut of an eight dollar item is eighty cents.
- **Too expensive** and conversion craters, because impulse buying falls off a cliff as the price climbs and people start "thinking about it" instead of clicking buy.

The reliable middle is the range where someone can say yes without a family meeting. For most audiences that is a comfortable, treat-yourself price point rather than a major purchase. Higher-ticket items can absolutely work, but they convert slower and need more trust, so weight your storefront toward the easy yeses.

> The best converting product is rarely the most expensive or the cheapest. It is the one priced where saying yes feels like no big deal.

## Filter 4: Is there demand right now?

A great product at the wrong time of year is a dead post. Demand is seasonal, and the creators who convert best are the ones surfing the wave instead of paddling against it.

Beach gear in winter, cozy blankets in July, tax-prep tools in November: technically relevant, badly timed. The same product that ignores you in the off-season can convert effortlessly when demand peaks. Part of product selection is simply asking, "is anyone searching for this right now?"

This is the filter that is hardest to run by eye, because seasonal demand shifts constantly and you cannot intuit it for every category at once.

## Putting the four filters together

Here is the whole framework in one pass. Before a product earns a spot on your storefront, it should clear:

1. **Relevance** to what your audience genuinely buys (the dealbreaker).
2. **A commission rate** worth your effort, used as the tiebreaker between relevant options.
3. **A price** in the easy-yes sweet spot.
4. **Live demand**, ideally with the season working for you.

A product that clears all four is a near-lock. A product that fails relevance is a skip no matter how shiny the other three look. Run this filter consistently and your storefront stops being a pile of hopeful guesses and starts being a curated set of things that actually move.
