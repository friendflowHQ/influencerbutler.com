Learning to read the Amazon best seller rank is one of the quietest superpowers a creator can pick up. While other people are guessing what their audience might want, you can look at a product page and get a rough read on whether the thing is actually selling right now. That little orange badge and the numbers around it are free demand signals, sitting in plain sight, and most creators scroll right past them.

Here is the core idea. Your content does a lot less convincing when the product already has built-in demand. Featuring something that is already selling well means you are riding a wave instead of trying to start one. Featuring something nobody is buying means your video has to do all the heavy lifting of creating desire from scratch, which is a much harder job. So before you decide what to film, it pays to ask the product page a few simple questions.

## What the Best Seller Rank actually tells you

The Best Seller Rank (BSR) is Amazon's way of saying how well a product is selling compared to others in its category, updated frequently based on recent sales. A lower number means more sales. A product ranked #50 in its category is moving far more units than one ranked #40,000.

But the rank by itself is only half the story. The trend matters more than the snapshot.

- **A low, stable rank** means steady, reliable demand. Great for evergreen content that keeps earning.
- **A rank that is climbing fast** (the number dropping) means something is heating up. Could be seasonal, could be a trend, could be a recent feature. Worth catching early.
- **A high or sliding rank** means demand is thin or fading. Your content would have to manufacture the interest.

> Rank is a snapshot of momentum, not destiny. A product climbing the ranks is a wave you can ride. A product sinking is one you would be paddling against.

One caution: ranks are relative to category. A #100 in a giant category like Kitchen is a monster seller; a #100 in a tiny niche category might be modest. Always read the rank against the category it sits in.

## Review velocity: the signal under the signal

If BSR tells you how much something is selling, review velocity tells you how fast interest is building. Look at how recently the reviews are landing, not just the total count.

A product with 50,000 reviews accumulated over a decade is established but maybe plateaued. A product with 2,000 reviews where hundreds arrived in the last month is catching fire right now. For a creator, the fast-accumulating one is often the better bet, because you are featuring something on the way up rather than something that already peaked.

The combination is what you want to read together:

1. **Healthy rank plus fast recent reviews** equals proven, rising demand. This is your sweet spot.
2. **Healthy rank plus slow reviews** equals a steady evergreen earner. Reliable, not exciting.
3. **Weak rank plus fast reviews** equals something new that might be heating up. Watch it.
4. **Weak rank plus slow reviews** equals a product asking your content to do all the work. Skip unless you have a special angle.

## Pair the signals with your own sales

Demand signals tell you what the broad market wants. Your own order history tells you what your specific audience wants, and those are not the same thing. A product can be a runaway best seller across all of Amazon and still flop for your followers because it does not fit your niche.

So treat best seller signals as a filter, not the final word. The real winners are usually products that pass both tests: they are selling well on Amazon AND they match the kinds of things your audience has already bought from you. That overlap is where conversion lives.

This is exactly why keeping honest order data matters. Your memory of what sold is unreliable and flattering; your actual sales record is neither. ## A simple product-picking routine

You do not need fancy tools to put this into practice. A repeatable habit beats a clever one-off analysis:

- **Check the rank and recent reviews** before you commit to filming anything. Thirty seconds on the product page saves you a wasted video.
- **Favor products climbing the ranks** over ones sliding down, especially heading into their season.
- **Cross-check against your own sales** so you are not chasing a best seller that your audience has no interest in.
- **Watch for the campaign overlap.** A product with good demand signals that also carries a Creator Connections bonus is a strong pick, because the demand is already there and the commission stacks higher.
