Most creators run out of outfit ideas long before they run out of products worth promoting. You can only photograph yourself wearing so many things in a week. AI Influencer Butler exists for the gap in between: it builds a post of a model wearing your real, in-stock products, without you putting on a single outfit.

It is not a stock-photo generator and it is not a face-swap gimmick. It searches your actual Amazon and Walmart catalogs for real products, renders a model wearing them, and assembles the result into a branded post, start to finish, from inside the desktop app. Here is exactly how it works, what one of these posts actually costs, and the guardrails that keep its autopilot mode from running away with your card.

## Picking who wears the clothes

The first decision is the model, and the app gives you three honest options instead of pretending there is only one right answer.

- **A generic AI model.** No setup, works immediately.
- **"My clone: [name]"** - a model trained on your own face, built with the separate AI Clone Butler feature. The panel has a direct "Create a model of you" button that jumps you straight into that setup if you have not made one yet.
- **"Use my own photo."** You upload a real photo of yourself and the app places it into the look as-is, with no AI face generation at all. It is the cheapest option in the panel because there is no model render to pay for, only the compositing step.

Whichever you pick, the same pipeline runs behind it: the app searches your real Amazon and Walmart listings, renders the model wearing those actual garments (not a generic mockup, the real product), and composites the result into a layout.

## Choosing a look

Next is style. You can pick from a saved style pack, or open "Generate styles with AI," type an aesthetic in plain words (the field's own placeholder suggests something like "New England preppy"), pick a season, and click "Suggest looks." The app proposes a cohesive style built from your own OpenAI key, so you are not stuck scrolling a fixed list of presets.

Below that are the practical settings that decide what actually shows up in the post:

- Which stores to pull from (Amazon and Walmart are both checked by default, you can turn either off).
- Pieces per look, from 1 to 9, defaulting to 4.
- A max price per piece, with 0 meaning no limit at all.

There is also a quietly useful checkbox: "Turn product photos that show a person into flat lays," on by default, which cleans up listing photos that already have someone modeling the item so they do not look strange sitting next to your AI model. That cleanup costs a few cents per photo, which the app tells you up front rather than burying in a settings page somewhere.

## The branding is the point, not an afterthought

A look is not just one picture. You pick from a grid of real collage templates, OutfitFinds, FlatLayMirror, WalmartCartMirror, AmazonBoxMirror, plus a handful of full "scene" templates that place the model in a seasonal setting (a fall porch, a snowy lodge, a beach boardwalk) rather than a flat product grid. You can fix the scene to a specific location yourself, or leave it blank and let the app pick something seasonally appropriate.

Branding fields sit right next to the template picker: a title (the placeholder example is "The Perfect Finds by You"), a subtitle ("Amazon Fashion" is the example), and background, accent, and text colors with their own hex fields, plus a one-click "Load my Collage Templates colors" button so a look matches whatever branding you have already set up elsewhere in the app. Nothing here is generic. The post comes out already wearing your name.

If you would rather post a single natural-looking photo instead of a branded collage, there is a "Candid photo instead of a collage" checkbox that swaps the whole output for one photo of the model wearing the look, no title band, no grid.

## What "Create this look" actually produces

Clicking the main "Create this look" button kicks off a real pipeline, not a single API call: it finds the actual products, renders the model wearing them, and either builds the branded collage or, for scene templates and candid mode, paints the whole image in one pass so the model, the real products, and the background design all land together.

What comes back is not just a finished image with nothing to do next. You get **Post now** (pushes straight to your connected platforms), **Add to storefront**, **Approve** (saves it to a looks library for later instead of posting immediately), plus **Show file**, **Copy image**, and **Download image** if you want to use it somewhere the app does not post to directly. An image-size selector lets you export the same look as a square for Instagram and Facebook feed, a 9:16 for Stories, Reels, and TikTok, or a 2:3 for Pinterest, so one generation covers every platform's aspect ratio instead of forcing you to regenerate per platform.

There is a separate tool in the same panel worth knowing about: a Facebook cover-photo maker. Give it a title, a tagline, whether it is for a Page or a Group, and a one-line "vibe," and it generates three cover-photo variants of your picked model with the title rendered right on the image.

## Autopilot, and the spending cap that actually holds

This is the part worth reading closely before you turn it on. Autopilot does not fire on a timer you have to babysit. It wakes up at wall-clock intervals inside a configurable daytime window, at least 20 minutes apart and never more than 8 hours apart, and if a scheduled cycle happens to wake up outside your posting window, it skips that cycle rather than posting in the middle of the night. Each cycle rotates through your style catalog and your chosen templates round robin, so you are not seeing the same layout every single time.

The number that matters most is the **daily spend cap**, which defaults to $5 and can be set anywhere from $1 to $500. A single look runs roughly $0.12 for the render, upscale, and captions, with add-ons on top: about $1.60 more if you also want a Reel or TikTok video made from the same look, $0.08 for the flat-lay photo cleanup, $0.05 if your clone is compositing directly onto a product. Here is the part that actually protects your card: the app checks whether generating the next look would push today's spend over the cap *before* it generates anything, not after. It does not generate and then apologize. Once a day's cap is hit, autopilot simply stops creating new looks until the next day.

Posting itself has its own per-platform "Auto" toggles, Instagram, Facebook (Pages or your personal Profile), Pinterest, TikTok, Amazon Storefront, and Walmart Storefront. If you turn on posting to your personal Facebook Profile, it deliberately shares the same daily pacing and limits as Deals Butler's Facebook posting, specifically so autopilot cannot post more often than is already considered safe for that account. There is also a "Facebook first comment" field that accepts a `{link}` token, so you can keep your shopping link out of the caption itself and tuck it into the first comment instead.

## A realistic first run

Say you pick "New England preppy," let the AI suggest a style, keep Amazon and Walmart both on with 4 pieces per look and no price cap, choose the OutfitFinds template, and click Create this look. What comes back is a branded collage: your model (clone, generic, or your own photo) wearing four real garments pulled live from your stores, with "The Perfect Finds by You / Amazon Fashion" across the top in your own colors. From there you either hit Post Now to send it to Instagram, Facebook, and Pinterest in one move, or Approve to park it in your looks library and decide later.

AI Influencer Butler is still tagged Beta in the app, which is the honest way to describe it: review your first handful of looks by hand before you hand autopilot the keys, especially if you are running it on your personal Facebook Profile where the daily posting limits are doing real protective work.
