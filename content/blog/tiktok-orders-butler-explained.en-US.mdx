If you sell through TikTok Shop's affiliate program, you already know the feeling: you want to know what you've actually earned, and TikTok's own interface makes you dig for it. Orders, commission, and settlement status all live in different corners of their creator tools, and none of it is quick to check from your phone between posts. TikTok Orders Butler is a small, deliberately simple answer to that: one dashboard, inside the app, that shows your TikTok Shop affiliate orders and commission without the digging.

It lives under a **TikTok Butler** section in the left nav, and right now the whole TikTok suite is marked **Beta**. That's not a throwaway label. TikTok Shop's affiliate program is newer than Amazon's or Walmart's, and this corner of the app has had less time in front of real creators than the rest of Influencer Butler. Treat it as a useful early tool, not a finished, battle-tested one.

## What it actually shows

Open the dashboard and you get four things:

- **Order count.** How many TikTok Shop affiliate orders you have on record.
- **Projected commission.** Commission on orders that haven't settled yet. These are real orders TikTok has recorded, but the payout hasn't been finalized.
- **Settled commission.** Commission that's actually finalized and payable. This is the number that matters when you're deciding what you can count on.
- **A table of individual orders**, so you can see the orders behind those totals rather than just trusting the summary.

There's a **Refresh orders** button, and that's effectively the whole interface. No charts to configure, no filters to learn, no settings panel. You load the dashboard, you look at four numbers and a table, and when you want current data you click refresh.

## It's read-only, and that's the point

Worth saying plainly: TikTok Orders Butler doesn't touch or modify anything on TikTok. It doesn't post, doesn't message buyers, doesn't change your shop settings, doesn't cancel or adjust orders. It reads data from your TikTok Shop affiliate account and displays it. That's the entire feature.

That might sound like a modest thing to build a whole dashboard around, and it is modest, on purpose. Most of Influencer Butler's other tools are trying to automate something, posting a look, scheduling a deal, drafting a message. This one isn't trying to do any of that. It exists because checking "what have I actually earned on TikTok Shop" is its own small chore, and a chore worth solving doesn't need to be complicated to be useful. If you've ever opened TikTok's creator interface just to answer that one question, this is meant to save you that trip.

## Refreshing doesn't duplicate your orders

The one technical detail worth understanding is how refresh behaves. Clicking **Refresh orders** pulls the latest data from TikTok Shop, and then merges and deduplicates it against what's already in your dashboard. It doesn't wipe the table and rebuild it, and it doesn't append a second copy of an order you already have on file. New orders get added, existing orders get updated if their status changed, and nothing shows up twice. You can refresh as often as you want to check in on a busy day without worrying that your order count or your totals will start drifting from reality because of duplicate rows.

## Projected versus Settled: the one piece of nuance

This is the part worth reading slowly, because it's the only genuinely non-obvious thing about the whole feature.

When someone buys through your TikTok Shop affiliate link, TikTok records the order and calculates a commission on it right away. That number shows up in your **Projected commission** total. But TikTok Shop doesn't pay out the moment an order is placed. Orders go through a settlement process, returns windows close, payment clears, TikTok finalizes its own books, and only then does that commission move from projected to **Settled**. Settled is the number that's actually been finalized and is payable to you.

So an order can show commission in your dashboard well before TikTok has actually finalized and paid it out. That's not a bug or a delay on the dashboard's end, it's just how TikTok Shop's own payment pipeline works. Projected commission is a reasonable, real-time estimate of what's coming. Settled commission is the number you can rely on as locked in. Neither one is "wrong," they're answering different questions: Projected answers "what's in the pipeline," Settled answers "what's actually mine."

## A realistic example

Say one of your TikTok videos featuring a product starts to take off. You open the dashboard and hit refresh. The order count ticks up. Projected commission climbs through the day as new orders keep landing, each one adding its share to that running total before TikTok has settled any of it. That's the useful, almost addictive part of watching a video do well: you can see the orders arriving in close to real time instead of waiting for TikTok's own reporting to catch up.

A couple of weeks later, you check back in. The order count from that day hasn't changed, but the split between Projected and Settled has shifted: a chunk of what showed as projected that day has now moved into Settled, because TikTok has finished processing those orders and the payout is finalized. Refresh again, and the table updates to reflect that without creating new rows for orders you already had. What you're left with is a clear before-and-after: what looked promising the day it happened, and what actually turned into money you can count on.

## Where this fits

TikTok Orders Butler isn't trying to be a TikTok Shop analytics suite, and it isn't trying to replace TikTok's own seller tools for anything beyond this one view. It's a focused answer to a specific, recurring question: orders, Projected commission, Settled commission, in one place, refreshed on demand. Given that it's still Beta, it's worth checking it against TikTok's own numbers periodically rather than treating it as the single source of truth on day one, but as a quick way to see where you stand on TikTok Shop without leaving the app, it does exactly what it sets out to do.
