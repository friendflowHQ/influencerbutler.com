If you have ever scrolled an Amazon product page and seen a little carousel of short customer videos showing the thing in someone's actual kitchen or bathroom, you have seen Amazon onsite video reviews in the wild. They are the quietest, most underrated income stream the Amazon Influencer Program offers, and most new creators ignore them in favor of chasing followers on social. That is backwards. Onsite videos are where you earn from people who are already on Amazon, wallet out, deciding whether to buy.

This guide walks you through what they are, how to make them, and how to get them seen. No studio required. The whole appeal is that a normal person filming a real product on their phone often converts better than a glossy ad, because shoppers trust it more.

## What onsite video reviews actually are

Once you are accepted into the Amazon Influencer Program, you can upload short product videos that appear directly on the product detail page, in the videos section, right next to the photos and reviews. When a shopper watches your video and then buys that product (or anything else in that session, within the window), you earn a commission.

The magic is the placement. You are not driving cold traffic from Instagram and hoping people convert. Amazon is putting your video in front of someone who searched for this exact product and is one decision away from buying. That is the warmest traffic in affiliate marketing, and you get it for free, on a platform that does the distribution for you.

A few things make onsite videos special:

- **They are evergreen.** Film once, and the video can keep earning for years as long as the product sells.
- **They scale with catalog size.** Each video is a small earning asset. Ten videos is ten little salespeople. A hundred is a sales team.
- **They reward usefulness over polish.** A clear, honest demo beats a cinematic ad most of the time.

## How to film one that converts

You do not need gear you do not already own. A phone, decent daylight, and a steady hand cover most of it. What matters is the content, and good onsite videos tend to follow a simple shape.

1. **Show the product fast.** The first second should make it obvious which product this is. No long intros, no "hey guys." Shoppers are deciding in real time.
2. **Demonstrate the thing in use.** Show it working. If it is a blender, blend something. If it is a jacket, wear it. People want to see it exist in a real space, not on a white background they already saw in the listing.
3. **Answer the question the listing photos cannot.** How big is it really? Is the material flimsy? Does the lid actually seal? You are filling the gaps that make people hesitate.
4. **Be honest about the trade-offs.** A small "the only downside is it's a bit loud" builds more trust than relentless hype, and trust is what converts.
5. **Keep it short.** Thirty to sixty seconds is plenty. You are confirming a decision, not making a feature film.

> The best onsite video is the one that answers the exact doubt a shopper has at 11pm with the product in their cart. Be that answer.

## Getting your videos to rank and show

Uploading is not the finish line. Amazon decides which videos surface in that carousel, and a few habits help yours show up.

- **Match the video tightly to the product.** Review the product you actually filmed, on its real listing. Mismatched or generic videos get buried.
- **Write a clear, keyword-honest title.** Describe what the video shows in plain language a shopper would search. No clickbait, just clarity.
- **Lean into products with traffic.** A great video on a product nobody searches for earns little. A decent video on a high-demand product earns steadily. Volume of shoppers matters as much as quality.
- **Keep filming.** Amazon tends to favor active creators, and more videos means more surface area. This is a quantity-and-quality game, not one or the other.

This is where product choice quietly decides your income. The skill is picking products that already have demand and a commission worth your time, so your video lands on a page shoppers are actually visiting. ## A simple starting routine

If you are brand new to onsite video reviews, here is a low-stress way to begin.

1. Pick five products you genuinely own and like, ideally ones with healthy demand.
2. Film a clean 30 to 60 second demo of each, phone and daylight only.
3. Upload them with clear, honest, searchable titles to the matching listings.
4. Wait, then check which ones earned. Make your next five based on what worked.
5. Repeat weekly. The catalog compounds, and so does the income.

That loop, run patiently, is how creators go from a few dollars to a real stream. The first videos are the hardest because they feel like they are doing nothing. Trust the compounding.
