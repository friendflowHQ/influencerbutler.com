Every creator has a short list of brands they consistently earn well with. Maybe it is a kitchen gadget brand, a skincare line, a supplement company, whatever it is, you know their products convert for your audience and you have posted them before. The problem is you only find out they released something new when you happen to scroll past it, or worse, when a competitor already posted it first and picked up the early clicks.

Brand Release Butler exists for exactly that gap. You follow the brands you care about, it watches their Amazon catalog in the background, and the moment something new shows up, it tells you, with a one-click path straight into a draft post.

## Following a brand

Following a brand is deliberately low-friction. Type a name into the search box, and if it matches a brand in the catalog, a dropdown shows up with the match, click Add and you are following it.

If the brand you have in mind is smaller and does not show up in that dropdown, you are not stuck. Press Enter, or click "Follow anyway," and it follows the exact brand name you typed. Detection still works by matching that brand name against product listings, so following a smaller or newer brand works the same way as following a big one. You do not need the brand to already be in some pre-built list for this to be useful.

## The first check is silent, on purpose

This is the part worth understanding before you follow your first brand, because it explains what you will and will not see on day one.

The first check after you follow a brand is silent. It baselines the brand's current catalog, everything that brand already has listed gets recorded, but none of it triggers an alert. That is the whole point: you followed this brand to hear about what is new, not to get a few dozen alerts for products that have been live for a year. Only products that show up after that baseline trigger an alert.

In practice this means the moment you follow a brand, nothing happens, and that is correct behavior, not a bug. The alerts start on the next check, once there is actually something new to compare against.

## How you hear about it

The panel gives you two ways to get notified: a popup inside the app, which is on by default, and email, which you can turn on alongside it or use instead. Either way, you are not stuck refreshing a page to find out if anything changed.

At the top of the panel there is a stats strip with four live counts: Brands followed, Products tracked, New releases, and Unread alerts. It is a quick gut check, at a glance you can see how many brands you have under watch, how large the tracked catalog has grown, and whether anything is sitting unread that deserves a look.

## Checking on a schedule, or checking right now

Brand Release Butler follows the same pattern as the other alert Butlers in the app. There is a "Check automatically" toggle, and when it is on, you pick a "Check every" interval: 3, 6, 12, or 24 hours. If you would rather not wait for the next scheduled check, there is a manual "Check now" button that runs one immediately. The panel also shows you next-check and last-check timestamps, so you always know where things stand instead of guessing whether it is actually running.

Worth being upfront about: coverage is a sample of each brand's catalog, not a guarantee that every single new SKU surfaces the instant it goes live. For most brands that is not a meaningful gap, but it does mean a brand-new item can take a little while to show up in an alert rather than being caught in the first minute it exists. That is the honest tradeoff of checking on an interval instead of hammering Amazon continuously, and it is still faster than finding out from someone else's post.

## A concrete example

Say you follow "Acme Home" because their small kitchen appliances have done well for you before. You follow it, the first check baselines their current catalog silently, nothing happens yet. Three days later, Acme Home lists a new air fryer. Your next scheduled check (or a manual Check now) picks it up, since it was not in the baseline, it counts as a new release, and you get a popup and an email.

You open the alert and click "Build a post." That is where the real time savings show up.

## What "Build a post" actually does

Clicking Build a post on a new release maps the product straight into a Deals Butler post, using the same mapping pipeline Deals Butler already uses for harvested deals. It auto-fills the product title, the product URL, the image URL (pulled directly from the real Amazon listing, so it is the actual product photo, not a placeholder), and the ASIN. It also drops in a notes field along the lines of "New release from Acme Home," so you know at a glance why this post exists.

A couple of fields are deliberately left blank: percent-off and original price. There is no discount yet, it is a brand-new release, so showing a fake percentage would be dishonest. The deal-floor guard that normally holds back posts with a weak discount is designed to not block a blank-percent new release, so an honest "nothing to discount yet" post does not get silently suppressed by a filter meant for something else.

The brand name carries onto the post's brand reference too, and that matters for a separate reason: if a brand-wide Creator Connections promo code exists for Acme Home, the Promo Code Hub feature auto-attaches it to the post. So in the example above, your air fryer draft could already be carrying a working promo code before you have touched a single field.

One thing to be clear about: this does not generate an AI-written caption for you. What you get is a structured head start, title, price scaffold, image, ASIN, not a finished post. It still goes through Deals Butler's normal caption-and-publish flow afterward. The win here is not having to go find the product, copy the URL, download the image, and look up the ASIN by hand. It is a running start, not a finish line.

Depending on your settings, the resulting post lands as either "draft," queued for you to review and publish, or "scheduled," flowing straight through your existing Deals Butler posting schedule.

## Choosing review-first or auto-post

Each brand you follow has its own "Auto-post new releases" toggle, and you choose between two modes: "Review first," which drafts the post and waits for your approval, or "Post right away," which auto-publishes straight through your existing Deals Butler posting settings. If you have a brand you trust completely and want to be first to post no matter what, auto-post handles it without you lifting a finger. If you would rather eyeball every new release before it goes out, review-first keeps you in the loop.

## What it costs

Brand Release Butler is free forever. It sits under the Amazon Butler section of the app and does not require a paid plan to use. That is worth distinguishing from Campaign Alert Butler, a separate paid feature that handles Creator Connections campaign alerting specifically, a different job entirely from watching brand catalogs for new releases.

If you have a handful of brands you already know convert, following them costs nothing and the only real decision left is whether you want review-first or auto-post once the alerts start coming in.
