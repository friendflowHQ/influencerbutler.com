You can find the best deal on the internet, write the cleanest post about it, and still earn nothing if you put it in the wrong place. Deal posting lives and dies on distribution. The product matters less than the question of where to post deals so the right people see them at the right moment. A 70% off find dropped into a dead Facebook group is worth less than a modest 20% off coupon shared with a hungry, active audience. So before you obsess over what to post, get clear on where.

This is a tour of the main channels deal posters use, what each one is actually good at, and how to think about spreading a single deal across several without losing your mind.

## Telegram: the deal poster's home base

If deal posting had a capital city, it would be Telegram. The platform was practically built for broadcast channels, and deal communities thrive there. Posts are instant, notifications are loud, and there is no algorithm deciding whether your followers see your message. You post, they get pinged, done.

What makes Telegram special for deals:

- **No feed algorithm.** Every subscriber gets every post in order. For time-sensitive deals, that immediacy is everything.
- **Link-friendly.** Telegram does not punish you for posting links the way some social platforms do.
- **Easy to forward.** A great deal gets forwarded to other chats, which grows your channel organically.

The downside is discovery. Telegram does not surface your channel to strangers, so you have to drive people there from somewhere else. Treat Telegram as the place your most committed deal fans live, and feed it from your other channels.

## Facebook Groups: built-in community, picky reach

Facebook deal groups can be enormous and genuinely engaged. People join them specifically to be sold deals, which removes the awkwardness you get elsewhere. The intent is already there.

The challenge is reach. Facebook decides how many group members actually see your post, and link-heavy posts sometimes get throttled. The workaround that works for a lot of posters is to lead with a genuinely useful description and let the link do its job in context, rather than dumping a bare URL. Engagement in the comments tends to push a post further, so a post that sparks replies travels.

Groups also reward being a real member, not a drive-by spammer. Posters who answer questions, react to others, and contribute beyond their own links build the kind of standing that keeps their posts from getting buried.

## Instagram and Stories: visual deals for a loyal base

Instagram is not a pure deal-feed platform, but it is where a lot of creators already have an audience, and that audience trusts them. Stories in particular are perfect for time-sensitive deals because they vanish in 24 hours, which matches the lifespan of most deals anyway. The Close Friends ring takes this further, letting you share your hottest finds with your most loyal fans without flooding everyone's feed.

Instagram works best when the deal is visual and the product photographs well. A gorgeous kitchen gadget at half price belongs here. A boring USB cable, less so.

> The channel is not the strategy. The channel is just where the strategy lands. Match the deal to the place, and match the place to the people who actually buy from you.

## WhatsApp, email, and the channels people underrate

A few quieter channels punch above their weight:

- **WhatsApp broadcast lists and communities** work like Telegram for audiences who live in WhatsApp. Very high open rates, very personal.
- **Email** is the one nobody can take away from you. Platforms change rules, algorithms shift, accounts get banned. Your email list is yours. A simple deals digest can become the most durable, highest-converting channel you own.
- **Pinterest** has a slower burn but a long tail, surfacing product pins to searchers for weeks.

You do not need all of these. You need the two or three where your specific audience actually hangs out, run well.

## How to choose your channels

Resist the urge to be everywhere. Spreading yourself thin across eight platforms usually means doing all of them badly. Start with one primary channel where your audience is most active and most ready to buy, then add a second to feed it. For most deal posters, that pairing is a broadcast channel (Telegram or WhatsApp) as the home base, plus a discovery channel (Instagram or a Facebook group) that drives new people to it.

Ask yourself three questions for any channel:

1. **Is my audience actually here, and are they in a buying mood?**
2. **Does this channel let deals move fast, or does an algorithm slow them down?**
3. **Can I realistically keep this channel fed every day?**

If a channel fails any of those, it is probably a distraction.
