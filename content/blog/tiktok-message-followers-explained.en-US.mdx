Somebody follows you on TikTok, and then nothing happens. No welcome, no link, no acknowledgment that you noticed. By the time you'd get around to messaging them yourself, the moment that made them hit follow has already passed. TikTok Message Followers is built for that exact gap: it sends a DM while the follow is still fresh, either to everyone who newly follows you or to a specific list of usernames you hand it, and it rotates through multiple message templates so the inbox doesn't read like a form letter.

It lives under TikTok Butler in the left nav, and the whole TikTok Butler section is still tagged Beta in the app. That matters more here than on most beta features, because this one sends real DMs to real people on your behalf. Get it wrong and you're not debugging a glitchy preview, you're spamming someone's inbox. The rest of this post is about how the feature actually works and, just as important, the sending limits that are supposed to keep you from doing that.

## Why message new followers at all

The pitch is simple: welcome new followers with something useful, like a link to your storefront or a specific offer, while it's still top of mind for them, instead of silence. Someone who just followed you is warmer than they will ever be again. A message that shows up within the hour and points them somewhere useful (your storefront, a current deal, whatever you're running) converts differently than the same link buried in your bio three weeks later when they've forgotten why they followed you in the first place.

That's the upside. The honest downside is that DMing people at scale is exactly the kind of automation that can go wrong fast if you're not careful. A tool that can message one new follower can just as easily message five hundred in an hour if nothing is stopping it, and TikTok treats that pattern as spam behavior regardless of how good your intentions are. That risk is the whole reason the app ships with explicit sending caps instead of leaving the pacing up to you, which is the part worth understanding before you turn anything on.

## Two targeting modes, two different jobs

TikTok Message Followers gives you two ways to decide who gets messaged, and they're meant for different situations rather than being two flavors of the same thing.

**Message New Followers** watches your account and automatically DMs anyone who newly follows you. This is the always-on welcome flow: set it up once, and every new follower gets a message without you having to notice they showed up. It's the right mode if what you want is a standing habit, a permanent "hello" for whoever arrives next, running quietly in the background.

**Message Select Usernames** is the opposite shape. Instead of reacting to new followers, you import a specific list via CSV and the app works through that list on its own. This is the one-time targeted outreach push: maybe you've got a list of accounts in your niche you want to reach, or people who engaged with a past campaign, or anyone else you've already identified by hand. It's a deliberate, bounded send rather than a continuous drip.

Knowing which one you actually need before you start is worth the thirty seconds it takes to decide. New Followers is for an ongoing relationship with whoever shows up next. Select Usernames is for a specific list you already have in mind.

## Templates that rotate, so it doesn't read like spam

However you're targeting, the actual messages come from a set of templates you write up front, not a single fixed line repeated to everyone. You can set them to rotate Random or in Round robin order, so two people who follow you back to back don't necessarily get the identical message.

The templates also support tokens, specifically `{handle}` and `{storefront}`, that get filled in per recipient. A template that reads something like "Hey {handle}, thanks for the follow! Here's where to find what I'm wearing: {storefront}" becomes a slightly different, personalized message for every single person it goes out to. That's a small detail that does a lot of work: it's the difference between a message that reads as obvious automation and one that at least looks like it was written with that specific person in mind, even though you only wrote it once.

## Follow-ups for the ones who go quiet

Not everyone replies to a first message, and TikTok Message Followers has a built-in answer for that: you can schedule a follow-up after 6, 12, or 24 hours, or set a custom delay, presumably aimed at recipients who haven't responded yet. Instead of a single message and then silence either way, there's a second touch built into the flow for people who didn't engage the first time, without you having to track who's already responded and manually chase them down.

## The sending caps, and why they're not optional

This is the part to read closely before you flip anything on. The app itself recommends explicit safety defaults: no more than 20 messages per hour, and 50 to 100 messages per day specifically for newer accounts. That last detail is deliberate. The app is upfront that the safe ceiling is lower for accounts without an established history, rather than defaulting everyone, brand-new account or not, to the same aggressive pace. A newer account sending at the volume an established one might handle fine is a much faster way to get flagged.

These aren't suggestions buried in a tooltip somewhere, they're the numbers the app puts in front of you as the recommended settings. If you're running TikTok Message Followers on an account you've had for a while with a track record of normal activity, the higher end of that range is more defensible. If your account is new, or new to this kind of outreach, start at the bottom of the range, or below it, and watch how it goes before you push further. The whole point of DMing people while a follow is still fresh is to build goodwill, not to get your account treated as a spam source before it's had a chance to grow.

## A realistic first setup

Here's a setup that stays inside the app's own recommended guardrails. You're running Message New Followers so every new follow gets a response automatically. You've written three templates that welcome the follower and point them to your storefront link using the `{handle}` and `{storefront}` tokens, set to rotate in round-robin order so the same line doesn't go out twice in a row. You've scheduled a 12-hour follow-up for anyone who hasn't engaged with the first message. And because your account is still relatively new, you've capped the whole thing at 50 messages a day, the bottom of the app's recommended range for accounts like yours, rather than reaching for the higher ceiling meant for accounts with more history behind them.

That's a flow that does what the feature is for, welcoming people while the moment is fresh, without treating every new follower as an excuse to blast messages as fast as the software allows.

## Worth remembering

TikTok Message Followers is still Beta, under the same TikTok Butler section as the rest of that suite, and it's the feature in that suite where getting the pacing wrong has the most direct consequence: real messages landing in real inboxes, and a real account reputation on the line if it's abused. Use the targeting mode that actually matches what you're trying to do, lean on the token-based templates so the messages don't read like a form letter, and treat the hourly and daily caps as the floor of good judgment rather than a limit to push against, especially while your account is still building a track record.
