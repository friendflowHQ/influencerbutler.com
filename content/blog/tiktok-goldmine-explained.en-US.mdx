Cold-prospecting creators for brand deals is mostly guesswork. You scroll a hashtag, open twenty profiles, and have no idea which of them have ever actually taken a paid post before. TikTok Goldmine skips that guesswork: you seed it with a handful of accounts or hashtags you already know are in your niche, and it goes out and finds more creators like them, then filters the results down to the ones who show a real track record of sponsored content.

That track record is the whole point. A creator with 80,000 followers who has never run a #ad is a cold outreach. A creator with 20,000 followers who has a "Paid partnership" label on three recent videos has already proven they take brand deals, have likely been paid before, and know how the process works. TikTok Goldmine is built to surface the second kind of account, not just the popular one.

It lives in the desktop app under TikTok Butler in the left nav, and the whole TikTok Butler section, including Goldmine, is marked Beta right now. It is the TikTok sibling of the existing IG Goldmine feature on Instagram, same idea of harvesting qualified creators from seeds, rebuilt for how TikTok actually structures accounts, hashtags, and partnership labels.

## Start with what you already know

You do not feed Goldmine a blank search. You seed it with usernames, hashtags, or both: five accounts you already follow in your niche, a hashtag like a product category tag, or a mix of both. The idea is to start from creators and content you already trust are relevant, and let the tool expand outward from there rather than crawling TikTok cold.

From there you pick a run mode, and the mode decides where the harvest actually looks for new accounts:

- **Seeds plus suggested** (the default). Starts from your seed accounts and also pulls in TikTok's own suggested and related accounts for each one, which is usually the widest net for a first run.
- **Seeds only.** Sticks strictly to your seed list and whatever their content surfaces, no expansion into TikTok's suggestions.
- **Suggested only.** Skips straight to TikTok's related-account suggestions without treating the seeds themselves as candidates.
- **Hashtag harvest.** Works from the hashtags you gave it instead of accounts, pulling creators who are posting under that tag.
- **Commenters.** Harvests the people commenting on specific videos, which is a different kind of signal: these are accounts actively engaging with content in your niche, not necessarily posting it themselves.

Seeds plus suggested is the one most people should start with. It gives the harvest both a known-good starting point and room to find accounts you have not heard of yet.

## The dials that keep a run from running away

Before you click run, there are four limits worth setting deliberately, because they decide both how long the harvest takes and how big the output is.

- **Minimum follower count**, defaulting to **10,000**. This is a deliberate floor, not an arbitrary number. It is tuned to skip past micro accounts and land on mid-size creators and up, the range where a brand-deal relationship is more likely to already exist and more likely to be worth your outreach time.
- **Max videos per profile to scan**, which caps how deep the tool digs into any one account's history before moving on.
- **Max suggested accounts per seed**, which caps how far the "suggested" expansion fans out from each seed you gave it.
- **Max profiles per run overall**, a hard ceiling on the whole harvest so a run does not balloon past what you actually meant to pull.

None of these change who qualifies. They change how much ground a single run covers, which matters because a wide-open harvest with no caps can turn into thousands of profiles to sift through by hand.

## Qualification rules: the part that actually makes the list useful

This is the step that turns "a pile of TikTok accounts" into "a prospect list." A found account only gets kept if it passes the qualification rules you turn on, and all of them are built around one question: has this creator actually done paid content before?

- **#ad disclosure hashtag.** Keep only accounts whose content carries a #ad-style disclosure tag.
- **"Paid partnership" label.** Keep only accounts whose videos carry TikTok's own built-in Paid partnership label, which is a stronger signal than a hashtag because TikTok itself is flagging it as a sponsored post.
- **Brand tagged or mentioned.** Keep only accounts where a specific brand is tagged or mentioned in their content, useful when you want creators who have worked with a particular kind of partner, not just any sponsor.
- **Verified only.** An option to restrict the list to verified accounts only, if you specifically want that extra layer of legitimacy.

Turn on any combination of these and Goldmine throws out every harvested profile that does not match. That is the difference between a list of popular accounts and a list of accounts with a history of taking brand money. The second list is the one worth actually emailing.

## Getting the list out

Once a run finishes, you export the qualified profiles to CSV or JSON, whichever fits your workflow. There is also a "Copy emails" shortcut sitting right next to the export options, which pulls just the contact emails out of the harvested list onto your clipboard so you can paste them straight into whatever you are sending outreach from, without opening the full export first.

## A realistic first run

Say you are working a home-fitness niche. You seed Goldmine with five accounts you already know post in that space, plus a hashtag that is common in fitness-gear content. You run Seeds plus suggested, leave the follower floor at 10,000, and turn on the Paid partnership qualification rule so you only keep accounts TikTok itself has flagged as running sponsored content.

The run goes out, expands past your five seeds into TikTok's own suggested accounts, scans each profile's recent videos, and throws out anyone who does not carry that Paid partnership label. What is left is a shorter list than you started with, but every account on it has TikTok-confirmed proof of taking a brand deal before. You export it as a CSV with emails attached, hit Copy emails for a quick batch, and you now have a prospect list built on an actual signal instead of follower count alone.

TikTok Goldmine and the rest of TikTok Butler are Beta, so treat a first run as a trial: check a sample of the qualified accounts by hand before you trust the list wholesale, and tighten your follower floor or qualification rules if the harvest comes back broader than you wanted. The tool is doing the filtering, but the judgment on who to actually reach out to is still yours.
