There is a specific kind of frustration that hits Amazon influencers about six months in. You have a storefront. It is packed with products. You have added dozens of idea lists, tagged everything, posted the link in your bio. And the commissions are, to put it kindly, sleepy. The instinct in that moment is to add *more*: more products, more lists, more links. That instinct is almost always wrong. If your storefront looks busy but earns little, what it needs is an amazon storefront makeover, not another fifty items. This checklist walks through the usual culprits, in the order worth fixing them.

The good news is that a tired storefront is rarely a lost cause. Most underperforming pages are not missing products. They are buried under them, organized for the creator instead of the shopper, and pointing visitors at the wrong things first. Fix those, and the same traffic you already have starts converting better.

## Start with the first screen, not the whole page

Shoppers decide whether to keep scrolling in the first few seconds. Your top idea lists and your cover images are doing almost all the persuasion work, and most creators never look at them again after setup.

Open your storefront on your phone, the way an actual visitor would, and ask three honest questions:

- Does the first list I see match what people actually come to me for?
- Are the cover images clear, or are they three blurry products crammed into one tile?
- If a stranger landed here, would they understand my niche in five seconds?

If the answer to any of those is no, you have found your first fix. Move your strongest, most on-brand list to the top. A storefront that opens with your best work converts better than one that opens with whatever you happened to make first.

## Cut the dead weight

A bloated storefront hides your winners. Every weak product is a small distraction that pulls attention away from the items that actually sell. Going through and removing low-quality or off-niche picks feels counterintuitive, but a tighter storefront almost always outperforms a sprawling one.

Here is a simple rule for the cull. For each idea list, ask: would I genuinely recommend everything in here to a friend? If a list is half filler, trim it down to the products you stand behind. A list of eight things you love beats a list of forty things you tolerate.

This is also where knowing what has actually sold becomes priceless. Memory is a terrible guide to which products earn. Our piece on [choosing products that actually convert](/blog/choose-products-that-convert) goes deeper on matching items to buyer intent, but the short version is: keep what sells, feature it prominently, and stop giving prime real estate to products that have never made you a dime.

## Fix your idea list titles

Titles are the most underused conversion lever on a storefront. "My Favorites" tells a shopper nothing. "Kitchen Gadgets I Use Every Single Day" tells them exactly what they are getting and why they should care.

Good list titles do two things at once. They describe the contents clearly, and they hint at a use case. Compare these:

- Weak: "Cleaning Stuff"
- Better: "Under-Sink Cleaning Supplies That Actually Work"
- Weak: "Travel"
- Better: "Carry-On Packing Must-Haves for a Week Away"

The better versions read like a person wrote them for a specific moment in a shopper's life. That specificity is what makes someone tap. Rewrite every generic title on your storefront and you will be surprised how much a few words move things.

> A storefront is a sales page, not a filing cabinet. Organize it for the person about to spend money, not for your own sense of tidiness.

## Order lists by intent, not by mood

Once your titles are clear, think about sequence. The lists nearest the top should serve your highest-intent shoppers, the people who are already close to buying. Save the aspirational, browse-y lists for further down.

If you sell a lot of one category, lead with it. If you have a seasonal moment coming up, like back-to-school or the holidays, surface the relevant list weeks ahead of the rush, not the day of. The goal is that the first thing a ready buyer sees is the thing they are most likely to buy.

## Check that your links actually work

This sounds basic, and that is exactly why it gets skipped. Products go out of stock. Listings get pulled. Prices change. A storefront that was perfect at launch slowly accumulates dead and dud links, and every one of those is a shopper who clicked, found nothing, and left.

Set a recurring reminder to spot-check your top lists once a month. You are looking for out-of-stock items, broken listings, and products whose price has crept up to the point where they no longer feel like the deal you originally featured. Swapping those out keeps your best lists earning instead of quietly leaking traffic.

## Make your storefront easy to find again

A makeover is wasted if nobody comes back. Once your page is tidy, make sure the path to it is obvious everywhere you show up: a clean link in your bio, the storefront mentioned in your content, a pinned post pointing people there. Returning visitors convert far better than first-timers, so reducing the friction to find you again pays off every month.

## The takeaway

An underperforming storefront almost never needs more products. It needs editing. Lead with your strongest list, cut the dead weight, rewrite vague titles into specific ones, order everything by buyer intent, and keep your links alive. That is the whole makeover, and it works on the traffic you already have.
