If you have spent any time trying to make money with Amazon links, you have run into the same confusing fork in the road that trips up almost everyone: Amazon Influencer Program vs Amazon Associates. They sound like the same thing. They both pay you a commission when someone buys through your link. And yet creators constantly sign up for the wrong one, or assume they need to pick a side, and quietly leave money on the table for months.

So let's clear this up. These are two different doors into the same building. One is built around web pages and text links. The other is built around your face, your storefront, and short product videos. The good news is you do not actually have to choose. The better news is that understanding the difference is what tells you where to spend your energy.

## The short version: same store, two front doors

Amazon Associates is the original affiliate program. It has been around forever. You grab a link to any product, drop it on a blog, in an email, or in a YouTube description, and you earn a category-based commission when someone buys. It is content-agnostic. Amazon does not care whether you have a face or a following, only that you are driving sales from somewhere off Amazon.

The Amazon Influencer Program is the newer, social-creator version. It gives you a **storefront**, which is your own page on Amazon where you curate products into idea lists. It also unlocks **onsite video reviews**, those short product videos that appear right on the product detail page where shoppers are already deciding whether to buy. That on-Amazon placement is the whole reason the Influencer Program exists.

Here is the part people miss: getting into the Influencer Program automatically gives you an Associates account too. They are linked. So the real question is not "which one," it is "which one deserves my attention this month."

## Amazon Influencer vs Amazon Associate: side by side

If you only want the comparison, here it is in one place. Amazon updates its program terms from time to time, so treat this as the general shape and check your own dashboard for the current details.

- **Who it is for.** Amazon Associates is for anyone sending traffic to Amazon from somewhere else: a website, a blog, an email list, an app or a social account. The Amazon Influencer Program is for social media creators with an audience on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok or Facebook.
- **How you get in.** Associates accepts most applicants up front, then reviews your account once you make your first qualifying sales (new accounts typically need three within 180 days to stay open). The Influencer Program reviews your social account itself, looking at things like following and engagement, before it approves you.
- **What you get.** Associates gives you product links, the SiteStripe link bar and earnings reports. The Influencer Program gives you all of that plus a storefront at amazon.com/shop/yourname, idea lists, onsite video reviews, livestreams and access to Creator Connections campaigns.
- **Where the sale happens.** Associates earns when you send shoppers to Amazon from off Amazon. The Influencer Program also earns from shoppers who are already on Amazon and find your storefront or your video on a product page.
- **Commission rates.** Both pay from the same category-based commission rate schedule, so the base rate on a sale is the same whichever way it came in. The difference is the extras: Creator Connections bonus commissions sit on top of that base rate, and only Influencer Program members can accept them.
- **Payments and reporting.** Influencer earnings flow through a linked Associates account, so you see everything in one report and get paid on one schedule.
- **Can you have both?** Yes, and most creators do. Joining the Influencer Program gives you an Associates account automatically, so it is never a true either-or.

## Where each program actually makes money

The mechanics matter, because they shape completely different workflows.

**Amazon Associates earns from off-Amazon traffic.** You send people from a blog post, a newsletter, a Pinterest pin, or a video description to a product page. If they buy within the cookie window, you get paid. Your job is to be a great recommender on platforms you already control. The ceiling is high if you have real traffic, and basically zero if you do not.

**The Influencer Program earns from on-Amazon shoppers.** Your storefront and your onsite videos get seen by people who are already on Amazon, mid-purchase, looking for a reason to click buy. You are not dragging cold traffic across the internet. You are nudging warm, ready-to-buy shoppers at the exact moment of decision. That is a fundamentally easier sale.

There is also a third layer that only Influencer Program members can touch, and it is where a lot of the upside lives in 2026: Amazon Creator Connections. Brands post campaigns with bonus commissions on top of the standard rate, and you accept the ones that fit. We wrote a full [guide to Amazon Creator Connections](/blog/amazon-creator-connections-guide-2026) if you want the deep version, but the headline is that those stacked bonuses can dwarf the base affiliate rate.

## So which one pays more?

The honest answer: it depends entirely on where your audience lives.

- **If your traffic is off Amazon** (a blog, a big email list, a YouTube channel with engaged viewers), Associates can pay beautifully, because you are pointing an existing river of attention at products.
- **If your "audience" is mostly Amazon shoppers you have never met**, the Influencer Program wins, because onsite videos and your storefront put you in front of buyers you could never reach otherwise.
- **If you are a social creator** (Instagram, TikTok, a newer YouTube), the Influencer Program is almost always the higher ceiling, because you get storefront curation, onsite video, and Creator Connections all at once.

> The trap is treating them as rivals. The creators who earn the most run both: Associates links wherever they post off Amazon, and the Influencer Program working quietly on Amazon in the background.

For most people reading this, the Influencer Program is the bigger opportunity, mainly because onsite videos keep earning long after you film them and Creator Connections adds bonus money on top. But Associates is the right tool the second you have meaningful off-platform traffic, and there is no reason to ignore it.

## Why the Influencer Program quietly compounds

There is a reason seasoned creators lean toward the Influencer Program over time. The work compounds.

An Associates link in a blog post earns as long as that post gets traffic, which is great. But an onsite video review sits on a product page that Amazon itself sends shoppers to, forever. You film once and it keeps converting. Layer in Creator Connections and a tidy storefront, and you have an asset that grows whether or not you posted today.

The catch, and there is always a catch, is the volume of housekeeping. To do the Influencer Program well you are supposed to keep your storefront current, check Creator Connections campaigns regularly so you do not miss the good ones, and accept the campaigns that actually match what your audience buys. ## How to choose, in one paragraph

If you are a creator with a social following, apply to the Influencer Program first. It hands you the storefront, onsite videos, Creator Connections, and an Associates account in one move. Use Associates links anywhere you post off Amazon, and let your storefront plus onsite videos do the on-Amazon work. If you are a blogger or have a big email list and no real social presence, start with Associates and add the Influencer Program later if you build a following. You are not picking a team. ## Amazon Influencer Program vs Amazon Associates FAQ

**What is the difference between the Amazon Influencer Program and Amazon Associates?** Amazon Associates is the general affiliate program: you earn a commission when someone buys through a link you shared off Amazon. The Amazon Influencer Program is built on top of it for social creators and adds a storefront, idea lists, onsite video reviews and Creator Connections campaigns, so you can also earn from shoppers already on Amazon.

**Is Amazon Associates the same as the Amazon Influencer Program?** No, but they are connected. Every Influencer Program account runs on an Associates account behind the scenes, which is why your influencer earnings show up in your Associates reports. You can be an Associate without being an Influencer, but not the other way around.

**Amazon influencer vs Amazon associate: which should I join first?** If you have a social following, apply to the Influencer Program first, because approval gives you both. If you have a website or email list but no real social presence, start with Associates and apply to the Influencer Program once you build a following.

**Do Amazon influencers earn a higher commission rate than associates?** Not on the base rate. Both use the same category commission schedule. Influencers tend to earn more in total because they have more ways to earn: onsite videos, a storefront shoppers find on Amazon, and Creator Connections bonuses on top of the base rate.

**Do I need a website to join Amazon Associates?** No. Associates accepts social media accounts and apps as well as websites. You will need to list where you plan to share links when you apply.

**Can I be in the Amazon Influencer Program and Amazon Associates at the same time?** Yes. That is the normal setup. Use Associates links wherever you post off Amazon, and let your storefront and onsite videos do the work on Amazon.

## The takeaway

Amazon Influencer Program vs Amazon Associates is the wrong framing. They are two doors into the same store, and most serious creators end up walking through both. Associates rewards off-Amazon traffic. The Influencer Program rewards on-Amazon presence, compounds over time, and opens the door to Creator Connections bonuses that Associates never touches. Pick based on where your people already are, then stop letting the busywork decide how much you earn.
