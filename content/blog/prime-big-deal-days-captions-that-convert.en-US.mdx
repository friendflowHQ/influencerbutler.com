During Prime Big Deal Days, every feed is a wall of deals. "Great price, grab it here" reads the same as the fifty posts around it, so it gets scrolled past. The creators who convert during the event are not louder. They are clearer. Here is the shape their captions take.

## The four-line formula

You do not need to be a copywriter. You need four lines, in this order:

1. **The specific thing.** Name the product and who it is for. "This under-desk treadmill" beats "this cool find."
2. **The number.** State the actual saving. "42% off, down to $118 from $203." A concrete number does the persuading for you.
3. **The reason it matters now.** One line on why today, not someday. "This is the lowest it has been all year" or "the price goes back up when the event ends."
4. **The plain instruction.** Tell them exactly what to do. "Tap the link in my bio, it is the first list." Do not make them hunt.

That is it. Specific, number, urgency, instruction.

## Let the app hand you the numbers

The reason most captions stay vague is that digging up the real discount and list price is annoying. It does not have to be. When you pull deals from the Promo Code Hub, the sale price, the list price, and the percent off are already sitting there. Copy them straight into line two. Accurate numbers are more persuasive than adjectives, and they take less effort.

## Urgency without the spam smell

The line between "act now" and "obvious spam" is honesty. Real urgency comes from a real fact: the event ends on a real date, the price really was higher last week, stock really is limited on lightning deals. Say the true thing plainly and you get the click. Invent fake countdowns and scarcity and you train your audience to ignore you.

## Write a handful, reuse them all week

Draft five or six of these captions today, before the event noise starts. Keep them in a note. During the event you swap in the product and the numbers and post, instead of staring at a blank box while the good hours tick by.

Clear beats loud every time, and never more than during a sale.
