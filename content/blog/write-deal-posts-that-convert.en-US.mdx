You can find the best discount on Amazon and still earn nothing from it if you write deal posts that nobody clicks. The deal is only half the job. The other half is the handful of words you wrap around it, the ones that decide whether someone scrolls past or stops, taps, and buys. The encouraging part is that high-converting deal posts are not magic or talent. They follow a simple, repeatable anatomy, and once you learn it, you can write deal posts that convert in under a minute, every time.

Let's break down that anatomy piece by piece, then put it together into a formula you can reuse for the rest of your deal-posting life.

## What a deal post is actually competing against

Before the formula, understand the job. Your deal post is not competing against other deal posts. It is competing against everything else on your reader's screen: their friend's vacation photos, a funny video, a news headline, a text from their mom. In that environment, you have roughly a second to earn a stop. Everything in a good deal post exists to win that second and then make acting feel easy and safe.

That is why the best deal posts are short, specific, and scannable. Long paragraphs lose. A wall of hype loses. A clear number and a clear reason win.

## The five parts of a deal post that converts

Every high-performing deal post has the same skeleton. Memorize these five parts and you will never stare at a blank caption again.

### 1. The hook

The first line has one job: stop the scroll. The best hooks lead with the thing your reader cares about most, which is almost always the saving or the product itself. "This $12 organizer is usually $40" stops people. "Hey everyone, hope you're having a great day, I found something I wanted to share" does not. Lead with the value, not the warm-up.

### 2. The number

Deals are about money, so put the money front and center. Show the old price and the new price together so the saving is instantly obvious. A reader should not have to do mental math to understand the deal. "$18, down from $45" does the work for them. The contrast between the two numbers is the single most persuasive thing in the whole post.

### 3. The honest reason

One short line on why this is actually worth it. Not three paragraphs of hype, just a single specific, truthful detail. "These are the ones with the lid that actually seals" or "I have this exact one and use it daily." Specificity reads as honesty, and honesty is what makes someone trust the link enough to tap it.

### 4. The urgency (only if real)

If the deal is genuinely time-limited, say so, because real urgency drives action. "Lightning deal, almost sold out" or "price snaps back tonight" gives the reader a reason to act now instead of later (which means never). The crucial word is *real*. Fake urgency works once, then trains your audience to ignore you. Only claim a deadline when there is one.

### 5. The link

Make the link obvious and the next step frictionless. Do not bury it. Do not make people hunt. The whole post has been building to this one tap, so put it where their thumb is already heading.

> The formula in one breath: stop them with the hook, prove it with the number, earn trust with the reason, push with honest urgency, and close with an easy link.

## A reusable template

Put the five parts together and you get something you can fill in for any deal in about sixty seconds:

1. **Hook:** the product plus the headline saving, in one punchy line.
2. **Number:** new price, old price, side by side.
3. **Reason:** one honest, specific detail about why it is good.
4. **Urgency:** the real deadline, if there is one.
5. **Link:** clear and immediate.

Here is what that looks like filled in: "This robe is buttery soft and it's $19 right now (normally $48). I gave one to my sister and she lives in it. Lightning deal, going fast. Link below." Five parts, one breath, done.

The beauty of a template is that it removes the agonizing. You stop writing each post from scratch and start running a format. That is also what makes posting several deals a day actually sustainable, which matters more than any single clever caption.

## Mistakes that quietly kill conversions

A few habits sink otherwise-good deal posts. Watch for these:

- **Burying the price.** If the reader has to dig for the number, you have lost them. Lead with it or put it in line two at the latest.
- **Over-hyping.** "INSANE DEAL YOU CANNOT MISS THIS" reads as spam. A calm, specific post out-converts a screaming one because it reads as trustworthy.
- **Fake or stale urgency.** Claiming "almost gone" on a deal that runs all month burns trust permanently.
- **No reason at all.** A price with zero context makes people hesitate. One honest line is all it takes to convert hesitation into a tap.

Avoiding these is mostly about discipline. The template above bakes the discipline in for you.
