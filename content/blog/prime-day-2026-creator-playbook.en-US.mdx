Amazon moved the calendar on us this year. Amazon Prime Day 2026 runs June 23 to June 26, which means the biggest commission window of your summer is about a week away, not parked in mid-July like it usually is. If you are an Amazon influencer, this is the four-day stretch that can quietly out-earn a normal month. So this is your Amazon Prime Day 2026 playbook: what to do before the 23rd, how to pick the deals worth posting, and how to keep up once the rush actually hits.

Here is the honest framing up front. Prime Day rewards two things, and only two: being ready before it starts, and being fast once it does. Most creators are neither. They scramble on the morning of the 23rd, post a handful of random deals, burn out by the 24th, and wonder why their commissions barely moved. The ones who win treat the days before like the event itself.

## Why Prime Day is different for creators

A normal day, you are competing for attention. During Prime Day, your audience is already in buying mode. They opened the app expecting to spend. That changes the math: the same product link that gets a polite scroll on a Tuesday gets clicked and bought on June 24, because the person seeing it has their card out and a self-granted permission to treat themselves.

That intent is the whole opportunity. You are not convincing anyone to shop. You are just deciding which products land in front of someone who already decided to.

It also means the window is short and crowded. Four days, every creator in your niche posting, deals expiring on their own timers. Speed and prep beat cleverness here. A perfectly edited video posted on the afternoon of the 26th is worth less than a quick, honest one posted the morning of the 23rd.

## The pre-event checklist (do this before June 23)

You have a few days. Spend them on setup, not on hoping. Knock out this list before Prime Day opens:

- **Clean up your storefront.** Pull dead products, fix broken idea lists, and make sure your best earners are easy to find. Shoppers who tap through from a Prime Day post land on your storefront, so it should look like a tidy shop, not a junk drawer. Our [storefront checklist that turns browsers into buyers](/blog/amazon-influencer-storefront-tips) walks through the specifics.
- **Pull your real order history.** Before you decide what to feature, know what your audience has actually bought from you. Your past sales are the single best predictor of what will sell again, especially during a buying surge.
- **Build a few themed idea lists in advance.** "Prime Day kitchen picks," "deals under $25," "the stuff I actually use." These give you something to link the moment a deal goes live, instead of scrambling to assemble a list mid-rush.
- **Stage your content.** Write hooks, draft captions, prep a repeatable video format. The less you have to invent on the 23rd, the more deals you can actually cover.
- **Know your rules.** Decide your minimum discount, your must-match-my-niche rule, and what you will skip. Tired-you at 9pm on day two will thank rested-you for setting them now.

## Pick deals the way your audience already shops

The temptation during Prime Day is to post everything with a red price tag. Resist it. A 60% discount on something nobody who follows you would ever buy is still zero dollars in commission. The deals that convert are the ones that match what your audience already trusts you for.

Run every potential post through one quick filter:

1. **Would my audience buy this at full price?** If yes, the discount is gravy. If no, the discount alone will not save it.
2. **Is the discount actually real?** Check that the "deal" price is genuinely below the recent baseline, not a fake markdown. Posting a fake deal during the one week everyone is price-checking will cost you trust fast. If price accuracy is a sore spot for you, [catching real Amazon price drops](/blog/catch-amazon-price-drops) covers how to tell a true low from a dressed-up one.
3. **Does it have momentum?** Healthy reviews and a high best-seller signal mean the product is already selling itself. Your content does less convincing.

> The creators who clean up during Prime Day are not the ones who post the most deals. They are the ones who post the right deals fast, then move on.

## Pace yourself across the four days

Prime Day is a sprint that lasts long enough to become a marathon if you are not careful. Four days of constant posting will wreck you if you go all-out on the 23rd. Spread the effort.

- **June 23 (open day):** Post your strongest, most obvious deals first. This is your highest-intent day. Lead with proven winners.
- **June 24-25 (the middle):** Rotate in your themed idea lists, answer questions, re-share the deals that performed. Mid-event is when fresh angles matter, because your audience has already seen the obvious stuff.
- **June 26 (close day):** Lean into urgency honestly. "Last day of Prime Day" is true and it works, as long as the deals behind it are real.

Build in actual breaks. A creator who paces across all four days out-earns the one who flames out by lunch on day two.

## After it ends: read the receipts

When the 26th wraps, do not just exhale and forget it. Spend twenty minutes looking at what actually earned. Which products converted? Which idea lists pulled traffic? Which formats flopped? That review is what makes next time easier, and it feeds directly into what you feature for the rest of the summer.
