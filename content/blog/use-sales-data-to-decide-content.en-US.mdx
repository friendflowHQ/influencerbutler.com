Most creators decide what to film next based on a feeling. A product caught their eye, a competitor posted something similar, or they are just tired of the last thing they made. Then they spend hours filming and editing, post it, and hope. Sometimes it lands. Often it does not. The frustrating part is that the answer to "what should I film next" is usually sitting right there in their own sales data, ignored. Your past sales are the single best predictor of what your audience will buy next, and reading them turns content from a guessing game into something a lot closer to a sure thing.

This is the difference between throwing darts blindfolded and aiming at a target you can actually see. Let's get into how to use your sales data to make smarter content decisions and stop wasting effort on products that were never going to convert.

## Your sales data already knows the answer

Here is the core idea: your audience has already told you what they buy. Every commission in your history is a vote. A product that sold once for you proves there is demand in your audience for that kind of thing. A product that sold five times proves it loudly.

When you ignore that and chase a shiny new product instead, you are betting against your own evidence. The product that just sold well for you is not "old news," it is a signal flashing "more of this, please." Doubling down on proven demand is one of the lowest-risk, highest-return moves a creator can make.

> Memory lies. You will swear a product flopped when it quietly sold all month, and you will hype a product that earned you nothing. Your sales data does not have feelings about it. Trust the data over the vibe.

## The patterns worth looking for

Reading your sales data is not about staring at a spreadsheet until your eyes cross. It is about spotting a handful of patterns that tell you what to do next.

- **Repeat sellers.** Products that keep selling, week after week, are proven winners. Make more content around them and around similar items. This is the clearest signal you have.
- **Category strength.** Maybe no single product dominates, but a whole category does. If your audience reliably buys kitchen gadgets, your next several pieces should probably live in that category.
- **Surprise hits.** Sometimes a product sells well that you did not expect or barely promoted. That is gold. It means there is untapped demand you have not fully served. Lean in.
- **Quiet duds.** Products you pushed hard that never sold are telling you something too: this is not what your audience wants from you. Stop forcing it.

You are looking for where the demand actually is, not where you assumed it would be. The two are different more often than creators like to admit.

## From data to a filming decision

So you have spotted the patterns. How do you turn that into a concrete decision about what to shoot this week? A simple loop works.

### 1. Start with your proven winners

Before anything else, ask: what sold recently, and what is close enough to it that I could feature next? Your strongest sellers and their cousins should be the first things on your filming list. This is the highest-confidence content you can make.

### 2. Expand outward from there

Once you have made content around the obvious winners, branch into adjacent products. If a specific water bottle sells, the broader hydration and gym category is worth exploring. You are following the demand outward, not jumping to an unrelated niche on a whim.

### 3. Match it to opportunity

Now layer in the opportunities. Is there a Creator Connections campaign offering a bonus on a product that also matches your sales data? That is the perfect storm: proven demand plus boosted commission. Those are the campaigns worth prioritizing, and our guide to [getting into more Creator Connections campaigns](/blog/amazon-creator-connections-guide-2026) covers how to spot them.

### 4. Retire the duds

Finally, stop spending effort on the products your data says nobody wants. Every hour you put into a proven dud is an hour stolen from a proven winner. Cutting losers is just as important as backing winners.

## A quick weekly habit

You do not need to do a deep analysis daily. A light weekly habit is plenty. Once a week, take ten minutes to look at:

1. What sold this week?
2. What kept selling that I should make more of?
3. What surprised me that I should explore?
4. What did I push that went nowhere, so I can drop it?

Four questions, ten minutes, and you walk away with a filming list grounded in evidence instead of mood. Over a few months, that small habit pulls your hit rate steadily upward, because you are increasingly making content about things your audience has literally already proven they will buy.
