If you are brand new to the Amazon Influencer Program, the hardest part is usually not filming. It is deciding what to film. You open your camera, look around the room, and freeze. Everything feels like it might be a waste of an afternoon.

Here is the good news: you do not need a warehouse of new gadgets or a big following to start earning. Your next profitable video is probably sitting on a shelf in your house right now. This guide shows you how to find it, using a simple, repeatable method and one tool inside Influencer Butler called Orders Butler.

## The #1 beginner mistake (and the fix)

The most common mistake new creators make is filming random products with no strategy. You grab whatever is nearby, shoot a nice clip, upload it, and then wonder why it earns almost nothing.

The problem is usually not your filming. It is your pick. A great video about a product nobody is searching for will sit quietly forever. A decent video about a product people are actively buying, on a page where almost no other creators have posted, can keep earning for months.

So the fix is simple to say and powerful in practice: film products that pair high demand with low competition. Demand means people are already buying it. Low competition means few other influencers have claimed that product page with a video yet. When you sit in that sweet spot, your video gets seen, and getting seen is the whole game.

## The winning workflow, step by step

Here is the exact routine we recommend for figuring out what products to film for Amazon shoppable videos. It takes just a few minutes.

1. **Open Orders Butler and refresh your order history.** Let it pull in your recent purchases so you are working from a complete list.
2. **Click Check Influencer Videos.** This fills in the Influencer videos and Est. sales/mo columns, the two numbers you are about to filter on.
3. **Click the Film These First filter.** This is a Quick Start Filter, a one-click chip sitting right above the table. It instantly narrows your whole order history to products that pair real demand with few competitor videos, and ranks what is left by opportunity, best pick at the top.
4. **Read the shortlist and pick your first three.** Do not overthink it. Grab the top few, favor things you can talk about honestly, and your next filming session is planned.

The sweet spot is the overlap: high sales, few videos.

## Quick Start Filters: one-click shortlists

Film These First is the filter most beginners should live in, but it is one of five Quick Start Filters above the table. Each one is a saved answer to "what should I film next," and clicking it both filters and sorts the table for you:

- **Film These First**: products with few competitor videos, a solid commission, and real demand, ranked by an opportunity score (estimated commission per sale times monthly demand, divided by the number of competitor videos). Products you have already covered drop to the bottom.
- **No Influencer Videos Yet**: scanned products with zero influencer videos, so yours would be the only one on the page.
- **High Commission**: products paying a 4% or higher commission rate, highest first.
- **Best Sellers**: products ranked in the top 1,000 of their category.
- **Hot Sellers**: products with 500 or more bought in the past month.

Film These First has its own settings, too. Click the gear next to it to tune the thresholds: max influencer videos, minimum commission percent, max sales rank, and minimum bought per month. The defaults (2 videos, 3% commission) are a good starting point, but if your list comes back empty, loosen them a little and run it again.

## Why this works: the carousel and the math

It helps to understand why the low-video threshold matters so much, because it is the part beginners almost always miss.

When a shopper lands on an Amazon product page, they do not see every video that exists for that item. They see a small carousel, often just a handful of videos, before they have to tap "see all." Most shoppers never tap "see all."

So think about what that means. On a product with 40 videos, yours is one of 40 fighting for a couple of visible slots, and your odds of being seen are tiny. On a product with 2 videos, you might be the third, sitting right there in the carousel where every shopper looks. Same effort, wildly different visibility.

And here is the encouraging part: even best-selling, high-demand products often have only a handful of videos total. Demand and competition do not always move together. That gap is the opening, and Orders Butler is built to find it for you.

## Bonus tips to get more from every session

A few small habits make this method work even better:

- **Refresh and enrich your order history first.** The more complete and up to date your data, the more accurate the demand and competition numbers. Run a refresh before each planning session.
- **Prioritize evergreen, everyday products.** A seasonal item spikes and fades. A kitchen tool, a storage bin, a phone accessory, or a cleaning gadget sells all year, so your video keeps earning long after you post it. These evergreen picks are also some of the best products for Amazon Inspire videos, because the feed keeps resurfacing them.
- **Batch-film your shortlist.** Once Orders Butler hands you five or ten winners, film them in one sitting. One setup, one burst of energy, a week or more of content.
