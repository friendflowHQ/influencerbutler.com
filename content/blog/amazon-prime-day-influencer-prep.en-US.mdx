If you are a Prime Day influencer, here is the thing nobody tells you until your first one goes sideways: the money is won in the days before the event, not during it. Prime Day moves fast. Deals appear and vanish in hours, your audience is buying with one tab open and a credit card in hand, and there is simply no time to be researching products, writing captions, or fixing broken links while the clock is running. The creators who clean up on Prime Day show up already prepared. Everyone else spends the event sprinting in circles.

So this is not a "post good deals" pep talk. This is a prep checklist. Do the work in advance, and Prime Day becomes a calm, profitable couple of days instead of a frantic blur. Skip the prep, and you will watch the best deals expire while you are still formatting a post.

## Why Prime Day rewards preparation over hustle

During the event, your audience is in buying mode and so is everyone else's. Attention is scarce, deals are time-limited, and the difference between a sale and a miss is often a matter of minutes. You cannot out-hustle that in real time. You can only out-prepare it.

Think about what eats your minutes when you are unprepared:

- Deciding which products to feature while deals are already live.
- Writing captions and finding the right angle on the fly.
- Double-checking that links work and go to the right place.
- Trying to remember which Creator Connections campaigns you accepted.

Every one of those is a task you can finish before Prime Day even starts. That is the whole game: move the work earlier.

## Your two-week prep checklist

### Two weeks out: pick your hero products

Do not wait for the deals to tell you what to feature. Start from what already sells for you. Pull your best-performing products from the last few months and the same period last year, then make a shortlist of items you would happily recommend even at full price. Those are your heroes. When the discounts land, a hero product on sale is an instant, high-confidence post.

This is also the moment to lean on your own order history. The products your audience actually bought from you are the ones most likely to convert again during a sale. If you are guessing from memory, you are leaving money on the table. Our guide on [choosing products that convert](/blog/choose-products-that-convert) walks through how to build that shortlist.

### One week out: line up campaigns and content

This is when you accept the right Creator Connections campaigns so the bonus commission stacks on top of the Prime Day discount. A product that is both on sale and carrying a campaign bonus is the best post you can make during the event, but only if you accepted the campaign in advance.

Then batch your content. Draft captions, prep your hooks, and stage your posts so that during the event you are scheduling, not creating. A simple template for each post type (the deal callout, the gift idea, the "I actually own this") removes all the agonizing.

> The goal for event day is to be a curator, not a creator. All the creating should already be done. On the day, you are just matching live deals to content you already made.

### Two to three days out: check every link

Nothing burns a Prime Day post like a dead or wrong link. Walk through your storefront, your idea lists, and your scheduled posts, and click every link. Confirm they resolve, confirm they tag you correctly, and fix anything broken now while you have the breathing room.

## During Prime Day: move fast on what is already prepped

When the event is live, your job narrows to three things:

1. **Catch the discounts that hit your hero products.** When a product you already planned to feature goes on sale, you post the content you already made. Fast and easy.
2. **Surface the campaign overlaps.** Any product that is both discounted and carrying a Creator Connections bonus goes to the front of the line.
3. **Stay present, not perfect.** Post consistently across the event rather than dumping everything at once. The sale runs for a day or two; pace yourself so you are visible the whole time.

The creators who try to discover and create simultaneously during the event always fall behind. The prepared ones just keep shipping.

## After Prime Day: capture what you learned

When the dust settles, do not just close the laptop and forget it. Spend twenty minutes reviewing what worked. Which products sold? Which posts drove clicks? Which campaigns actually paid?

That little review is what makes next Prime Day easier. You walk in with a proven hero list and a clearer sense of your audience's appetite. Each event compounds on the last, but only if you write down what you learned.

## The takeaway

Prime Day is a sprint you win in advance. Pick your hero products from real sales, accept the right campaigns early, batch your content, check every link, and on the day itself just match live deals to work you already finished.
