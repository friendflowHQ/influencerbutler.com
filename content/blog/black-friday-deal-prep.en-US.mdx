There's a version of Black Friday where you spend the week exhausted, scrambling to keep up with deals that drop faster than you can post them, and you end it with decent earnings but a body that feels like it ran a marathon. And there's a version where you cruise through the busiest shopping week of the year because everything was set up in advance. The difference between those two is Black Friday prep, and the good news is that almost all of it happens weeks before the actual day.

Black Friday is the deal influencer's biggest single opportunity of the year. Audiences are primed to buy, budgets are loaded, and people are actively looking for someone to point them at the best stuff. If you walk in prepared, the week mostly runs itself. If you walk in cold, the week runs you.

## Why prep beats hustle on Black Friday

Black Friday isn't won by who works hardest during the event. It's won by who decided what to do before the event started. When deals are flooding in and your audience is in a buying frenzy, you don't have time to design a workflow, write captions from scratch, or figure out which channels to prioritize. Every one of those decisions you make in advance is a decision you don't have to make at the worst possible moment.

The creators who earn the most on Black Friday tend to look almost calm during it. That's not luck. That's a checklist they completed in early November.

## The pre-game checklist

Here's what to have locked down before the week begins. Knock these out while things are quiet.

- **Your category shortlist.** Identify the product categories your audience actually buys from you. When the deals avalanche starts, you check these first instead of trying to cover everything.
- **Caption templates ready to fill.** Pre-write your deal post structure so the live work is just dropping in the product, price, and link. Our [guide to writing deal posts that convert](/blog/deal-posting-influencer-guide) covers the framing that works; bake that into your templates now.
- **Your channel order.** Decide exactly where you post and in what sequence. Trying to remember your distribution plan at peak chaos is how channels get neglected.
- **A verification routine.** Black Friday is prime fake-deal season. Inflated list prices are everywhere. Have a quick way to confirm a discount is real before you post it, so you don't burn trust during your highest-traffic week.
- **A pacing plan.** Black Friday momentum often runs from the week before through Cyber Monday. Decide how you'll spread your energy across that stretch instead of emptying the tank on Friday morning.

> The boring secret of a great Black Friday: the work that makes the week easy is the work you do before the week starts. Prep is the highest-paid hour you'll spend all season.

## Stock your inventory of go-to products

Before the deals drop, build a running list of the products you'd love to see discounted: the items your audience consistently buys, the categories that convert, the brands people ask you about. When Black Friday markdowns land, you cross-reference against this list instead of evaluating thousands of deals from a blank slate.

This is where your own sales history is gold. The products that earned you commissions all year are your best bets for what your audience will grab on Black Friday. You're not guessing what people want; you already know, because they told you with their wallets every other month.

## Plan your pacing across the whole week

Modern Black Friday isn't one day. Deals start trickling in the week before, peak around Friday, and roll straight through Cyber Monday. That's a five-to-seven-day window of elevated buying intent, and the creators who treat it as a single sprint burn out before the most lucrative stretch.

A simple way to pace it:

1. **Early week:** post the genuine early drops, set expectations, get your audience watching.
2. **Friday peak:** go heavier, but in focused blocks with breaks, not a constant manic stream.
3. **Weekend and Cyber Monday:** keep going. A lot of deliberate shoppers buy after the initial frenzy, and many creators have already quit by then, leaving the field open.
