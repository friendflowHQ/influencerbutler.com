Every "real person used this" post has the same bottleneck: a real person has to actually use it, in front of a camera, in decent light, holding still long enough for the shot. AI Clone Butler exists to remove that bottleneck without removing you from the picture. You give it reference photos of yourself once, and from then on it can put you in a new scene with a real product in your hand, on demand, without a photographer, a studio, or another photoshoot.

If you have looked at the bundled in-app help for this feature, ignore it. It still describes an old flow (upload 10 to 20 photos, click "Train this clone," wait a few minutes for training) that does not exist in the current app. There is no training job anymore. The real flow is faster and simpler than that, and it is worth walking through properly because the design choice behind it, skipping training entirely, is the whole point.

## Why no training step matters

Training a model on your face used to be the standard approach across this category: upload a batch of photos, kick off a job, wait, then generate. It works, but it puts a wall between "I want a photo of myself with this product" and actually getting one. AI Clone Butler's reference photos are not training data. They are used directly, as reference images, in the same generation call that also carries the product photo. The app says this plainly: "No training step: your photos are ready to use right away." Add one photo and you can generate immediately. There is no minimum that blocks you, no job queue, no waiting.

That does not mean more reference photos do nothing. More references, and a mix of distances, give the model more to work from, so the app nudges you toward a fuller set without making it mandatory.

## Section 1: Your clone

The first screen is where you manage who the clone is. You pick an existing clone or create a new one with a name (the field's placeholder reads "My clone"), which matters if you ever want more than one look, say a clone with glasses and one without, or you and a co-host both using the same account.

Under that are three categorized photo slots, each with its own "Add photos" button:

- **Face close-ups**
- **Waist up**
- **Full body**

The app's soft target is roughly 8 face photos, 6 waist-up, and 6 full-body, about 20 total, and it marks a clone "ready" once you clear a recommended minimum of 12 photos total with at least 3 full-body shots in the mix. That status is a nudge, not a gate. Generation works from even a single reference photo; "ready" just means the app thinks you have given it enough variety to produce consistent results across different scenes and crops.

Before any of this runs, there is a required consent checkbox, something to the effect of "These are photos of me, or of someone who agreed to be used this way." You cannot proceed without checking it, and that is deliberate: this feature is compositing a real person's likeness, so the app makes you confirm you have the right to use the photos before it will touch them.

One more thing worth knowing up front: each photo you add costs a few cents on your own OpenAI key. That is the entire cost of this section. There is no separate training charge, because there is no training.

## Section 2: Create a photo

Once your clone has at least one reference photo, the second section is where you actually generate something. It has three parts.

**Scene.** A plain textarea where you describe what you are doing or where you are, in your own words. Below it sit tappable scene-idea presets if you would rather start from a suggestion than a blank box, things like a kitchen counter, a morning walk, an unboxing moment at a desk, then edit from there.

**Product.** This is the part that separates AI Clone Butler from a generic AI photo generator. You pick a store, Amazon or Walmart, and enter the ASIN or Walmart item ID, then click "Look up." That fetches the actual, currently listed product photo for that item, the same photo a shopper would see on the listing. If the item is not on Amazon or Walmart, or you would rather supply your own image, you can switch to "Upload product" or paste a direct image URL instead.

**Advanced Options.** Shape (Square 1024x1024, Tall 1024x1280, or Wide 1280x1024) and Quality (High or Standard), which cover the practical range from a square feed post to a tall Story or Pinterest pin.

## What the generation call actually does

Here is the mechanic that makes the output trustworthy rather than just plausible: your reference photos and the real product photo go into the AI image model together, in one call. This is the same underlying likeness engine used elsewhere in the app, the one behind Deals Butler and the "clone wears products" option inside AI Influencer Butler. Because the product photo is a real input to that single call rather than something the model is asked to invent from a text description, the product itself is never hallucinated. It is the actual photographed item, composited with you, in a new scene you described. If you generate a photo holding a specific coffee maker, that is the coffee maker from the listing, not the model's best guess at what a coffee maker looks like.

This is new scene generation, not editing. It is not touching an existing product listing photo or inserting you into someone else's picture; it is building a new image from your reference photos, the real product photo, and your scene description, all at once. Once you have a result you like, you can optionally send it through an upscale step to roughly 4K, which is useful if you want something sized for a large print or a hero image rather than a quick social post.

## A realistic run-through

Say you want a photo of yourself with a specific water bottle you are promoting. You look up its ASIN, click "Look up," and the real listing photo loads into the Product block. In the Scene box you write something like "sitting on a park bench on a sunny morning, water bottle resting on the bench beside me, candid, not posed." You leave Shape on Square and Quality on High, and click generate. What comes back is a photo of you (built from your reference set) on a park bench, with that exact water bottle, not a generic stand-in bottle. If the crop or framing is not quite what you wanted, you adjust the scene description and run it again. If you want a bigger file for a print ad, you upscale the one you keep.

## Manual by design, and that is deliberate

AI Clone Butler does not post anything. It creates and saves images for you to download, full stop. That is a real difference from AI Influencer Butler, which has an autopilot mode that generates and posts on its own inside a spending cap. AI Clone Butler is the opposite sibling: everything it makes sits in front of you first. You decide which photo is good enough to use, where it goes, and when. For a feature that is compositing your actual face and body into new scenes, that is not a missing feature, it is the right default. You stay in full control of what leaves the app with your likeness on it.
