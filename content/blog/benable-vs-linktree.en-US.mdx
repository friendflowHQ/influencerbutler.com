If you have ever stared at the "link in bio" line on your profile and wondered whether you are using the right tool, you are not alone. The Benable vs Linktree question comes up constantly with creators, because both tools live in that same precious slot, and on the surface they look like they do the same job. They do not. One is a tidy menu of links. The other is built to make you money. Picking the wrong one quietly costs you commissions every single month.

Here is the short version before we get into the weeds: Linktree is a link hub, and Benable is a recommendation engine that happens to live in your bio. If your only goal is to point people to your YouTube, your shop, and your email signup, Linktree is fine. If your goal is to earn when people shop your recommendations, that is a different tool with a different design, and the comparison stops being close.

## What each one is actually for

Linktree was built to solve one annoying problem: social platforms only let you post one link, and you have more than one place to send people. So Linktree gives you a single page with a stack of buttons. Your podcast, your newsletter, your merch, your latest video. Tap a button, go to the destination. Clean, fast, done.

[Benable](https://benable.com/i/CJAQK) was built to solve a different problem: creators recommend products constantly and earn nothing from most of those recommendations. So instead of a stack of outbound buttons, Benable gives you curated lists of products you actually like, and it turns every item into an affiliate link automatically. People browse your lists, shop the things you recommend, and you earn a commission.

That difference in purpose is the whole story. Linktree routes traffic. Benable monetizes it.

## The money question

This is where the comparison gets lopsided, so let's be specific.

With Linktree, monetizing your bio is something you bolt on yourself. You can drop affiliate links into your button list, sure, but you are the one sourcing each program, applying to each brand, getting approved (or rejected), and pasting tracking URLs by hand. Linktree does not create those links for you and does not bring brands to the table. It is a container. What goes in it is your problem.

With Benable, the earning is the point of the tool:

- **It partners with 35,000+ brands.** Nike, Sephora, Wayfair, eBay, Ulta, and tens of thousands more.
- **You are approved for all of them instantly.** No per-brand application, no waiting room.
- **It auto-creates the affiliate link as you add each item.** You are not hand-pasting tracking URLs.
- **It takes no cut.** You keep the full commission, which typically lands between 2% and 15% depending on the brand.

So if you put the same creator on both platforms and gave them the same audience, the Benable setup is earning across thousands of brands from day one, and the Linktree setup is earning on whatever handful of affiliate programs that creator personally managed to wrangle. That is not a small gap.

> The mental model: Linktree is a signpost pointing people elsewhere. Benable is a storefront that keeps a little money each time someone shops your taste.

## Where Linktree still wins

This is not a hit piece, so let's be fair. Linktree is genuinely better at a few things, and pretending otherwise would not help you choose well.

- **Pure link routing.** If you mostly need to send people to non-shopping destinations (your Patreon, a booking page, a Discord), a button hub is simpler and cleaner than a list of products.
- **Total flexibility.** Linktree does not care what you link to. Benable is specifically about product recommendations, so if your bio is not about products, Benable has less to do.
- **Familiarity.** Your audience has tapped a thousand Linktrees. There is zero learning curve.

If your creator business is not built on recommending things people buy, Linktree probably is the right call, and you can stop reading here with a clear conscience.

## Where Benable pulls ahead for creators who sell

But most creators reading this *do* recommend products. Your audience asks "where did you get that" in the comments. You already drop Amazon links in your captions. You are, whether you have monetized it or not, a recommendation business. For that creator, Benable's design does several things Linktree simply was not built to do.

First, **discovery.** Your Benable lists are findable on Benable itself and through search engines, so they keep pulling in new shoppers long after you publish them. A Linktree page does not get discovered in search the way a well-titled recommendation list does. If you want to go deeper on the earning side specifically, [our guide on what Benable is](/blog/what-is-benable) lays out the full mechanics.

Second, **compounding.** Every list you build is a small evergreen asset that keeps earning. A Linktree button is only as valuable as the traffic you actively send to it today. A Benable list can quietly earn for months off search alone.

Third, **breadth without busywork.** The instant-approval, one-roof model means you can recommend across beauty, home, fashion, and travel without juggling a dozen affiliate logins. That breadth is exactly where multi-niche lifestyle creators leave the most money on the table with a plain link hub.

## So which should you pick?

Run it through one question: does your bio mostly *route* people, or mostly *sell* to them?

If you route (links to your other platforms, your booking page, your community), Linktree is the simpler, cleaner fit. If you sell (you recommend products people buy), Benable is built for that and Linktree is not, and the earning gap between them is the difference between a signpost and a storefront. Plenty of creators even run both: Linktree for the non-shopping links, Benable for everything they recommend.
