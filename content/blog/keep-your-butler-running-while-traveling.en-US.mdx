## Step 1: Set up remote access so you can check in from your phone

The idea is simple: leave a computer plugged in and awake at home, then remote into it from your phone whenever you want to peek at it.

1. On the home computer, go to [remotedesktop.google.com/access](https://remotedesktop.google.com/access), click the download arrow, install it, and set a 6-digit PIN.
2. Set the computer to never sleep. On Windows: Settings, then Power, then Screen and Sleep, set both to Never. On Mac: System Settings, then Lock Screen, set displays and sleep to Never.
3. On your phone, download the Chrome Remote Desktop app, sign in with the same Google account, tap your computer, and enter your PIN.
4. That is it. You are now looking at your full desktop, Butler and all, right from your phone, anywhere you have signal.

Now you can check in from a beach chair without wondering whether everything is still humming along at home.

## Step 2: Safeguard it with a UPS battery backup

This is the part I skipped and regretted.

A UPS (uninterruptible power supply) is a battery backup that sits between your wall outlet and your computer. It has its own cord that plugs into the wall, and then it has standard three-prong outlets on it that your computer and monitor plug into, just like plugging into the wall.

When the power flickers or briefly cuts out, which is what most outages actually are, the battery kicks in instantly and keeps your computer running so your Butler never misses a beat. Bonus: it also protects your equipment from the power surge that hits when electricity comes back on, and that spike is what fries computers.

## Step 3: The pro tip that actually saves your trip

A battery bridges short outages. For longer ones, you need one more setting.

Hop into your computer's BIOS and turn on "Restore on AC Power Loss," sometimes called "AC Power Recovery." Every brand words it a little differently. With that on, when power returns after a longer outage, your computer powers itself back on automatically. And because you already disabled sleep in Step 1, your Butler picks right back up without you touching a thing.

Battery plus auto-restart means you are covered whether the outage is 2 minutes or 2 hours.
