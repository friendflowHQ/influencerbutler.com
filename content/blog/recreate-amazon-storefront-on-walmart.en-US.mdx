The single-list flow in Collection Builder is great when you have one Amazon list you want on Walmart today. But if you have been an Amazon Influencer for a while, you probably have twenty lists, and converting them one paste at a time is an afternoon you keep postponing.

**Recreate my whole storefront** is the bulk mode. It sits in a card at the top of Collection Builder inside [Walmart Butler](/features/walmart-butler), and it does exactly what it says: finds every public idea list on your Amazon storefront and rebuilds each one as a Walmart collection, in a single run, while you do something else. The mechanics are in the [Collection Builder tutorial](/help/tutorials/walmart-collection-builder). Here is how to use the bulk mode well.

## How the run works

1. Paste your Amazon storefront link (it looks like `amazon.com/shop/yourname`) or just type your handle, and click **Find my lists**. Your public lists are found without signing in to Amazon.
2. Tick the lists you want. Lists you already turned into a collection start unticked, and the checklist is remembered if you close the app.
3. Set **Matching** and the other choices in the step below the card. They apply to every list in the run.
4. Click **Recreate selected lists**.

Collection Builder then reads, matches and builds each list one after another and shows a status next to each one: **Created**, **Already created**, **No Walmart matches**, or **Could not build**.

Two things make this safe to walk away from. A list where nothing matches on Walmart is skipped, not created empty, so you never end up with a hollow collection on your storefront. And if Walmart or Amazon asks you to sign in or show a check, the run stops and tells you why, so nothing is built halfway by mistake.

## The one difference from single-list mode

There is **no review step** in bulk mode. Lists are built automatically from your matching settings. That is the trade: speed for the per-row control you get when you paste a single list.

So decide your matching choice before you click:

- **Both** gives you full collections, mixing identical items with popular similar picks.
- **Same product only** gives you exact collections that may be shorter.
- **Similar picks only** always goes for the closest popular Walmart item.

If a particular list is important enough that you want to see the picks first, click **Review first** next to it. That opens the list in the single-list flow, where you get the full review table with the Rate column, the Swap for another button and the name and description editor. A good pattern is to bulk-build the straightforward lists and Review first the two or three that carry your reputation.

## Stop, resume, repeat

You can click **Stop** at any time. When you click **Recreate selected lists** again later, it carries on with the lists that are not done yet instead of starting over. Because the checklist is remembered, you can also tick a few lists today and a few more next week without losing track of what has been built.

Collection Builder remembers which lists you have already turned into collections and warns you before making a second one, so re-running the bulk mode does not spray duplicates across your storefront.

## Stock is handled on the way in and after

Every chosen product gets a fresh stock check right before its collection is built, and anything out of stock on Walmart is left out and listed on the result card. That alone avoids the most common ugliness on a new Walmart storefront: a collection launched with a sold-out item front and center.

Later, when Walmart flags items in an existing collection as out of stock (Walmart Performance shows this per collection), tick those collections in **Your Walmart collections** and click **Remove out-of-stock items**. Collection Builder uses Walmart's own cleanup option on each one.

## Before you press the button

- Sign in to Walmart Creator once from Walmart Butler. The session is saved.
- Connect OpenAI under API Integrations if you want the better matching and the rewritten names and descriptions. Without it, the run still works with a simpler ranking.
- Turn on **Keep my Amazon wording** if you want names and descriptions copied exactly rather than rewritten.
- Make sure the lists you care about are actually public on Amazon. The bulk finder can only see what Amazon shows to everyone.

## A realistic plan for a full storefront

If you have a lot of lists, here is an order that works:

1. Run **Find my lists** and look at the checklist before ticking anything. Untick seasonal lists that are out of season and anything you would not want on Walmart in its current form.
2. Set Matching to **Both** for the first run. It produces the most complete collections, and you can prune later.
3. Use **Review first** on your top-performing lists so you can check the Rate column and untick zero-commission rows. Grocery and household items pay nothing on Walmart, and a perfect match in those categories is still a pick that earns nothing.
4. Click Recreate selected lists and turn on **Watch it run** the first time so you can see the browser build a collection or two.
5. When it finishes, open Your Walmart collections, tick the new ones, and either post them to Facebook or add them to your Deals queue. That part has its own post.

## Why bother with Walmart at all

Walmart counts sales from a collection link under your storefront, so a collection is a sellable unit with no affiliate link to build. The audience is different from Amazon's, the competition from other creators is thinner, and the content is work you already did. The bulk mode is what makes it worth the time: twenty lists in one run instead of twenty afternoons.

If you then want the videos and photos on those products to appear on Walmart too, Video Repost and Photo Repost fill in the storefront content the same hands-free way. The [video repost post](/blog/reload-videos-amazon-repost-walmart) covers that side.
