If you run an Amazon storefront with any real catalog, you already know the morning ritual: open Creator Connections, scroll the campaign list, squint at each one, try to remember whether your audience actually buys that kind of thing, accept a few, second-guess yourself, and lose twenty minutes you meant to spend filming. Creator Connections automation exists because that ritual does not scale, and doing it by hand is where good campaigns quietly slip past you.

The fix is not "accept everything" and it is not "stop checking." It is a rules-based workflow. You decide your standards once, then apply them the same way every single day, so the decision stops being an agonizing judgment call and becomes a quick filter. Let's build that workflow.

If you are brand new to how these campaigns work, start with our [plain-English guide to Amazon Creator Connections](/blog/amazon-creator-connections-guide-2026) and come back here for the daily system.

## Why the manual approach quietly costs you money

Three things go wrong when you accept campaigns by hand, and all three are about logistics, not talent.

- **You check too rarely.** Campaigns expire. The best matches for your niche fill up or wind down, and a once-a-week scroll means you arrive after the good ones are gone.
- **You accept on vibes.** A 15% bonus looks thrilling until you remember your audience has never bought a single thing in that category from you. A high rate on a product nobody wants is still zero dollars.
- **You burn out on the decision.** With a real catalog and dozens of refreshing campaigns, eyeballing each one against "did my people actually buy this" becomes a part-time job. So you either rubber-stamp everything or quit and accept nothing. Both leak money.

A rules-based workflow kills all three, because it makes checking fast, makes acceptance objective, and removes the fatigue.

## Start with these five rules

You do not need a perfect system on day one. You need a starting filter you can tighten later. Here is a solid set of opening rules.

1. **Set a minimum commission rate.** Pick a floor, say 5% bonus, and skip anything below it. This alone removes half the agonizing.
2. **Require recent sales of a matching product.** Only accept campaigns for product types your audience actually bought in the last 30 days. Past sales are the best predictor of future ones.
3. **Skip anything expiring within 48 hours.** If a campaign is about to close, you may not have time to produce content that earns on it. Let it go and protect your focus for campaigns you can actually fulfill.
4. **Dry-run before you commit.** Preview which campaigns your rules would accept before anything is locked in. Eyeball the list once, make sure it looks sane, then commit.
5. **Review what paid, then tune.** After a couple of weeks, look at which accepted campaigns actually generated commissions. Raise your minimum rate or tighten your product match based on real results, not gut feeling.

> The goal of a rule set is not to be clever. It is to make the same smart decision every morning whether or not you feel like thinking. Consistency beats brilliance here.

## The daily workflow, step by step

Once your rules exist, the morning routine collapses to something fast and repeatable.

### Step 1: Check the catalog against live campaigns

Before accepting anything, confirm the products in each campaign actually line up with your storefront and your real sales. A campaign for a product you have never sold and your audience has never bought is a skip, even at a tempting rate. This catalog cross-check is the part that protects you from accepting impressive-looking duds.

### Step 2: Apply your rules

Run the five rules above against today's open campaigns. Minimum rate, recent matching sales, not expiring too soon. What survives the filter is your shortlist. What does not, you let go without a second thought, because you already made that decision when you set the rules.

### Step 3: Dry-run, then accept

Look at the shortlist your rules produced. Does it look reasonable? Good. Accept the survivors. You are done, and it took a few minutes instead of half your morning.

### Step 4: Review weekly, raise the bar

Once a week, look back at what actually earned. Tighten the rules that let through duds. Loosen anything that turned out to be too strict. Over a month, a rough "accept the obvious ones" filter becomes a sharp, profitable one.
