If you want to promote Prime Day deals on Instagram and actually move product, the event happening right now is your best shot of the year. Amazon Prime Day 2026 runs June 23 to June 26, which means as you read this the deals are live and your audience is in buying mode. Instagram is where a lot of that buying decision gets made, because it is the platform where people scroll, see something they want, and tap through in the same minute. The trick is knowing which surface to use for what, because a Story, a Reel, and a Close Friends share each pull a different lever.

Here is the thing most creators get wrong: they pick one format and hammer it. They post the same deal grid to their feed eight times and wonder why engagement tanks. Instagram during Prime Day is not one channel, it is three or four, and the creators who win use each one for what it is actually good at.

## Stories are your Prime Day workhorse

If you do nothing else this week, post deals to your Stories. Stories are where Prime Day lives, because they are fast, they expire, and that built-in urgency matches the deals perfectly.

A few rules that make Story deal-posting actually convert:

- **One deal per frame.** Do not cram five products into one slide. Each frame gets one product, one price, one link sticker. Clarity beats density every time.
- **Lead with the number.** "47% off" or "lowest price all year" in big text up top. People decide whether to tap in the first half second.
- **Use the link sticker, not "link in bio."** Friction kills Prime Day clicks. Every extra tap loses people. Put the link right there in the frame.
- **Post in bursts, not all at once.** Five frames in the morning, five at midday, five in the evening. Stories that all drop at 9am are gone by lunch, and you miss the afternoon shoppers entirely.

## Reels for reach and discovery

Stories reach the people who already follow you. Reels reach the people who do not, and during Prime Day there are a lot of people actively searching for deals. That is your discovery engine.

You do not need a polished production. The Reels that work for Prime Day are simple:

1. **The haul format.** "5 Prime Day deals I actually bought" with quick cuts of each product and the price on screen.
2. **The reaction format.** You showing genuine surprise at a price. Authentic beats scripted, especially on a deal nobody believes is real until they see it.
3. **The category roundup.** "Best Prime Day kitchen deals under $50." Specific, searchable, and exactly what someone types into the search bar mid-event.

Put your link in your bio, mention it out loud, and pin a comment with the storefront link. Reels are a slower burn than Stories, but a single good one can bring in new buyers for all four days of the event.

> A Story sells to your fans. A Reel finds you new ones. During Prime Day you want both running at the same time.

## Close Friends: your highest-converting room

Here is the underused move. Your Close Friends list, the green-ring group, is the most loyal slice of your audience, and they convert better than anyone. During Prime Day, that intimacy is worth real money.

The reason it works is psychological. A deal shared to Close Friends feels like a tip from a friend, not a broadcast. People act on it because it feels chosen, like you picked it specifically for the inner circle. Use that:

- Share your *best* deals there first, before they hit your main Stories. Give the inner circle a head start.
- Be more personal. "Okay this one is genuinely the best deal I have found today, grabbing it myself" lands completely differently in Close Friends than in a public post.
- Do not overdo it. The whole value is that it feels exclusive. Twenty Close Friends posts a day burns the magic fast.

## Match the message to the surface

A quick cheat sheet for the rest of the event:

- **Feed posts:** save for one or two roundups. The grid is not where impulse Prime Day buying happens, so do not over-invest here.
- **Stories:** your main channel. High volume, one deal per frame, link stickers, posted in bursts across the day.
- **Reels:** discovery. One or two genuinely good ones beat ten lazy ones.
- **Close Friends:** your best deals, shared first, framed personally, used sparingly.

When you map each deal to the right surface instead of blasting everything everywhere, your engagement holds up across all four days instead of cratering by Wednesday.

## Stay honest and you stay trusted

One closing note that matters more during a frenzy like this. Only post deals you would actually buy. The temptation during Prime Day is to share everything because the volume feels like the point. But your audience can smell a padded list, and the trust you spend chasing one extra commission costs you ten future ones. The creators who come out of Prime Day stronger are the ones whose followers learned, over four days, that when this person shares a deal, it is real.

Prime Day 2026 ends June 26. Between now and then, your Instagram can do an enormous amount of work for you if you use each surface for what it is built for. Run Stories for volume, Reels for reach, Close Friends for your best stuff, and keep it honest.
