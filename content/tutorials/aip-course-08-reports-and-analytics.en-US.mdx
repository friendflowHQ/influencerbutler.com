## Where the numbers live

Your Associates/Influencer dashboard has an **Earnings** section (commission income by day and by product) and **reports on your content** (how your videos and storefront perform). Amazon has moved these screens around over the years, but the numbers underneath stay the same.

Two timing facts that prevent 90% of beginner panic:

- **Reporting lags.** Today's sales can take a day or two to show up.
- **Payment lags more.** Commissions are paid monthly, roughly 60 days after the month ends. January earnings arrive around late March.

- [ ] I know my first payment arrives about two months after my first earning month {#payment-lag}

## The only three numbers a beginner needs

1. **Clicks / views**: are shoppers seeing and engaging with your content at all? Views on onsite videos mean placement is working.
2. **Ordered items**: engagement turning into orders. The ratio teaches you which videos convert.
3. **Earnings**: the running total. Watch the **trend across weeks**, not the daily amount.

Everything else (conversion breakdowns by category, bounties, per-tag detail) becomes useful later. At the start it is noise.

## What normal looks like

The typical beginner arc, so you can calibrate instead of quitting:

- **Weeks 1 to 2 after onsite approval**: single-digit views per day, maybe a first sale of a few cents. This is success, not failure; the flywheel is turning.
- **Months 1 to 3**: as your library passes 30 to 50 videos, days with several sales appear. First payments arrive and are small. Everyone's are.
- **Months 3 to 12**: the library compounds. Creators with hundreds of solid videos commonly report hundreds to thousands per month. Your slope depends on volume, niches, and luck with which videos take off.

One or two videos usually produce a surprising share of earnings. You cannot predict which, and that is the strongest argument for volume.

## The ADHD-safe check-in cadence

Checking stats releases exactly the same dopamine as making progress while producing none of it. Protect yourself with a rule:

- **Check once or twice a week, on a schedule.** Sunday evening, coffee, ten minutes.
- In those ten minutes: note weekly views, orders, earnings, and your top three videos. Ask one question: "what should I film more of?"
- Then close the tab and go film.

- [ ] Pick my weekly stats day and put it in my calendar {#stats-day}
- [ ] Write down this week's baseline numbers, even if they are all zero {#baseline-numbers}

## Do this now

Do your first ten-minute report check-in, record the baseline, and schedule the next one. Numbers only mean something in a series.
