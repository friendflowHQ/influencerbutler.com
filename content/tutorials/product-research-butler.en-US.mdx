> **It fills up by itself.** There is nothing to run. Install the browser extension, pair it with the app, and browse Amazon like you normally do. Every product page you open is recorded.

## What Product Research does

Picking a product to feature is a guess unless you know whether it actually sells. Product Research answers that with data that builds itself:

- **Price history**: every price the extension sees for a product is stored permanently, so you can see whether today's "deal" is really a low.
- **Sales-rank history**: the product's best-seller rank over time. A falling rank means it is selling more.
- **Estimated monthly sales and revenue**: when Amazon shows a real "bought in past month" figure, we use it. When it doesn't, a rank-based model estimates it (estimates show with a "~" so you know which is which). The model calibrates itself against the real figures you collect, so it gets sharper as you browse.
- **Seasonal signal**: once your history covers the same season in a prior year, the product's detail view shows whether it sells stronger, weaker, or the same at this time of year. This is the "will it sell for fall or Christmas?" question answered with data instead of luck.

## Before you start

- **Install the Influencer Butler browser extension** and pair it with the desktop app (Extension panel, 6-digit code).
- That's it. Data starts accumulating the first time you open an Amazon product page.

## Reading the table

Open **Product Research** under Amazon Butler. Each row is a product you have browsed:

- **Price / Rank / Bought/mo**: the latest observed values. Bought/mo is Amazon's own figure when shown on the page.
- **Est. sales/mo and Est. revenue/mo**: real figure when available, "~" model estimate otherwise.
- **Last seen**: when the extension last saw the product.

Use the search box to filter by ASIN, title, or brand.

## Reading a product's history

Click **View history** on any row:

- The **price chart** shows every recorded price. A green dot marks the lowest.
- The **sales-rank chart** is drawn so up means selling better. A rising line is a product gaining momentum.
- The **seasonal badge** appears once there is enough prior-year history to judge. "Strong seasonal seller" means its rank improves sharply around this time of year.

## Tips

- The more products you browse, the more useful the library gets. It costs nothing and runs itself.
- Browse the products you are considering a few times over the weeks before a season. Each visit adds a data point to the trend.
- The extension also shows the same history right on the Amazon product page when the app is running, so you can judge a product without leaving Amazon.
