## Why this matters

The Butlers post a lot for you, but some of your best conversions come from posts you make yourself: a quick Story, a post in a group, a link in your bio. Manual shares earn commissions just like automated ones, and they follow the same two Amazon rules:

1. **Amazon must know where you post.** Every website, app, or social profile where you place affiliate links has to be listed in your Amazon Associates site list.
2. **Every post needs a disclosure.** The FTC and Amazon's operating agreement both require you to tell people it is an affiliate link, clearly and before they tap it.

Skipping either one can get commissions reversed or, in the worst case, your Associates account closed. Both take minutes to get right.

## Step 1 (one time): add each platform to your Amazon site list

1. Open your site list in Associates Central: [Edit Your Website and Mobile App List](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/home/account/profile/sitelist). You can also reach it from Associates Central under Account Settings.
2. Add every place you post links, using your exact profile where possible: `instagram.com/yourhandle`, `facebook.com/yourpagename` (add your group URL too if you post links in a group you run), `tiktok.com/@yourhandle`, `pinterest.com/yourhandle`.
3. Click **Add** and save your changes.

If you start posting on a new platform later, add it to the list first, then post. Links on a site that is not in your list can cost you the commissions from those clicks.

## Step 2 (every post): disclose that it is an affiliate link

The FTC standard is "clear and conspicuous": someone should see the disclosure before or right next to the link, without tapping "more". Amazon adds its own required statement for Associates.

What works:

- Start captions with `#ad` or `#CommissionsEarned` (first, not buried at the end of twenty hashtags).
- Where you have room, include the full sentence: **"As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."**
- For bio links, put the disclosure in the bio itself, right next to the link.

What does not count: a disclosure only on your profile page while individual posts have none, vague tags like `#sp`, or a disclosure hidden behind "See more".

## Instagram Stories

1. Create your Story (photo, video, or a screenshot of the deal).
2. Tap the sticker icon and choose the **Link** sticker.
3. Paste your product link, storefront link, or Calling Card link. Use "Customize sticker text" to label it, for example "Shop it on Amazon".
4. Add a text element with the disclosure on the Story itself: `#ad` or "Commissions earned". The link sticker alone is not a disclosure.

Example Story: the product photo, a text layer that says `Restock deal I use daily #ad`, and the link sticker labeled `Shop it on Amazon`.

## Instagram bio and broadcast channels

- Bio: place the disclosure next to the link. Example bio line: `My Amazon storefront below (I earn commissions)`.
- Broadcast channel or DM shares: start the message with `#ad`, then the link. Example: `#ad Price just dropped on my favorite blender, grab it here:` followed by your link.

## Facebook (profile, Page, or group)

1. Paste the link into a normal post and let the preview load.
2. Make the first line the disclosure, then write your caption.

Example post:

```
#ad As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.
This is the vacuum I showed on my Story yesterday. Under $90 today:
(your link here)
```

Posting into someone else's group? Check that the group rules allow affiliate links, and keep the disclosure in the post itself either way.

## TikTok

- TikTok captions do not allow tappable links, so send viewers to your bio link and disclose in the caption of the video: `#ad link in bio`.
- Turn on TikTok's own promotional content toggle when the video features your affiliate products.

Example caption: `The kitchen gadget from my video, linked in bio #ad #CommissionsEarned`

## Pinterest

1. Create a Pin with your product image or video.
2. Paste your affiliate link as the Pin's **destination link**.
3. Put the disclosure at the start of the Pin description, and enable Pinterest's paid partnership label if your account has it.

Example description: `#ad My favorite fall porch light under $40. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.`
