> **Beta workspace.** Pinterest Butler sits in the dashboard behind a beta badge, but the workspace panel is a preview only: it explains what is coming and has no controls yet. Nothing posts to Pinterest today, no sign-in is requested, and there is nothing to configure. Do not expect production behavior yet.

## What the workspace shows today

Open **Pinterest Butler** and you get an **In Beta** notice, a short description of what it will be (an end-to-end Pinterest pipeline: ASINs from your Amazon Idea Lists, auto-rendered pins, SEO copy, and scheduled posts, all driven by your active Creator Connections campaigns), and a list of the modules that are shipping. There is no Run button, no settings, and no results table yet.

## What the engine will do once it is wired up

The posting engine behind the panel is built and tested; a follow-up release connects it to the workspace. When it goes live, a run works like this:

- **Browser sign-in with a remembered session**: the first run opens a visible browser window on Pinterest so you sign in yourself (including any two-step verification). The session is saved to a dedicated profile folder, so later runs reuse it silently until it expires, and the Creator Connections scanner and the pin poster share the same sign-in. **On Windows:** `%APPDATA%\InfluencerButler\browser-profiles\sites\pinterest`. **On Mac:** `~/Library/Application Support/InfluencerButler/browser-profiles/sites/pinterest`.
- **One board per sub-niche, plus at most one cross-pin board**: every pin is saved to its sub-niche's own board (created if it does not exist yet) and, when one of the niche's cross-pin boards matches the pin's intent, modifier, or seasonal hook, to one more board. Never more than one: saving the same pin to three or more boards in quick succession is a Pinterest spam signal.
- **Five pin templates**: Cream Grid Classic, Pink Starry, Amazon Finds Classic, Sports Mom Specific, and Country Spring, each a product-grid pin rendered with your font, colors, and background. More templates arrive as their design briefs are approved.
- **Creator Connections scanner**: filters CC campaigns by your niche keywords, records which ASINs are in an active campaign (bonus rate, remaining budget, end date), and ranks those products first when a pin is assembled.
- **Niche and season validity gate**: a pin idea is kept only when its niche is in season this month, or when its seasonal hook is evergreen. A gardening pin does not go out in January unless it is an evergreen one.
- **Pacing**: at least a 2 hour gap between pins, at most 15 pins per day, at most 3 per day during your first 14 days of posting, and no pins between 1am and 6am your local time. A pin that is not allowed yet is deferred to the next allowed slot rather than dropped.
- **Retry and watchdog**: failed pin runs can be retried in one click, and a watchdog re-checks pins that were scheduled natively on Pinterest and flags any that vanished before their date.
- **Local scorecard**: pins, saves, and outbound clicks for this month so far against the same window last month, computed from a database on your computer. Nothing is sent to our servers.

## What you can do today

- **Read the preview** in the workspace so you know what is coming.
- **Curate your Amazon Idea Lists**: the pin engine reads from them. The cleaner those lists are, the better the first runs will look.
- **Get your Pinterest account ready**: a business account with a few boards already in your niche gives the engine somewhere sensible to start.
- **Tip**: if Amazon's own Pinterest auto-publish is turned on for your storefront, set it to Draft rather than Publish before Pinterest Butler goes live, so the same product does not get pinned twice.

## Tracking the rollout

The beta badge stays on the nav button until the generator and poster are connected to the panel. Watch the app's release notes (Settings > About > Release notes), the What's New toast after an update, or the Help & Tutorials community feed for the announcement.
