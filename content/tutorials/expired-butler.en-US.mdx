## What Expired Butler does

Every deal you post has a shelf life. Once the sale ends or the promo code stops working, the post keeps running, sending shoppers to a price that no longer exists. Expired Butler finds those posts for you and cleans them up.

It reads the deals your Butler actually posted, works out which ones have ended, and then removes the expired posts across your social platforms. The one exception: a post whose deal had a creator campaign attached is edited instead of deleted, so the link you submitted to the campaign stays live while the post stops advertising an offer that is gone.

## How it decides a deal is expired

A post is flagged when either is true:

- The deal's end time has passed.
- A fresh check shows the product is no longer on sale or no longer available.

That combination catches both the deals that ran their full window and the ones that died early.

## Delete or edit

- **No campaign attached**: the post is deleted.
- **Campaign attached**: the post is edited, not deleted. You choose what the edit does, in Settings:
  - Add an EXPIRED banner to the caption.
  - Remove the promo code and the dates from the caption.
  - Both (the default).

**Where the banner goes** is your choice too: add it to the post itself, or to a new comment so your original caption stays untouched. You can change the banner wording in Settings as well.

**Per-platform overrides.** If you want different handling on different platforms, open **Per-platform settings** and turn on an override for Facebook Groups, Facebook Pages, Telegram, or Reddit. A platform left off uses the main Settings above.

## Before you start

- A Facebook login in Influencer Butler (the same profiles Deals Butler posts with).
- Some posted deals to work on. Expired Butler reads them straight from your Deals Butler history.

## Running a cleanup

1. Pick your Facebook profile and click **Scan for expired**. Expired Butler lists every expired post it found.
2. Review the list in card or table view. Filter by platform, by delete vs edit, or by whether a campaign is attached, and search by title or ASIN.
3. Tick the posts you want to handle (everything supported is selected by default), then click **Clean up selected**.

Nothing is removed until you approve it, and every run stays within your per-run cap.

## Keeping a record and reposting

The **History** section keeps a permanent log of everything Expired Butler deleted or edited, so you always know what happened. If a deal comes back on sale, use **Post again** on any row to drop it back into Deals Butler as a fresh draft.

## Running it automatically

Open **Schedule** and turn on **Clean up expired posts for me** to have Expired Butler scan and clean up on a repeating cadence. By default a scheduled run only reports what it would do; switch on **Auto-act** in Settings when you want it to clean up on its own.

- **How often**: pick a preset (6 hours, 12 hours, 1, 3, or 7 days) or set a custom interval, then click **Apply Schedule**.
- **Warm-up ramp** (optional): ease a new schedule in by capping how many posts a scheduled run acts on each day, raising that cap a little every day up to a limit you choose, instead of jumping straight to a full day's worth.
- **Sleep (quiet hours)**: scheduled cleanups pause overnight, on the same quiet hours shared with Deals Butler and every other Facebook-posting Butler on that account.

## Platform coverage

Expired Butler cleans up your deal posts on Facebook groups and pages, Telegram, Reddit, and Threads. Telegram and Reddit are handled directly through their APIs, so no browser window is needed for those. Reddit deals are usually link posts, which can only be deleted, not edited, so a Reddit post with a campaign attached is reported rather than removed. Instagram deals go out as a direct message, not a feed post, so there is no Instagram post to remove.
