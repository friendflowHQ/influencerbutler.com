## What YouTube Butler does

YouTube Butler takes the videos from your Amazon storefront (synced by Storefront Butler) and uploads them to your YouTube channel as either regular videos or Shorts. Each upload gets:

- A title, description, hashtags, and search tags, written by AI or from a template you control.
- Affiliate-tagged Amazon links in the description or pinned comment, with the parent product optionally linked too.
- A thumbnail captured from the video (or an AI-generated one).
- Automatic detection of YouTube Shorts format (vertical or square, 3 minutes or less) vs regular landscape, with options to avoid Shorts entirely.
- A QR code image option for product-overlay use.

The point is to turn your Amazon storefront catalog into a YouTube content feed without re-editing each clip. With the daily schedule on, even a 4,000-video storefront uploads itself, a batch a day, until the whole catalog is live.

## Guided Setup: the fastest way to start

New to YouTube Butler? Click **Guided Setup** at the top of the workspace (or answer the banner that appears when nothing is set up yet). It walks you through everything in order: bringing in your videos, signing in to YouTube, avoiding Shorts, AI titles, thumbnails, links, hashtags, country links, and your daily upload schedule, then queues and starts your first batch.

Pick **Fast Track** to answer about four questions and get the recommended setup, or **Step by step** to walk every option with an explanation. You can close it any time; it remembers where you were and offers to resume. You can also ask the AI Assistant to walk you through it.

## Before you start

- **A YouTube channel**: personal or brand channel both work.
- **Storefront Butler has synced your videos at least once**: YouTube Butler reads from that inventory.
- **An OAuth token** for your YouTube channel. The **Setup Wizard** walks you through it in 5 steps; it's a one-time setup.
- **Your Amazon affiliate tag** in Settings → Amazon, so links in descriptions are tagged correctly.

## First-time setup

The workspace opens to the **Setup Wizard** tab. Run it once:

1. Click **Start setup**.
2. Step 1: confirm your Amazon affiliate tag.
3. Step 2: paste your YouTube channel URL.
4. Step 3: sign in to YouTube (a Chrome window opens - sign in normally and grant the requested scopes).
5. Step 4: pick where affiliate links go - **description** (default) or **pinned comment**. Pinned comment dodges YouTube's link-suppression in descriptions for newer channels.
6. Step 5: verify a test upload (YouTube Butler uploads one short test video and immediately deletes it to confirm your token works).

You only do this once. After it succeeds, the wizard tab shows a green "Setup complete" banner.

## Uploading your first batch

1. Switch to the **Inventory** tab - every video from Storefront Butler shows here with a checkbox.
2. Tick the videos you want to upload. Start with 5-10 for the first batch.
3. Switch to **Settings** and confirm:
   - **Title template**: uses `{title}`, `{asin}`, `{price}` placeholders.
   - **Description template**: same placeholders plus `{deeplink}` for the affiliate link.
   - **Tags**: comma-separated; YouTube uses these for discoverability.
4. Click **Upload Queued**.

YouTube Butler runs through the queue with built-in rate-limit delays. The **Upload Log** tab streams each result. A batch of 10 typically takes 15-25 minutes depending on file size.

## Daily cap and automatic daily uploads

YouTube limits how many videos a channel can upload per day, and new channels get flagged for uploading too fast. Two settings in **Settings → Upload settings** handle this for you:

- **Max uploads per day**: Butler counts your uploads over the last 24 hours and stops each session at the cap. 25 a day suits most channels; new channels should start at 10. The **Ease in new channels** option automatically lowers the cap during a channel's first weeks.
- **Upload automatically every day**: turns on a daily schedule that runs a session once a day until every queued video is uploaded. Queue your whole catalog once, leave the app running, and Butler works through it day after day. The status pill shows campaign progress, like "212 of 4,000 total".

If YouTube itself says the daily limit is reached mid-run, Butler ends the day's session and keeps the remaining videos queued for tomorrow instead of failing them. Videos interrupted by a crash or force-quit are requeued automatically on the next run.

## The Shorts auto-detector, and how to avoid Shorts

Videos that are vertical or square AND 3 minutes or less get uploaded as Shorts automatically. Shorts get a different feed in YouTube's algorithm, so this matters - you don't manually flag anything.

Most storefront videos are vertical, so without a fix your whole catalog would land on the Shorts shelf. The **Avoid Shorts** setting (Settings → Video Processing) gives you three choices:

- **Convert vertical videos to landscape** (recommended): pads them to 1920x1080 so they upload as regular videos.
- **Extend videos past 3 minutes**: keeps the vertical look and adds an outro until the video clears the Shorts limit.
- **Off**: uploads as-is; vertical clips become Shorts with a #Shorts hashtag.

## AI titles, descriptions, hashtags, and tags

Turn on **AI titles and descriptions** (Settings) and AI writes a search-friendly title, a description with a hook, a call to action, your affiliate links, and hashtags, plus 10-15 search tags, for every video. It uses your OpenAI key from API Integrations and costs about a tenth of a cent per video (roughly $4 for 4,000 videos). Each video is only ever written once, so re-runs are free, and a monthly spend cap keeps the bill bounded. If anything fails, your templates are used instead, so uploads never stop.

## Post only Shorts, or only regular videos

In **Settings → Upload settings**, the **Post which videos** control lets you filter a run by format:

- **Shorts and regular videos** (default): upload everything you queued.
- **Only Shorts**: skip anything that isn't a Short this run.
- **Only regular videos**: skip the Shorts.

This is handy when you keep Shorts and long-form on separate channels, or want to drip one format at a time. Skipped videos stay queued and show up in the Upload Log with the reason, so nothing is lost.

## Post to a secondary channel

If your account has more than one channel, the Setup Wizard lets you pick a **secondary channel** alongside your primary. Then, in **Settings → Upload settings**, turn on **Post to my secondary channel** and the run uploads to that channel instead. Both channels must be on the same signed-in Google account. Leave the toggle off to keep uploading to your primary channel as usual.

## AI voiceover (translated audio)

The **AI voiceover** card replaces a video's audio with an AI voice speaking a translated version of what's said. Turn it on, pick the language to **dub into**, the **source language**, and a **voice**. On each upload the app transcribes the audio, translates it, and swaps in the AI narration before the video goes to YouTube.

This needs your OpenAI key in **API Integrations** and spends tokens per run. If the target language matches the source, nothing changes. When the key is missing, the app tells you and uploads the original audio instead of quietly skipping the voiceover.

## Product QR codes

The **Product QR** tab generates a QR code per ASIN that you can overlay on a video before uploading. Useful for shoppable content where viewers can scan instead of clicking through.

## Where the data goes

Per-video upload status lives in the Upload Log panel (filterable by status, search, marketplace, etc.). Uploaded video IDs are stored locally so YouTube Butler won't re-upload anything twice.

## Common gotchas

- **"OAuth token expired"**: re-run the Setup Wizard. Tokens last about a year.
- **Uploads stuck in "Processing" on YouTube**: that's YouTube's encoding queue, not Butler. New videos often sit in "Processing" 10-30 minutes; nothing you can do.
- **Pinned comment didn't post**: your YouTube account needs to be the channel owner, not a manager. Manager accounts can't pin comments.
- **A batch of 30 uploads abruptly stops**: you hit YouTube's daily upload cap. Wait 24 hours.

## Marketplace links

If your viewers come from more than one country, the **Marketplace links** setting (next to Link Placement) sends each one somewhere they can actually buy:

- **Off (one link)**: the usual single affiliate link.
- **Country links**: a short list, one tagged link per country the product sells in.
- **Single redirect link**: one link that forwards each viewer to their country's store automatically (uses your DeepLink provider).

Enter your per-country associate tags first in [API Integrations](/help/tutorials/api-integrations). A country with no tag is left out, so you never post a link that earns nothing.

Two extras for international audiences:

- **Add a native-language shop line for each country link**: country links get a local call to action, like "France (Achetez ici)".
- **Translate my title and description to the AI voiceover language**: when you dub the audio with AI voiceover, the title, description, and tags post in the same language, so international stores and viewers see a consistent upload. AI-written titles follow the voiceover language automatically.
