## What Video Butler does

Video Butler is the closest thing in InfluencerButler to a "free engagement boost." It takes the shoppable videos already on your Amazon Creator storefront and re-uploads them with refreshed metadata - new titles, new descriptions, new affiliate links, optional watermark/caption overlays. Amazon's algorithm treats a re-upload like a fresh post, so a 6-month-old video can get a second wind without you having to film anything new.

You can also use it to **prune low-performing videos** (the engine has a built-in "views below threshold = delete" pass) and to **re-load deleted videos** if you previously removed something by mistake.

## Before you start

- **Storefront Butler has synced your videos at least once**: Video Butler reads from that catalog.
- **Signed-in Amazon Creator Hub session** in Chrome.
- **FFmpeg staged**: happens automatically on app install. If you ever see a "ffmpeg not found" error, run `npm run stage:ffmpeg` from the repo root.
- Optional: a watermark image and/or caption font ready, if you want overlays.

## Running your first conversion + re-upload

1. Open **Video Butler** under Amazon Butler.
2. In the **Filters & Options** card:
   - **Min length / Max length**: leave wide open for the first run.
   - **Skip videos with views ≥**: defaults to 1000. This keeps your high-performers untouched. Videos *below* that view count are eligible for re-upload.
   - **Time window**: defaults to last 30 days. Walk through what's eligible before committing.
3. **Convert**: pick `none` for a pure re-upload, or `vertical` / `square` / `horizontal` to re-encode shape. `none` is faster.
4. **Run mode**: `post-keep` (recommended) re-uploads then keeps both versions for a day; `post-delete` replaces the original immediately. Start with `post-keep` so you can verify the new upload looks right before letting the old one go.
5. Click **Run**.

A Chrome window opens, downloads each eligible video from your storefront, runs the conversion (if any), and posts it back through Creator Hub with the new metadata.

## Refresh your whole library across marketplaces (one click)

When you want to refresh *everything* at once - re-post your US videos and fan them out to Canada, the UK, Australia, and Singapore - there is a one-click formula for it. Under **Preferred Formulas**, click **US re-upload + fan-out → US (delete first) + CA + UK + AU + SG (≥31s)**.

A single run then:

- **Re-posts your US videos that are at least 31 seconds long** (Amazon's monetization floor) to the US storefront *and* to CA, UK, AU, and SG.
- **Deletes the US original first, then re-posts it.** This matters: Amazon's duplicate detection is per-storefront, so a US re-upload is rejected as a duplicate unless the original is gone first. The other marketplaces are separate storefronts, so their originals are left alone.
- **Skips any video whose product is out of stock, unavailable, or untagged**, so you never re-post a dead link.
- **Skips anything you already (re)loaded in the last 45 days.** A reload resets the video's "added" date on Amazon, so on a monthly cadence each video comes back up for a refresh roughly every 45 days instead of on every run.

**Run Retag Butler first.** Retag Butler re-tags stale or unavailable products on your existing videos with available ones. Running it before this formula means the videos it fixes get re-posted with working product links, while the ones it could not fix are skipped. See the [Retag Butler tutorial](/help/tutorials/retag-butler).

### Your videos are safe even if a re-post fails

Because this formula deletes the US original before re-posting, Video Butler guards every step:

- The video is **downloaded to your computer first**, before anything is deleted.
- The original is **only deleted after** Video Butler confirms the product is available and taggable, never for a video it cannot re-post.
- If a re-post fails after the delete, the video lands in the **Deleted from Amazon** list with its downloaded file, so you can recover it with **Re-load selected**.

### Test on two videos first

This formula deletes videos from Amazon, so prove it on a small batch before running your whole library:

1. Turn **Process All** off and set **Max videos this run** to **2**.
2. Turn **Watch it run** on so you can follow each step.
3. Apply the formula and click **Run**. You will be asked to type **DELETE** to confirm.
4. Check that the US original is deleted *before* its re-post, that CA, UK, AU, and SG do not delete anything, and that the two videos look right on your storefront.

Once that looks good, turn **Process All** back on for the full library.

## Tuning over time

- **skipViewsAtLeast**: the most important knob. Raise to protect more high-performers from re-upload; lower to be more aggressive about refreshing older content.
- **deleteOriginal / deleteBeforeReupload**: when both off, originals stay. The "Cleanup" section lets you batch-delete originals later after you've verified the re-uploads. With "Delete before re-upload" on, the **Don't delete if added within (days)** field keeps any video added to Amazon within that many days from being deleted, so fresh listings stay put. 0 disables it.
- **joinMode + joinClip**: splice a short intro/outro clip onto every re-upload. Useful for brand consistency.
- **Caption / watermark overlays**: there's a separate panel for these; they survive re-encoding.

## Product tagging options

Two settings in the **Filters & Options** card control how each video's products get tagged.

**Parent ASIN handling.** A storefront video is tagged with one or more child ASINs (a specific size or color), and each child has a parent ASIN (the listing that groups every variation). Choose how parents are handled:

- **Tag parent only if the child can't be tagged** (recommended): tags the child ASINs and only falls back to the parent when no child could be tagged, so the post still has a working link. Parent ASINs usually can't be tagged directly, so this keeps the post clean instead of logging a failed parent on every video.
- **Tag only the child ASINs**: never tries the parent. The parent is still looked up for duplicate detection.
- **Tag both child and parent ASINs**: tags everything, the original behavior.

**Unavailable products.** Two independent checks keep out-of-stock products off your posts:

- **Check 1 (before upload)**: turn on **Skip uploads to unavailable products** and Video Butler asks the Amazon Creators API about each product's stock, then skips uploads to ones that are unavailable. Posting to a dead product earns 0 views, which drags down the Avg view duration and Avg % viewed that feed your Creator Stars score.
- **Check 2 (while tagging)**: a final safety net for products that still show "Currently unavailable" in Amazon's Add products window at tag time, which can disagree with the API check. Choose **Tag them anyway** to keep them, or **Remove them from the post** so viewers never land on a dead link.

**Shortcut.** You don't have to set these by hand. Under **Preferred Formulas**, click **In-stock products only (skip unavailable)** to turn on Check 1 and set Check 2 to remove unavailable products in one click. Your other filters stay exactly as they are.

## Find a match by title when the ASIN doesn't exist abroad

International uploads normally match a product to its listing by ASIN. But the same physical product often lives on a foreign marketplace under a different ASIN, so an ASIN-only match leaves a lot of uploadable videos on the table. Turn on **Search by product title when no ASIN matches the marketplace** in the Filters & Options card to recover them.

When none of a video's ASINs exist on a destination marketplace, Video Butler searches that marketplace by the product title (using the Amazon Creators API, and falling back to a search of the marketplace when needed) and scores each result against your product title, the same close-title match Retag Butler uses. Then it surfaces what it found in **Results history** as a **Title match to confirm** row, so nothing uploads on a guess.

You control how matches are handled:

- **When a match is found**: choose **Suggest the single best match** (one candidate with Confirm and Reject) or **Let me pick from a short list** (choose the right listing from the top few). Pick from a list is the safer choice on non-English marketplaces, where the foreign listing's title can score low against your English one even when it is the right product, so it helps to eyeball it.
- **Minimum match score to suggest**: the floor a candidate must clear before it is shown. Default 50.
- **Auto-upload above this score**: leave at 0 to always confirm matches yourself. Set it high (for example 90) to let very strong matches upload automatically without asking.

To act on a suggestion, open the Video Log, filter to **Title match to confirm**, and on each row click **Confirm** to upload the video to the chosen listing, or **Reject** to discard the suggestion. A confirmed match re-downloads the original and posts it to that marketplace straight away.

## Auto-correct titles Amazon flags as non-compliant

Amazon's title field quietly rejects some words. For example, "german" inside "Germany" trips its Community Guidelines filter, and the post will not save until you fix the title. Video Butler can handle this for you. In Filters & Options, set **If Amazon flags a banned word in the title**:

- **Remove the flagged word** (the default): the butler strips just the offending word, tidies up the spacing, retypes the title, and continues posting.
- **Rewrite the title with AI**: the butler asks AI to rewrite the title so it keeps your product meaning and your #ad tag, but drops whatever Amazon objected to. This uses your OpenAI key (the same one as AI cover images).
- **Skip the post**: leave the post untouched and move on, the original behavior.

If a correction still cannot get past Amazon's filter after a couple of tries, the post is skipped so nothing broken goes live.

## Accept Creator Connections offers in the same run

Many of the products you already feature in storefront videos have a live Creator Connections (CC) campaign or a Shoppable Video Creator Connections (SPCC) offer waiting for you. Finding them by hand means scrolling endless opportunity lists. Video Butler can do it for you while it works through your videos.

Turn on **Accept Creator Connections offers** in the Filters & Options card. As Video Butler reads each video, it collects the tagged ASINs and checks them against our lookup service, which knows which ASINs have a CC campaign or SPCC offer available anywhere. For the matches, it then uses your signed-in Amazon session to:

- **Accept the CC campaigns** for those ASINs in bulk.
- **Opt into the SPCC offers**, and (when "Submit storefront video as content" is left on) submit the matching storefront video as the content link, so the offer is fully completed rather than just accepted.

A couple of things to know:

- The lookup only tells you whether an offer exists at all. Whether you are eligible to accept it is decided locally by Amazon against your own account and region, so a global match does not guarantee every offer goes through. Anything that is not eligible is simply skipped.
- If the lookup service is unreachable, the accept pass is skipped quietly and your re-upload or refresh run is unaffected.
- This never uploads or deletes a video on its own. It only accepts offers and attaches the storefront video you already have.

## Refresh your data automatically (overnight)

Video Butler can keep your data current on a clock so you do not have to babysit it. In the **Refresh data automatically** card, turn on "Refresh data on a clock (no uploads)" and pick a cadence: every 12 hours, every 24 hours, or a specific time of day (for example, 1am). On that schedule the app re-scans your storefront for new videos and re-checks international product availability for your ASINs, then saves the results to the Video Log.

This is a data-only pass: it never uploads, deletes, or edits anything. The benefit is that your data is pulled and saved ahead of time, so a later upload can skip the slow data-gathering step and the Video Log stays current instead of showing missing or out-of-date availability.

One thing to know: like every schedule in the app, a refresh only runs while the app is open. If the app is closed at the scheduled time, the run is caught up the next time you launch it. For a true overnight refresh, leave the app running.

## See which sellers you have already covered

When you make videos for products from lots of different sellers, it is easy to lose track of who you have already covered and end up filming a second product for a seller you handled last month. Video Butler now keeps score for you.

Every video records its seller (on Creator Connections the campaign seller is the brand). In the **Video Log** you get a **Seller** column and a seller filter, so you can pull up every video for one seller at a glance. Below the log, the **Sellers you have made videos for** panel rolls it all up: each seller you have covered, how many videos and products you made for them, and the date of your most recent video, sorted by who you have covered the most.

The same data powers a **Video Coverage** column in the Storefront Butler results table. While you browse products to film, each row shows "Video" (this exact product already has a video), "Seller (N)" (you have N videos for this seller but not this product yet), or nothing at all. A matching filter lets you show only products whose seller you have already covered, or hide them, so you can spread your videos across new sellers instead of doubling up.

## See every country a video is loaded to (Country view)

The Video Log normally lists one row per upload, so a video you fanned out to five marketplaces shows up five times. When you want to confirm at a glance that each video reached every country, switch the Video Log to **Country view** with the **List view / Country view** toggle above the table.

Country view collapses those rows into one row per video, with a column for each Amazon marketplace: the verified markets (US, CA, UK, AU, SG) first, then the experimental ones. Each cell shows that country's status:

- a green check: the video is loaded there.
- a red cross: an upload was attempted and failed.
- an amber dot: an upload is in progress.
- an amber question mark: the video is not loaded there yet.

The cells are not just a report. Each cell for a verified market a video is not loaded to yet (or one that failed) is a checkbox: click it to tick that video and country. Tick as many as you like, across as many videos as you like, then press **Re-load Selected** in the toolbar to load them all in one pass. Each video row also has a checkbox that ticks all of that video's loadable markets at once, and the header checkbox (and the **Select visible** button) ticks every loadable cell on screen. The experimental markets are shown for reference only and can't be ticked.

When you load, Video Butler reuses the same re-upload pipeline: it re-downloads the original if needed, applies your current overlays, and posts the video to each selected marketplace. Your original storefront copy is never deleted, because a cross-marketplace load only adds a new listing.

## Retry failed uploads by reason

When an upload fails, Video Butler records why: an Amazon compliance rejection, a duplicate flag, a rejected thumbnail, no taggable products, a submit or upload error, or some other reason. In the **Video Log** these failures are grouped for you. A **Retry by failure reason** panel above the table shows one chip per cause with a count and a **Retry these (N)** button, so once you have fixed the root issue you can re-run every video that failed the same way in one click. There is also a **Failure reason** filter so you can narrow the table to a single cause first. Each retry re-posts only the videos you picked, using their original settings and destination, and leaves genuine rejections alone.

## The "Prune underperforming videos" pass

Distinct from re-upload: this scans your storefront, applies your filter rules (views floor, age window), and produces a **delete candidate list**. You review and approve before anything goes. Useful for pruning videos that performed under target so they stop pulling down the average.

## Delete + store videos with broken products

Sometimes a video can't stay live: its product went out of stock, or it lost its product tag entirely. The **Delete + store: unavailable / untagged** run mode removes only those videos and saves each one to the **Deleted from Amazon** tab, so you can re-upload it later once the product is back or after you re-tag it.

Pick it from **Run mode**, then choose your rules:

- **Delete videos with no tagged products**: videos that currently have zero products attached.
- **Delete videos whose products are all unavailable**: videos where every tagged product fails the stock check.
- **Protect sponsored (#ad) videos**: skip anything tagged #ad, so brand-deal content is never removed.
- **Protect videos with recent commissions**: skip videos whose products earned recently.

It is fail-safe: a video is deleted only when its products are confirmed unavailable (or it has none). Anything Video Butler can't confirm is left in place. Every deleted video is downloaded first, so **Re-load selected** on the Deleted from Amazon tab puts it back whenever you're ready.

## The "Recently deleted" recovery flow

The bottom panel lists videos you've deleted (within Amazon's recovery window) with a **Re-load selected** button. If a delete pass removed too much, you can recover from this list - no need to re-edit from scratch.

## Where the data goes

Per-run logs land in the video-butler row of the Video Log panel. Original video files are downloaded to a temp dir during conversion and cleaned up after each run.

## Common gotchas

- **"FFmpeg not found"**: run `npm run stage:ffmpeg` from the repo root, restart the app.
- **"Creator Hub session expired"**: re-sign in via the Chrome window Video Butler spawned, click Resume.
- **Re-uploaded video has wrong thumbnail**: Amazon picks a thumbnail from the first frame by default. To pin one, use the **Thumbnail font/Watermark** panel to overlay a static frame.
- **A re-upload gets fewer views than the original**: Amazon's algorithm sometimes deprioritizes content it recognizes as a re-post. Try a meaningful metadata change (new title, new product hook) rather than a cosmetic-only refresh.
