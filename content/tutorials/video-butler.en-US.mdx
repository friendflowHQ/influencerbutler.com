## What Video Butler does

Video Butler is the closest thing in InfluencerButler to a "free engagement boost." It takes the shoppable videos already on your Amazon Creator storefront and re-uploads them with refreshed metadata - new titles, new descriptions, new affiliate links, optional watermark/caption overlays. Amazon's algorithm treats a re-upload like a fresh post, so a 6-month-old video can get a second wind without you having to film anything new.

You can also use it to **prune low-performing videos** (the engine has a built-in "views below threshold = delete" pass) and to **re-load deleted videos** if you previously removed something by mistake.

## Before you start

- **Storefront Butler has synced your videos at least once**: Video Butler reads from that catalog.
- **Signed-in Amazon Creator Hub session** in Chrome.
- **FFmpeg staged**: happens automatically on app install. If you ever see a "ffmpeg not found" error, run `npm run stage:ffmpeg` from the repo root.
- Optional: a watermark image and/or caption font ready, if you want overlays.

## Running your first conversion + re-upload

1. Open **Video Butler** under Amazon Butler.
2. In the **Filters & Options** card:
   - **Min length / Max length**: leave wide open for the first run.
   - **Skip videos with views ≥**: defaults to 1000. This keeps your high-performers untouched. Videos *below* that view count are eligible for re-upload.
   - **Time window**: defaults to last 30 days. Walk through what's eligible before committing.
3. **Convert**: pick `none` for a pure re-upload, or `vertical` / `square` / `horizontal` to re-encode shape. `none` is faster.
4. **Run mode**: `post-keep` (recommended) re-uploads then keeps both versions for a day; `post-delete` replaces the original immediately. Start with `post-keep` so you can verify the new upload looks right before letting the old one go.
5. Click **Run**.

A Chrome window opens, downloads each eligible video from your storefront, runs the conversion (if any), and posts it back through Creator Hub with the new metadata.

## Tuning over time

- **skipViewsAtLeast**: the most important knob. Raise to protect more high-performers from re-upload; lower to be more aggressive about refreshing older content.
- **deleteOriginal / deleteBeforeReupload**: when both off, originals stay. The "Cleanup" section lets you batch-delete originals later after you've verified the re-uploads.
- **joinMode + joinClip**: splice a short intro/outro clip onto every re-upload. Useful for brand consistency.
- **Caption / watermark overlays**: there's a separate panel for these; they survive re-encoding.

## Accept Creator Connections offers in the same run

Many of the products you already feature in storefront videos have a live Creator Connections (CC) campaign or a Shoppable Video Creator Connections (SPCC) offer waiting for you. Finding them by hand means scrolling endless opportunity lists. Video Butler can do it for you while it works through your videos.

Turn on **Accept Creator Connections offers** in the Filters & Options card. As Video Butler reads each video, it collects the tagged ASINs and checks them against our lookup service, which knows which ASINs have a CC campaign or SPCC offer available anywhere. For the matches, it then uses your signed-in Amazon session to:

- **Accept the CC campaigns** for those ASINs in bulk.
- **Opt into the SPCC offers**, and (when "Submit storefront video as content" is left on) submit the matching storefront video as the content link, so the offer is fully completed rather than just accepted.

A couple of things to know:

- The lookup only tells you whether an offer exists at all. Whether you are eligible to accept it is decided locally by Amazon against your own account and region, so a global match does not guarantee every offer goes through. Anything that is not eligible is simply skipped.
- If the lookup service is unreachable, the accept pass is skipped quietly and your re-upload or refresh run is unaffected.
- This never uploads or deletes a video on its own. It only accepts offers and attaches the storefront video you already have.

## Refresh your data automatically (overnight)

Video Butler can keep your data current on a clock so you do not have to babysit it. In the **Refresh data automatically** card, turn on "Refresh data on a clock (no uploads)" and pick a cadence: every 12 hours, every 24 hours, or a specific time of day (for example, 1am). On that schedule the app re-scans your storefront for new videos and re-checks international product availability for your ASINs, then saves the results to the Video Log.

This is a data-only pass: it never uploads, deletes, or edits anything. The benefit is that your data is pulled and saved ahead of time, so a later upload can skip the slow data-gathering step and the Video Log stays current instead of showing missing or out-of-date availability.

One thing to know: like every schedule in the app, a refresh only runs while the app is open. If the app is closed at the scheduled time, the run is caught up the next time you launch it. For a true overnight refresh, leave the app running.

## See which sellers you have already covered

When you make videos for products from lots of different sellers, it is easy to lose track of who you have already covered and end up filming a second product for a seller you handled last month. Video Butler now keeps score for you.

Every video records its seller (on Creator Connections the campaign seller is the brand). In the **Video Log** you get a **Seller** column and a seller filter, so you can pull up every video for one seller at a glance. Below the log, the **Sellers you have made videos for** panel rolls it all up: each seller you have covered, how many videos and products you made for them, and the date of your most recent video, sorted by who you have covered the most.

The same data powers a **Video Coverage** column in the Storefront Butler results table. While you browse products to film, each row shows "Video" (this exact product already has a video), "Seller (N)" (you have N videos for this seller but not this product yet), or nothing at all. A matching filter lets you show only products whose seller you have already covered, or hide them, so you can spread your videos across new sellers instead of doubling up.

## The "Prune underperforming videos" pass

Distinct from re-upload: this scans your storefront, applies your filter rules (views floor, age window), and produces a **delete candidate list**. You review and approve before anything goes. Useful for pruning videos that performed under target so they stop pulling down the average.

## The "Recently deleted" recovery flow

The bottom panel lists videos you've deleted (within Amazon's recovery window) with a **Re-load selected** button. If a delete pass removed too much, you can recover from this list - no need to re-edit from scratch.

## Where the data goes

Per-run logs land in the video-butler row of the Video Log panel. Original video files are downloaded to a temp dir during conversion and cleaned up after each run.

## Common gotchas

- **"FFmpeg not found"**: run `npm run stage:ffmpeg` from the repo root, restart the app.
- **"Creator Hub session expired"**: re-sign in via the Chrome window Video Butler spawned, click Resume.
- **Re-uploaded video has wrong thumbnail**: Amazon picks a thumbnail from the first frame by default. To pin one, use the **Thumbnail font/Watermark** panel to overlay a static frame.
- **A re-upload gets fewer views than the original**: Amazon's algorithm sometimes deprioritizes content it recognizes as a re-post. Try a meaningful metadata change (new title, new product hook) rather than a cosmetic-only refresh.
