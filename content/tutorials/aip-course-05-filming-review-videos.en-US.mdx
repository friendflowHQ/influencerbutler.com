## The mindset shift

You are not becoming a YouTuber. Nobody subscribes to onsite videos, nobody sees your channel, and nobody cares about your intro. A shopper on a product page wants one thing: **someone real showing them the product before they spend money.** Your job is to be that person for 60 to 120 seconds.

This is why "I hate being on camera" barely matters here. Many top-earning influencer videos never show a face: just hands, the product, and a voice.

## The minimum viable setup

- **Camera**: your phone, horizontal, at 1080p or higher.
- **Light**: face a window, or film near one. Daylight beats any cheap ring light.
- **Sound**: quiet room, phone within arm's reach or so of your voice. Sound quality fails more reviews than picture quality.
- **Stability**: prop the phone against a stack of books or use a $15 tripod. No handheld walking tours.

That is the whole list. Upgrading gear later is a reward, not a prerequisite.

## The 3-part script that never fails

Write two or three bullet points per part, not a word-for-word script (you will sound like a hostage reading a statement):

1. **Hook (5 to 10 seconds)**: what it is and the one question you will answer. "This is the mini blender everyone buys for smoothies. Is it strong enough for frozen fruit?"
2. **Demo (60 to 90 seconds)**: the product doing its job. Blend the frozen fruit. Show the mess, the noise level, the cleanup. Answer the questions YOU had before buying it: read the product's own reviews and questions section for ideas.
3. **Verdict (10 to 20 seconds)**: the honest wrap-up. "Great for single servings, struggles with ice, buy it if X, skip it if Y." Honesty converts; infomercial voice does not.

Done is better than perfect: your fifth video will embarrass your first, and that is normal and fine. Ship the first one anyway.

## Batch filming: the consistency cheat code

The creators who succeed do not film one video when inspiration strikes. They batch:

- Collect 10 to 20 products around the house
- Film all of them in one afternoon (bullet scripts make this fast)
- Edit and upload across the following week

One batching session a week comfortably produces 10+ videos. Keep a running list on your phone of every product you own and could review: kitchen gadgets, tools, toys, pet gear, tech, storage bins. Boring household items often earn best because shoppers research them constantly and few creators bother covering them.

## What NOT to do

- Do not mention price, discounts, or "link in bio"
- Do not use copyrighted background music
- Do not film vertical and crop later; start horizontal
- Do not spend an hour editing a 90-second video: trim the ends, cut dead air, done
- Do not review products you have never used; shoppers can tell, and so can Amazon

## Do this now

If you only do one thing from this module: film one video today. The list and the batching can start tomorrow.
