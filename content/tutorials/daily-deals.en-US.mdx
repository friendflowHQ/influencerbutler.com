## What Deals Influencer Butler does

Deals Influencer Butler is the most complete automation in InfluencerButler. It runs a full pipeline in one workspace:

1. **Harvest**: pulls active Amazon deals matching your filters (discount %, commission floor, price range, keywords, brand).
2. **Build the post**: wraps each deal in your template with hashtags, affiliate links, and optional AI-generated images.
3. **Schedule**: posts to your connected social destinations (Instagram, Instagram Threads, Facebook groups + pages, Telegram, Reddit, Benable) on whatever per-platform cadence you set.

You set it up once and it ticks along - most users only revisit it to refine filters or add a new social destination.

If you would rather answer a few questions than visit each tab, use **Guided Setup** (the chip at the end of the tab bar, or the banner when nothing is configured yet). It picks your keywords, applies a post template, walks you through each destination you choose, and arms the schedule. See the [Guided Setup tutorial](/help/tutorials/daily-deals-guided-setup) for details. Everything below still applies when you want to understand or fine-tune what it set up.

## Before you start

- **Amazon affiliate tag** configured (Settings → Amazon).
- **CC Storefront ID** set in the topbar (so commission data is accurate).
- **At least one social destination** connected in the Social Destinations tab. Telegram is the simplest first destination - the bot setup is a one-time paste.
- **Data Refresh Butler** active (or coupled by default) - Deals Influencer Butler reads from the catalogs Data Refresh Butler maintains.

## Running your first harvest

The workspace opens to a chip-bar with 10 tabs. The setup flow follows them left-to-right:

1. **Deal Filters**: start with the defaults: no minimum discount, all categories, all commission rates. Click **Search Deals**. Confirm you see results, then tighten filters (a good next step is raising **Minimum percent off** to 30% or more). A **Result priority** chip row controls how results are ordered: drag the chips to rank by SPCC first, then Creator Connections, then Best Deal (highest discount). Drag a chip past the **Not prioritized** divider to stop using it; Best Seller sits there by default. The **Max deals per keyword** limit keeps your best-earning deals first, so a smaller cap still surfaces your top SPCC and Creator Connections products, not just whatever showed up first. The search still checks every keyword in your list either way, and with both Amazon and Walmart turned on, the best deals across both stores win on merit. Your seasonal keyword buckets live on the **Keywords** tab. Open a month's **Per-day keywords** drop-down to target specific calendar days, and each harvest automatically uses today's day bucket, falling back to the month's list when that day is empty.
2. **Build Your Post**: the post template uses placeholders like `{title}`, `{price}`, `{discount}`, `{deeplink}`. New here? The **Quick-start formulas** row at the top fills a complete caption and pinned comment in one click (for example percent-off with the link in the first comment, a promo-code feature, or a star-rating layout). Pick one, then edit anything you like. Posting to Telegram? Telegram channels have no first comment, so use the **Telegram** formula: it puts the affiliate link right in the caption (with the percent off, no dollar prices) instead of a pinned comment. Need more than one comment? Click **Add comment** to stack a second, a third, and as many as you want, each with its own pills. Butler posts them in order under the main post on Facebook Groups and Pages, and adds them wherever another channel allows. Want each comment to feature a different deal? Build a reusable **Comment template** with the same pills, then click **Fill comments from deals** and Butler stamps that template onto each product from your Search Deals, one unique product per comment (no repeats). You can also save your own current post as a formula, then rename or delete it later. Edit the template, hit **Preview** to see how a real deal renders. A draggable **Pricecrash hype** pill is also in the tray: it drops in a high-urgency line with the real (cents-masked) sale price and, when used, replaces any CTA and Hook pills. Click it to edit those lines: add your own, disable ones you don't want, and pick cycle or single. The **{image}** chip at the bottom of the caption sets the photo: keep the Amazon product photo, upload your own, generate one with AI, or post text only. With the Amazon photo chosen you can also turn on **Cycle through the product's photos** so your posts rotate through a product's different images (in order or at random) instead of always using the main one; products with only one photo simply post that one. In **Settings**, there's a related option for tall product photos: turn on **Add a second photo when the product image is tall** and, whenever the main Amazon photo is taller than Facebook likes, Butler attaches a second product photo so Facebook shows them side by side instead of cropping one tall image (Facebook only, and only when the product has more than one photo). There's also **Post the product photo and an AI image together**: turn it on and your Facebook posts show both the Amazon product photo and an AI-generated image side by side, and you pick which one comes first. It needs AI images enabled (and a key set), and it applies to Facebook only; other channels keep using a single image. Also in **Settings**: **Automatically add the affiliate link to the first comment** is off by default. If your link already goes in the post, leave this off so Butler never adds the link as a comment (posting the same link in a comment on every post can get your account flagged or comment-blocked). Turn it on only if you want the link added to the first comment and you keep your posts per day sensible. Deal-field pills include **Deal Type** (for example Best Deal or Lightning Deal), **Ends** (the deal's end date and time), and **Ends in** (a short countdown such as "Ends in ~3 days"); each drops out silently when that deal has no value. There is also a **Store** pill (the `{{store}}` token) that prints the store a deal came from, so you can add a source label like `Ad - {{store}}` to your caption and each post shows where it is from.
3. **Social Destinations**: connect at least one. Each platform has its own sub-flow (Telegram bot token, Facebook page picker, Instagram session) - the workspace explains each one inline. Turning a destination on or off here is the single switch that controls where posts go. The Scheduler's "Where to post" list just reflects these toggles, so a destination you leave off is never opened or posted to. Instagram Threads needs no setup of its own: it reuses your Instagram sign-in and posts to your own Threads feed, so you just toggle it on.
4. **Scheduler**: turn on the **Post scheduler** and pick a cadence. The defaults are conservative: groups every 1h, pages every 2h, Telegram every 15 min, Reddit/Instagram/Threads off.
5. Click **Run now** under Harvest to do one search-and-post round so you can see end-to-end behavior before turning on the schedule.

## Starter keywords by niche

The first time you open the **Keywords** tab with every month empty, Butler asks what niches you post deals for: Auto, Travel, Motherhood & Parenting, Home Decor, Beauty, Fitness, Kitchen, Pets, Fashion, Tech & Gadgets, Health & Wellness, Food & Drink, Baby & Kids, Outdoors & Camping, Office & Organization, and Crafts & Hobbies. Check any that fit and click **Add keywords**: every month gets about 20 ready-made seasonal keywords per niche. Back-to-school gear lands in July and August for Motherhood, holiday decor in November and December, New Year fitness in January, and so on. The suggestions favor daily-use products and multi-packs that sell well when discounted, and they match your app language (the English, Spanish, and French sets are each curated for their own Amazon marketplace). Prefer a blank slate? Click **Skip, I'll enter my own**. You can reopen the picker any time with **Suggest keywords by niche** at the top of the Keywords tab, and re-running it never duplicates keywords you already have.

## Harvest deals from Amazon promotion pages

Amazon "spend and save" promotion pages (their web address looks like `amazon.com/promotion/psp/...`) list dozens of qualifying products in one place. On the **Harvest** tab, the **Pull deals from Amazon promotion pages** card lets Butler mine those pages for you. Turn on **Include Amazon promotion deals** and pick where the promotions come from:

- **Find them automatically from my keywords**: Butler checks the products your normal search already finds and, when one belongs to a promotion, follows that promotion and scans the rest of its products too. You paste nothing.
- **Use the promotion links I paste below**: paste one promotion link per line (handy for a promotion you check every day). Butler scans each one.

You can use either or both. For every product Butler finds on a promotion page, it checks that product's own price and keeps it only when it passes your regular deal filters (price range, minimum percent off, Real discounts only). The promotion itself is never posted, only the individual discounted products on it. Because this reads real product pages, it adds a little time to a search, and it needs you to be signed in to Amazon in the app's browser.

In **Build Your Post**, two new pills come with this: **Promo Name** and **Promo Link**. Drop them into a caption or comment and posts made from a promotion deal will show which promotion the product came from; on ordinary deals they simply disappear.

## Filter presets

The **active preset** (default named `default`) saves your filter config. You can have multiple presets for different niches - say one for "tech under $50" and one for "home decor under $100" - and swap between them from the Overview tab. Clones (a paid add-on) give each clone its own preset store so you can run multiple niches in parallel with no preset switching.

## Real discounts only, and topping up to your target

Two Filter Recipe settings decide how strict your harvest is, and they pull in opposite directions:

- **Real discounts only** (on by default) keeps only products with a genuine markdown: a real percent off, a flat savings amount, or an active Amazon deal flag. Products listed at full price are dropped so your posts feature real bargains. Turn it off and full-price products can enter your queue with a blank "% Off" line, which is why Butler asks you to confirm first. Most people keep it on.
- **Top up to reach your target** (off by default) only matters when you set **Max deals harvested**. If your filters find fewer deals than that target, Butler adds the best near-miss deals until you reach it. Topped-up deals already passed your price range, excluded keywords, and repost guard, but they can fall below your Minimum percent off, review, and star minimums, and can even lack a real discount. Turn it on when hitting your daily post count matters more than strict filtering; leave it off if you never want a deal that misses your filters.

In short: Real discounts only trims your harvest down to true markdowns, and Top up to reach your target fills the harvest back up when that trim leaves you short of your target.

## If a deal changes before it posts

Deals are found ahead of time and posted on your schedule, so a price or discount can change between when Butler finds a deal and when it actually goes out. Right before each deal posts, Butler re-checks its current discount, price, and stock. If it no longer meets your filters (dropped below your Minimum percent off, fell below your Min price, or went out of stock), Butler follows the choice you set in **If a deal changes before it posts** on the Deal Filters tab:

- **Hold it for review** (default): the deal moves to your Upcoming posts with a "deal changed" note, so nothing goes out that you did not mean to send. Click **Post anyway** to send it as is.
- **Skip it quietly**: the deal is dropped and nothing is posted.
- **Post it with current numbers**: Butler updates the caption to the current discount and price, then posts it.

This runs on every post, so you never post a 10% off deal that was 50% off when it was found. Products with an ongoing Always-Post discount are never held here.

Held deals normally wait in Upcoming posts until you review them. If you would rather not review them, turn on **Clear held deals automatically** on the Deal Filters tab and choose how many days a held deal can wait. Any held deal that stays unreviewed longer than that is deleted for you, so held deals never pile up.

## Include deals with a clippable coupon

Some Amazon products have a clippable coupon (for example "Apply 17% coupon", or a set dollar amount off) on top of any sale price. Amazon's product feed does not report coupons, so Butler reads them from Amazon's coupons page directly.

- On the **Deal Filters** tab, open **Amazon coupons** and turn on **Include Amazon coupon deals**. Your next search also scans Amazon's coupons page in the app's Amazon browser, prices each coupon product, and adds the ones that pass your filters. The first time, make sure you are signed in to Amazon in the app browser.
- Every coupon deal is added with its coupon amount, so you can feature it in a post. In **Build Your Post**, drag in the **Coupon** pill (a short badge such as "17% coupon"), the **Coupon Price** pill (the price after the coupon, such as $19.87), or the **Coupon % Off** pill.
- Want coupon products only? Turn on **Only coupon deals** in the filters and Butler keeps just the products that have a clippable coupon.
- Don't want coupon deals at all? Turn on **Exclude coupon deals** and Butler drops every product that needs a coupon clipped, so only straight price-drop deals stay. This also removes coupon deals that arrive through your regular keyword searches, which the harvest-source toggles above cannot. **Only coupon deals** and **Exclude coupon deals** are opposites, so turning one on clears the other.

## Posting from more than one Facebook account

If your groups and pages live under different Facebook logins, the **Facebook profiles** card at the top of the Social Destinations tab lets each one post from the right account.

1. Click **Add profile** and give it a name (for example "Main" and "Deals account").
2. Click **Sign in** on that profile. A normal Chrome window opens on its own isolated session. Finish signing in to that Facebook account, then close the window. The pill turns to **Signed in**.
3. Under your Facebook groups and pages lists, each destination shows a dropdown. **Pick which account posts it** from the dropdown (only signed-in profiles are listed). Choose **Don't post this** to skip a destination. This works on a copied workspace too, so each copy can post from its own account.

For a Facebook **Page**, Butler now posts **as the Page** for you. As long as the assigned login manages that Page, Butler switches to acting as the Page, publishes, adds your first comment, then switches back to your personal profile. You no longer have to sign a profile in **as the Page**. Use the Facebook profiles above when your Pages live under **different** accounts, so each posts from the right login.

A destination with **no profile assigned is skipped** at posting time, and the reason is saved to that deal's archive (you also get a toast). Destinations you already had keep posting from your main login, so nothing you set up before changes; only newly added groups and pages start unassigned and wait for you to drop a profile onto them.

When you click **Sign in**, the pill shows **Checking** for a moment. On your **Default** profile, if you are already signed in, no window opens. Named profiles always open a sign-in window. To point an already-signed-in profile at a different Facebook account, click **Switch account** on that row to open a fresh sign-in window.

At posting time, Butler groups your destinations by profile and posts each group from its own signed-in browser. If one account is signed out, only its destinations wait for you to sign back in. The rest still go out.

## Give each group or page its own tag or GeniusLink group

The same rows where you assign a Facebook account also let you route earnings per destination, so a niche group can track on its own.

- **Its own affiliate tag.** Once the app has read your Amazon tracking IDs (from a Storefront scan), each group and page row shows a tag picker. Leave it on **Default** to use your main account tag, or pick a specific tag so that destination's links earn on it. That lets you see which group drives sales in your Amazon Associates reports.
- **Its own GeniusLink group.** When GeniusLink is your chosen link provider in **API Integrations**, each row also shows a **GeniusLink Group ID** box. Leave it blank to use your main group, or type a group's ID so that destination's links are created under that group. Each group can then report separately in GeniusLink. The box only appears while GeniusLink is your selected provider.

Both are optional. Leave them alone and every destination keeps using your main tag and group.

## Staying within Facebook's posting limits

Facebook limits how often an account can post, and posting too many deals too fast (especially across several Deals workspaces on one Facebook account) can trigger a temporary block. Butler has built-in safeguards.

- **A daily limit and spacing.** On the Dashboard, under **Facebook posting limits**, set the **max Facebook posts per day** (counted across all your Deals workspaces) and the **minutes between posts**. A safe starting point is 25 per day, 20 minutes apart. When you reach the limit, extra posts wait for the next day instead of piling on.
- **A warm-up ramp.** Leave **Warm up gradually** on, especially on a newer account: Butler starts slower and builds up to your limit over a few weeks. The Dashboard shows the current week and how many posts are allowed today.
- **If Facebook blocks posting anyway.** Butler notices the block, stops scheduled Facebook posting on its own so the block does not last longer, and shows a banner plus emails you. If your Facebook Page is on a separate standing and still posting, only your groups and profile are paused. Do not post to Facebook by hand while a block is active, as that can extend it. Facebook does not show the end date in the block itself, but the banner's **Open Account Status** button takes you to the page that does. Posting resumes gradually once you click **Resume Facebook posting** or the limit lifts on its own.
- **Comment blocks pause everything too.** Facebook can block commenting on its own (the "You Can't Use This Feature Right Now" message), which matters because the affiliate link lives in the first comment. Butler detects it while commenting, pauses Facebook commenting AND posting together, shows the banner, and emails you. By default the rest lasts 3 days; change it with **Pause after a Facebook block (days)** in the Scheduler's quiet-hours card, and repeat blocks rest longer on their own. You can resume early any time with the banner's **Resume commenting and posting** button. One caution: comment blocks often do not appear on the Account Status page, so a clean status page does not mean the block has lifted.

## Tuning over time

- **percentOffMin** in the preset - start at 30%, raise to 40-50% once you have a sense of which discount threshold gets engagement.
- **trackingDays**: how many days a deal sticks around in the "tracked" history before being eligible to re-post. Default 14.
- **excludeLastMonth**: once on, won't re-post any deal that posted in the last 30 days. Keeps your feed feeling fresh.
- **perDestination cadences**: Telegram tolerates much higher volume than Instagram. Don't apply Telegram's 15-min cadence to Instagram or you'll get rate-limited.

## My Caption Voice: captions that sound like you

Automated captions can start to sound the same. **My Caption Voice** (in **Build Your Post**, just under the caption variants) learns how you write and adds caption variants in your own style.

1. Open **Build Your Post** and expand **My Caption Voice**.
2. Paste 2 to 5 posts you wrote yourself, the way you actually post them: your emoji, your hooks, your line breaks.
3. Pick how many variants to generate and click **Generate caption variants**.

The AI studies your examples (your hook style, emoji habits, layout, and sign-offs) and writes new caption variants that keep your voice while using pills for the moving parts: the product link, price, discount, and promo code always come straight from each deal, so they are always exact. The new variants land in **More caption variants**, where every post rotates through them. Edit or remove any of them like a variant you wrote yourself; generating again replaces only the ones you have not touched.

This feature uses your OpenAI key from **API Integrations**, the same key as the AI keyword generator. No key connected? The section shows a **Connect OpenAI** button that takes you there.

## Caption by deal type

Different deals deserve different wording: a straight price drop, a coupon code to type at checkout, a clippable coupon, or a mix of them. **Caption by deal type** (in **Build Your Post**, under the caption variants) gives you a separate caption box for each style, so you can write them once and edit any one without touching the others.

There are seven boxes: **Price drop**, **Code**, **Coupon**, **Price drop + code**, **Price drop + coupon**, **Coupon + code**, and **Price drop + coupon + code**. You do not have to fill them all. Write captions only for the styles you care about and leave the rest empty.

How Butler picks a box: for each product it checks whether the product has a price drop, a coupon code, and a clippable coupon, then uses the box that matches that exact combination. If that box is empty, Butler falls back to the closest box you did fill, and if none apply it uses your main **Post caption**. So the boxes never fight your main caption: they only take over when a deal matches one you wrote. Every box takes the same pills as your main caption (title, link, price, percent off, promo code, and the rest), and the live **Preview** shows which box a deal will use.

If a deal ends up with no caption at all (no matching box, an empty main caption, and no variants), Butler posts a basic caption (percent off and the product title) and the preview shows a note telling you so, with a shortcut to connect OpenAI or add a caption.
## Auto-build Amazon Idea Lists

On the Harvest tab there is an **Auto-build Amazon Idea Lists** card. Turn it on and, after each search, Butler creates Amazon Idea Lists on your storefront from the deals you just harvested. Lists are ranked so the deals active in **Creator Connections** and **Sponsored Products for Creators** go first, putting your highest-earning products at the top.

Set it up in three steps:

1. **Pick how products are grouped**: one list per keyword, a single top-deals list, or let AI cluster them into themed lists.
2. **Pick your storefronts**: your Creator Connections storefronts appear automatically from the storefront ID check, each with an on/off toggle. Turn on the ones to build on. Re-run the storefront ID check in Settings to refresh the list.
3. **AI copy (optional)**: leave it on and Butler writes the list description and a short comment for each product in a friendly, honest voice. Turn it off for a plain templated description and no comments.

Use **Build now** to run it once on demand, or flip the toggle so it runs after every harvest. The status line shows progress and the last run summary.

## Idea List Rotation: post your existing idea lists on a schedule

Already have idea lists on your Amazon storefront? The **Idea List Rotation** tab posts them for you on a schedule, one list at a time. It never posts the same list twice in the same day, so your feed stays varied.

Set it up:

1. **Refresh my idea lists**: click it once and Butler scans your storefront and pulls in every idea list you have, each with an on/off checkbox. Uncheck any you do not want in the rotation. This finds all your lists, whether you built them by hand or with Auto-build.
2. **Pick your times and days**: add the times of day and the days of the week you want a list to go out.
3. **Choose the order**: **In order** goes top to bottom through your lists; **Shuffle** picks at random. Either way, no list repeats within a day. If you schedule more times in a day than you have lists, the extra times are skipped and the rotation starts fresh the next day.
4. **Write the caption**: use your own caption with a **List name** and **List link** you drop in, or let AI write one for each list when you have an OpenAI key set. Add a photo per list by uploading one, or let AI create one.

Your lists post to every destination you have connected under **Social Destinations**. Use **Post the next list now** to send one immediately and see it work. Turn the rotation on and Butler handles the rest.

## Promo Code Hub

The **Promo Code Hub** tab does two things: it surfaces the Amazon Associates **Deals Hub** so you can post curated deals, and it keeps your promo codes organized.

**Deals Hub browser (read-only).** This is the same curated Deals Hub that Prime Day Butler harvests, including **upcoming** deals (with their future start date and future price) and a green **Lowest all year** badge when a deal beats the year's lowest price. Each row shows a product thumbnail when one is available, plus its product **category**. Filter to upcoming only or lowest-all-year only, narrow to one or more product **categories**, then pick where the deal should go from the dropdown next to **Add to post**: keep it as a draft, queue it to **post next**, drop it at the **end of your queue**, or **shuffle it into** the queue. Queue placements go straight into your auto-post schedule; a draft waits until you publish it yourself. Either way the caption and first comment are pre-filled from your **Build Your Post** template, and the product image rides along to the post. Refreshing the deals stays in **Prime Day Butler**; this view only reads what was last harvested, and shows when that was. Turn on **Auto-refresh deals every 6 hours** to have Butler pull the latest deals in the background on its own, so this list stays current without you clicking Refresh. Upcoming deals are confidential until Amazon publishes them, so Butler asks you to confirm before you queue one.

**Deals Hub Auto-Pilot.** Expand **Deals Hub Auto-Pilot** above the table to let Butler add deals for you on a schedule instead of picking each one by hand. There are three independent rules: one for deals above a minimum percent off, one for deals with a **Creator Connections** opportunity, and one for deals with an **SPCC** opportunity. For each rule you set how often it runs (every so many minutes, hours, or days), whether to limit it to live deals, which product **categories** to include (leave empty for all), how many deals to add per run, and how many days to wait before the same product can be added again. Choose whether matches are added as drafts you review or scheduled straight into your auto-post queue. **Run now** triggers a rule immediately so you can see what it would add.

**Promo codes.** Amazon's Deals Hub does not include discount codes, so add yours here: the code, an optional label, an expiration date, notes, and the ASINs it applies to. Got a batch of codes at once? Paste them into the import box (one per line: code, label, expiration, ASINs, notes) and **Add pasted codes** brings them all in together. Expired codes dim automatically. When you send a deal to a post, attach a code from the picker (Butler pre-selects one tagged with that product's ASIN), and it lands in the post.

**Using a code in your captions.** In **Build Your Post**, the **Promo code** dropdown attaches a code to the posts you build there, and a **Promo Code** pill is available in the pill tray. Drag it into your caption (or type the `{{promoCode}}` token) and the chosen code fills in at publish time. No code attached means the token simply drops out.

## Always-Post Products

Some brands give an ongoing discount instead of samples, so the product is always a deal even when Amazon shows no live price drop. The **Always-Post** tab lets you pin those products and post them on their own dedicated schedule, separate from your keyword deals.

Turn it on with the **Post my Always-Post products for me** switch at the top, then set **When to post**: either a simple interval (every day, every few days, or your own number of hours or days) or specific days of the week and times of day. The status line shows your next and last post, and **👀 Watch it run** keeps the browser visible while it posts so you can watch.

- **Add a product**: paste its Amazon URL or ASIN, an optional title, the discount to show (for example "15% off"), and an optional promo code, then **Add product**. By default a product follows the shared schedule above. Switch its **Schedule** to **Custom for this product** to give that one product its own days and times.
- **Add from Creator Connections**: click it to see your full Creator Connections list, both extra-discount and sample campaigns. Items you have already filmed are marked **Filmed** and shown first, so the ready-to-post ones are easy to spot. Add any of them in one click. Use **Only show ones I have filmed** to narrow the list, or turn off **Include sample campaigns** if you only want the extra-discount ones. You can also send items straight from the **CC Check** workspace: select rows there and click **Send Selected to Auto Scheduler** to drop them into this Always-Post list.
- **Paste a batch**: one product per line (ASIN or URL, title, discount, promo code) adds many at once.
- **Post these now**: posts every enabled product right away, so you can watch them go out without waiting for the schedule.

Always-post products skip the "don't repost recently" rule on purpose, because the whole point is that they keep posting. The discount you entered shows in the caption through the **Discount** pill (the `{{discountLabel}}` token). Turn a product off any time with its **Enabled** switch, or remove it.

## Roundup Campaigns: one post, one deal per comment

Want to post "Top 50 kitchen gadget deals today" as a single post and put the actual deals in the comments? The **Roundup Campaigns** tab does exactly that, on a schedule. Each campaign publishes one Facebook post that contains only your campaign text (plus an optional photo you upload), then adds the deals underneath as comments, one product per comment, each with its photo, price, percent off, and your affiliate link.

Setting one up:

- **Add campaign**: give it a name and the post text your followers will see (for example "Top 50 kitchen gadget deals today! All links in the comments.").
- **Search profile or custom keywords**: pick a saved **Search Profile** (a named set of keywords and filters, like "Kitchen Gadgets", that you can reuse across campaigns), or stay on Custom and enter keywords (one per line) plus exactly how many deals to include. A campaign set to 20 posts exactly the top 20 it finds, ranked by percent off. Save any Custom setup as a profile with one click; editing a profile updates every campaign that uses it.
- **Deal source**: *Fresh search at post time* runs a new Amazon search with the campaign's keywords right before posting (freshest deals, adds a few minutes). *From already harvested deals* picks instantly from the deals your searches already found.
- **Days and times**: turn on each day you want to post and set the times just for that day, so one campaign can post Monday at 9:00 and Wednesday at 2:00 and 6:00. Each day can have up to four times, and different days can post at different times.
- **Two roundups a day**: create a second campaign with its own keywords, its own count, and a later time. For example, kitchen gadgets at 9:00 and "Top 20 Amazon Finds on Sale Today" at 10:00. Each campaign only ever posts its own deals.
- **Comment options**: choose whether each comment includes the product photo, and optionally write your own comment template with tokens like `{{title}}`, `{{salePrice}}`, `{{percentOff}}`, and `{{url}}`. The pill tray matches Build Your Post exactly, including the orange library pills (Hook, CTA, Hashtags, Urgency): click one to edit its lines, drag it in to use it.
- **Which groups**: when Facebook Groups is a destination, each campaign shows a checkbox for every group you have in Social Destinations. Leave them all checked to post to every group, or pick just the ones this campaign should post to. That lets two campaigns target two different groups. Facebook Pages still go to all your pages.
- **Amazon Idea List**: turn on *Also create an Amazon Idea List from each roundup* and the campaign builds a real Idea List on your Amazon storefront at post time: the roundup's products, a description, and a short note on each product. Choose *New list every post* for a fresh, dated list each time, or *Keep one list and add to it* so every post adds its new deals to the same list. Tick *Put the list link in the post* to add the tagged link to your list at the end of the post text. One thing Amazon does not allow: a custom cover photo on an Idea List (the thumbnail is always a collage of the product images), so your uploaded campaign photo appears on the Facebook post only.

Comments post slowly on purpose, about 20 to 45 seconds apart, so Facebook does not flag rapid activity. A 50 deal roundup takes roughly 20 to 40 minutes, and other scheduled posts wait for it, so leave at least 45 minutes between campaigns. If Facebook asks to slow down mid-run, the roundup stops early and the campaign card shows how many comments made it (for example "posted 17/50"). Everything auto-saves as you type, and **Run now** fires a campaign immediately so you can watch a small test roundup end to end.

## AI Content Butler: images and multi-product collages

The **AI Content Butler** tab turns your selected deals into ready-to-post images. Pick a deal in **Search Deals**, choose a style and destinations, and hit Generate; the image is saved to this workspace and can be attached to the post in one click.

**Product photo on AI background (the default).** This style keeps your actual Amazon product photo exactly as it is and places it on a clean AI-generated backdrop that suits the product. No redrawn product and no text on the image, so your audience always sees the real item, not an AI drawing. It is the default for new posts, and "Auto" uses it too, so your product stays exact without any setup. The other styles, like Product in use and Deal graphic, let the AI recreate the product's appearance, which can make it look different from the Amazon listing. Pick one of those only when a lifestyle or graphic look matters more to you than an exact match.

**No prices on generated images.** Every style shows discounts as percent-off only. Dollar amounts never appear on AI images, so your posts stay inside Amazon's rules even when a price changes after you post.

**Brand it with your logo.** Turn on **Watermark**, then use **Choose logo** to add your own logo image onto every generated image. You can set where it sits, how large it is, and how bold or subtle it looks with the size and opacity controls. Prefer text? Type a handle like @yourshop instead. Your choice is saved, so posts made on autopilot carry the same branding.

**Multi-product collage.** Want one image that shows several deals at once, like a "Today's Deals" lineup? Select several deals in **Search Deals** (or send them to the AI Content Butler), then open the **Multi-product collage** card and choose:

- **Render mode**: *Designed template* drops your real product photos into a branded frame for an exact, predictable result, or *AI background plus real photos* lets the AI paint the backdrop and lays your real product photos on top.
- **Style**: the *Today's Deals preset* gives you the branded pink and navy look (header banner, feature badges, and a "Shop now" banner), or *Configurable* lets you set your own header text, call to action, and colors.
- **Layout**: pick 1 hero, 3 across, a 2x2 or 3x2 grid, 5 across, or leave it on **Auto** to fit the number of products you selected.

Choose your destinations (Facebook square, link card, Telegram, Reddit), Generate, then click **Use this image** to attach the collage to your post.

## Tracking your deals: Upcoming and Archive

The **Deals** tab is where every deal goes after **Search Deals**. When a harvest finishes, Butler now sends the visible deals to the scheduler automatically, so you do not have to. You can still send deals yourself: per row, or with **Send selected to Scheduler**. Either way the deal moves out of the Search Deals list, so that list only ever shows deals you have not scheduled yet. The cadence you set on the **Scheduler** tab decides when each one actually posts, not the moment it was sent.

- **Upcoming** lists the deals still waiting to post, each with the time it will go out. Use **Publish now** to send one immediately, or **Delete** to drop it.
- **Archive** holds the deals that have finished: posted, partially posted, or failed, with the result of each. Use the search box to find one. **Delete all** clears the whole archive at once. Because it permanently removes those records, it asks you to type "Delete" to confirm.

A deal in Upcoming is either a **Draft** or **Scheduled**. Only Scheduled deals auto-post; a Draft waits until you act on it. You control this per row:

- **Schedule** turns a Draft into a Scheduled post so the auto-poster will send it on your cadence. **Hold** does the reverse, moving a Scheduled post back to Draft so it stops auto-posting (without deleting it).
- **Schedule all drafts** (top of the Upcoming list) arms every Draft at once.
- **Filter chips** above the table let you show only Drafts, only Scheduled, and so on.
- **Drag the grip** on the left of a row to reorder the queue. The order you set is the order deals go out (Butler switches your schedule to "in order" the first time you drag, and tells you so). Reordering is available when no search or status filter is narrowing the list.

Each row's preview also shows the exact link that deal will post, so you can check it before it goes out instead of opening the post afterward. A small chip names the link generator for that post: a blue **Amazon native** chip for a plain affiliate link that opens in the Amazon app, or a colored chip like **linktw.in** or **Geniuslink** when you use a branded link provider. The link is clickable (it opens in your browser to verify) and has a **Copy link** button. For a branded provider, the short link is created when the post goes out, so the preview shows a working stand-in with a **Generate exact link** button. Click it to create that one link now and see the real short link right away.

Want to post one deal right now without waiting for the schedule? Use **Post one now** on the Scheduler tab. It sends the next deal that is queued or scheduled.

### Deals you send from the Chrome extension

Browsing Amazon and spot something worth posting? The Influencer Butler Chrome extension's **Push to Deals Influencer Butler** button sends that product straight into this queue. The **Deals from the Chrome extension** card on the Scheduler tab decides where it lands: saved as a draft for you to review (the default), **Post it next** so it jumps to the front of the queue and goes out at the next posting slot, or added to the end of the queue. Deals you push this way skip the minimum-discount hold, since you picked them yourself.

## Send deals to a Google Sheet

If you keep your own deals spreadsheet, you can push Search Deals results straight into it. Open the **Google Sheet Sync** panel above the results table and follow the short setup: in your Google Sheet, open Apps Script, paste the script the panel gives you, deploy it as a Web app with access set to Anyone, and paste the resulting URL back into the app. The setup takes about two minutes and only needs to be done once.

After you connect, the app reads your sheet's column headers and matches them to deal fields automatically (Product ID to ASIN, Image Link to the product image, and so on). Every match is shown in a grid so you can fix any that look wrong, and columns you set to "Do not fill" are left untouched. Connecting a brand-new, empty sheet is fine too: the app sets up the standard deal columns in row 1 for you, so there is nothing to type by hand. A **Creator Connections** column is available as well: name a column **Creator Connections** (or **CC**), or pick it in the match grid, and each row shows that deal's campaign status (Accepted, Available, or None) so you can sort and filter your sheet by it, just like in the app.

You control how rows are added and when:

- **When adding rows**: add only new deals (skipping any ASIN already in the sheet), add every deal, or replace the sheet contents each time.
- **When to send**: a button for all results, a button for just the rows you have selected, or automatically after every search.

Your sheet URL is stored encrypted on your own machine, and only you can write to your sheet.

If you run more than one deals workspace, you do not have to set this up again for each one. Once your columns and options look the way you want, use **Copy this setup to your other deals workspaces** at the bottom of the panel to send the same layout and options to any of your other Deals Influencer Butler workspaces at once. Each workspace keeps its own connected sheet, so your data never crosses over. If your workspace is set to replace its sheet each time, the app asks you to confirm first and names the workspaces it will change, so you never wipe a sheet by accident.

## Harvest deals from other sites (Deal Sites Harvester)

Creators share long lists of "where to find codes" daily-deal sites (savewithcindy.shop, jungle.deals, promos4creators.com, shared Google Docs, and so on). The **Deal Sites Harvester** in the free Chrome extension turns any of those lists into deals in your workspace, so you do not have to open each site and copy products by hand.

1. Open the extension popup and click **Open Deal Sites Harvester**. It opens in its own tab.
2. Choose your sites: keep **Include the recommended sites** on, paste any URLs you follow (one per line), and optionally **Save these sites** so they are one click next time.
3. Click **Harvest deals**. The extension asks your permission to read those sites, reads each one, pulls out every Amazon product, and fills in the title, price, and image from the Creator API when you have it connected.
4. Review the results table and untick anything you do not want.
5. Pick a **Deals Influencer Butler workspace** and click **Send selected to workspace**. With the desktop app paired (from the extension popup), the deals land in that workspace's Search Deals list, where they sort, filter, and post out on your schedule exactly like deals you harvested from Amazon.

Every harvested deal is also saved to your dashboard, so you have a record even before you send it. A few notes:

- The harvester reads the Amazon product links on a page, so it works on almost any site, including a published Google Doc. Shortened links (like `amzn.to`) cannot be read without opening them, so those are skipped.
- Harvest a handful of sites at a time. Very large lists are capped, and the harvester tells you when that happens.
- Only the sites you choose are ever read, only when you click Harvest, and your Amazon sign-in is never sent to those third-party sites.

## Where the data goes

Harvested deals live in the preseed cache; posted-deal history lives in `{userData}/InfluencerButler/workspaces/preseed/data/daily-deals/`. The Console Log tab streams every post attempt + outcome.

## Common gotchas

- **"No deals match your filters"**: usually a too-narrow filter combo. Drop one filter at a time until results return.
- **Posts fail silently to Telegram**: bot token revoked or chat permissions changed. Re-paste the bot token in Social Destinations.
- **Instagram posts go to message requests, not inbox**: that's not a Deals Influencer Butler issue; that's Instagram's own routing for accounts you don't follow.
- **Scheduler appears stopped**: check the Scheduler tab's status banner. The runner stops itself if it detects bad credentials and reports the reason there.
- **The same deal posts more than once**: the **Don't repost recently** guard in the Deal Filters holds a deal at publish time if a matching one already went out inside your window. It is on by default with a 30-day window. If you post product variants, also tick **Product title** under it, so different sizes or colors of the same item count as one deal.

## Marketplace links

If you have followers in more than one country, the **Marketplace links** control in Build Your Post sends each shopper somewhere they can actually buy. Pick a mode:

- **Off (one link)**: the usual single link.
- **Country links**: a short list, one tagged link per country the product sells in, so a shopper picks their own store.
- **Single redirect link**: one link that forwards each shopper to their country's store automatically (uses your DeepLink provider).

Set a default for every post, or override it per post from the Posts queue. Enter your per-country associate tags first in [API Integrations](/help/tutorials/api-integrations). A country with no tag is skipped, so you never post a link that earns nothing.

### Butler Link routing for measurable clicks

If you have not set up a smart DeepLink provider, deals normally go out as plain tagged Amazon links, and those clicks are invisible to Earnings Intelligence. The **Route deal links through Butler Links** checkbox in the same Marketplace links group (on by default) gives each sent deal a free Butler Link instead, so the Ledger and the new Conversion Leak report can show clicks, EPC, and what dead deals cost you, per deal. Turn it off if you prefer plain tagged links.
